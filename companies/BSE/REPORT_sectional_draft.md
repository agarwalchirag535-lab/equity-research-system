# 0.1 THE ANSWER, BEFORE THE EVIDENCE

**BSE's numbers are reliable in amount and its disclosure perimeter is not reliable in scope — and the price is being paid for a decade of growth in a variable a regulator allocates.**

1. **The accounts can be relied on; the most-used cash number cannot be used as printed.** Earnings-quality verdict **MODERATE**. Five independent reconstructions of the FY2026 accounts close to between **0.02% and 0.13%**; twenty-six restatement movements in ten years contain exactly **two** earnings restatements, one mechanical. But ten-year cumulative operating cash flow converts at **0.712 own-account against a printed 1.272** — `DERIVED: 1.272 ÷ 0.712 = 1.787`, **78.7% too high** — and no bound tested reaches parity (§9).

2. **The price is 56.26× reported earnings and 109.74× the same year's own-account operating owner cash.** `DERIVED: 3,409.80 ÷ 60.61 = 56.26×`; `DERIVED: 1,30,34,590 ÷ 1,18,779 = 109.74×`. **The constraints roughly double the effective multiple** (`DERIVED: 109.74 ÷ 56.26 = 1.95×`) by removing money that is in the reported number but is not a claim on the exchange (§18).

3. **83.80% of the market capitalisation is growth that has not been earned.** Treasury at book, CDSL at market and a no-growth capitalisation of own-account free cash flow together are `DERIVED: 22,52,841 ÷ 1,39,08,031 = 16.20%` of the price. The remainder requires own-account operating free cash flow to compound at **26.8%–31.7% a year for ten years** against a demonstrated pre-windfall six-year rate of **13.89%** (§18).

4. **8.20% of the assets carries 93.72% of the price.** ₹1,10,285 lakh of operating capital earns **195.02%** after tax; the ₹5,22,509 lakh treasury — **78.30% of shareholders' capital** — earns **4.42%** and is **3.76%** of the price. Stripping the balance sheet at fair value makes the multiple **worse** (§7, §18).

5. **The margin of safety required is that a reader pay on ₹1,18,779 lakh of own-account owner cash rather than ₹2,49,698 lakh of reported profit** — `DERIVED: 1 − 1,18,779 ÷ 2,49,698 = 52.43%` haircut — on growth no faster than 13.89%, with the clearing subsidiary at a wider discount than the exchange (§18).

6. **The register is long and it has never cost money.** Thirty-six issues, twenty-three at High. Against that: **in ten years, zero rupees of realised loss at BSE is attributable to an accounting-quality channel** (§14.4).

> **In one sentence.** BSE's arithmetic is trustworthy and its perimeter is not; the money risk is regulatory rather than accounting; and at ₹3,409.80 the price requires a regulator-allocated expiry calendar and a market-structure ratio management calls unforecastable to deliver a decade at roughly twice the rate the company demonstrated before a one-year share transfer it did not price.

**The margin gained 10.06 points, half of it one cost line whose movement is three-quarters a revenue denominator and one-fifth consolidation arithmetic. The largest expense in the group moved the margin by six basis points.**

### 5.1 The margin bridge

As a share of total income (FY2026 ₹5,14,810 lakh; FY2025 ₹3,23,631 lakh) `[AR FY26, Consolidated P&L, PDF p164/pr153]`:

| Other expenses | 6.43% | 4.80% | −1.63pp |
| Employee benefits | 7.31% | 5.78% | −1.53pp |
| Technology | 5.08% | 3.95% | −1.13pp |
| Depreciation and amortisation | 3.49% | 3.09% | −0.40pp |
| **Regulatory contribution (the levy)** | 12.68% | 12.62% | **−0.06pp** |
| Finance cost | 0.00% | 0.00% | +0.00pp |
| **Total expenses ÷ total income** | **45.75%** | **35.69%** | **−10.06pp** |

**(a) Half the margin gain is one line** — `DERIVED: 5.31 ÷ 10.06 = 52.8%`. The **denominator** alone supplies −3.99pp (`DERIVED: 34,805 ÷ 3,23,631 − 34,805 ÷ 5,14,810`), i.e. **75.1% is revenue growing, not cost falling**; the **numerator** supplies −1.32pp. Of that, only −0.25pp survives grossed up — **the remaining −1.07pp (`DERIVED: 5,496 ÷ 5,14,810`) is the intra-group charge growing and eliminating, 10.6% of the entire margin gain.**

**(b) The largest expense in the group contributed −0.06pp.** The levy grew 58.28% and moved the margin six basis points. Management's claim that *"regulatory contribution does not impact the operating efficiency of the business"* `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p76/pr65]` is **very nearly true for FY2026 and false as a general statement** — the company's own table on the same page shows the levy moving **−140 basis points** as a share of standalone operating revenue.

**Cost-increase attribution.** Of the ₹35,679 lakh rise in total expenses: **regulatory contribution ₹23,923 lakh = 67.05%**, employee benefits ₹6,119 lakh = 17.15%, depreciation ₹4,598 lakh = 12.89%, other expenses ₹3,912 lakh = 10.96%, technology ₹3,884 lakh = 10.89% — **and clearing and settlement subtracted ₹6,770 lakh = −18.97%.**

### 5.3 The levy — a permanent turnover tax on a base 959× the base BSE bills on

`DERIVED: 64,969 ÷ 4,83,395 = 13.44%` of consolidated revenue, **permanently**, on a base `DERIVED: 1,87,15,375 ÷ 19,523 = 958.6×` the base BSE bills on. It is `DERIVED: 64,969 ÷ 1,83,742 = 35.4%` of total expenses — **the largest single expense in the group.**

**The levy tracks its base almost exactly, which is the proof the base is notional.** SEBI regulatory fees `DERIVED: 56,616 ÷ 33,141 = +70.83%` against options notional turnover `DERIVED: 1,87,15,375 ÷ 1,10,66,674 = +69.12%` — a movement of **+1.02%**. Transaction charges tracked **premium** to within 1.83%. **Two different bases, each tracking its own driver to within 2%.**

**The FY2026 increase decomposes 101.7% volume, −1.7% rate, 0% base mismatch in the delta** — so the wedge is a **level** exposure, not a growth driver. **The mismatch moved in BSE's favour by ₹16,402 lakh**; the levy **fell** as a share of revenue, 13.88% → 13.44%. **Both halves ship: the same ratio that flattered the margin also flattered the levy, and it hands both back on reversion.**

**The retrospective element is settled and small.** SEBI advised by letter of **26-Apr-2024** that the fee be computed on the **notional** value of options **from FY2006-07 onwards, with 15% per annum interest**, reiterated **14-Aug-2024**, which ended the contest. The retrospective element is **₹7,347 lakh = `DERIVED: 7,347 ÷ 77,166 = 9.52%` of FY2024 consolidated PAT** `[AR FY24, Note 46, PDF p233/pr227]`. **No provision and no contingent liability survives at 31 March 2026.**

**Five independent tests settle it, all pointing the same way.** (i) The provisions note prints ₹5,444 lakh as a **closing balance** beside ₹16,977 lakh as its opening balance. (ii) The FY2025 cash-flow provision line is a **decrease**, `(10,793)`. (iii) The FY2026 report **reclassified** the ₹5,444 lakh into other current liabilities — `DERIVED: 47,947 − 42,503 = 5,444`, to the rupee — **and a charge cannot be reclassified.** (iv) The implied levy rate is flat across three years: `0.002819 / 0.002993 / 0.002972`. (v) The FY2024 note says so in terms: *"including ₹7,347 lakhs in respect of periods up to March 31, 2023."*

> **The forward number is the larger one.** The ongoing levy at ₹56,616 lakh is `DERIVED: 56,616 ÷ 7,347 = 7.7×` the entire retrospective charge, **every year**. **And the FY2026 annual report carries no SEBI-fee note at all**, in the year the item rose 70.8%.

**The governance point underneath the arithmetic:** a regulator re-read its own seventeen-year-old rule, applied 15% penal interest, and **the company's contest lasted one letter.**

### 7.3 Return on equity — four bases, and the reported figure UNDERSTATES the return

| Basis | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| (a) PAT ÷ average **total equity** | 32.93% | **43.75%** |
| (b) PAT attributable ÷ average **equity attributable** | 34.32% | 45.00% |
| (c) PAT attributable ÷ average **distributable equity** | 43.36% | **52.70%** |
| (d) (c) **ex after-tax float income** | 38.00% | **49.99%** |

`DERIVED: 6,79,550 − 12,249 − 66,179 − 10,530 − 4,437 − 9 − 81 = 5,86,065` distributable at FY2026.

**Two errors of opposite sign sit inside the reported 43.75%.** Non-distributable reserves in the denominator **understate** the return by **8.95 points**; income on members' float in the numerator **overstates** it by **2.71 points**. **Net: the reported figure understates the true return on distributable capital by 6.24 points. The direction is up, not down.**

**This is worth stating plainly, because the general rule for this sector predicts the opposite.** The rule assumes restricted funds sit inside equity and inflate the return. **At BSE the ₹1,24,683 lakh core settlement guarantee fund is presented as its own caption *between* equity and liabilities and is already outside the denominator.**

> **The generalised rule, corrected: do not assume the direction. Establish where the restricted funds actually sit — inside equity, or in a caption of their own — before choosing a basis. Both errors are available, and which one applies is a presentation fact, not a principle.**

**And the legal control is narrower than the economic one.** Note 15.4 caps distribution at the **parent's** retained earnings, **₹4,55,828 lakh — 67.1% of consolidated total equity.** ₹5,86,065 lakh is the economic claim; ₹4,55,828 lakh is what the board could legally declare out of today.

### 7.4 CONTRADICTION CARRIED — the ₹10,60,000 lakh guarantee was removed and the prior year actively restated

| As at 31-Mar | Guarantees given by the Group (₹ lakh) |

**This is not a collapse. It is a restatement of the prior-year comparative by ₹10,60,000 lakh with no note.** The FY2025 report explains it in terms: the subsidiary *"has given bank guarantee of ₹10,60,000 Lakhs… towards **Inter CCP collateral under Interoperability framework as prescribed by SEBI**"*. **The phrase "bank guarantee" occurs nowhere in the FY2026 annual report, and "Inter CCP" appears nowhere in it either.**

**A guarantee released during FY2026 would leave the comparative at ₹10,60,024 lakh and drop only the current column.** Restating the comparative is possible only on a change of view that it was **never** a Group contingent liability — an Ind AS 1.41 reclassification or an Ind AS 8.49 error correction. **Neither is disclosed.**

**THE SIZING — three arithmetically correct answers on three bases, all on the record.**

| Position | Figure | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **PUBLISHED** | **2.32× total equity and 102.5% of total assets at 31-Mar-2025** | `DERIVED: 10,60,024 ÷ 4,57,570 = 2.3167`; `10,60,024 ÷ 10,34,165 = 1.0250` |

| **Not the headline** | 1.56× equity / 78.8% of assets | FY2026 denominators |

**All three are arithmetically correct on different years and denominators.** The published basis is FY2025 for one reason: **the guarantee existed at 31 March 2025, and it is that comparative that was restated. A number must be sized against the balance sheet of its own date.** Between those dates equity grew **+48.5%** and assets **+30.0%** — **so measuring a FY2025 exposure against FY2026 denominators understates it by roughly a third.**

> **The published sizing is an off-balance-sheet exposure that exceeded the group's entire balance sheet.**

**One corroboration that identifies this as a disclosure event rather than an economic one.** The restated line is **the only one in Note 37 that moved down.** Every other line grew — income tax +20.8%, indirect tax +168.3%, claims +29.2%, total non-guarantee contingent liabilities **+38.1%**. **The one line that vanished is 32× larger than everything that stayed, in a note that expanded.**

**And the evidence cutting the other way, which is why this settles at High rather than as a terminal finding.**

- **No recognised amount is affected, in any year.** The core settlement guarantee fund and clearing-and-settlement liabilities both **grew**; the guarantee never appeared in the cash flow. **This bounds the completeness of the risk disclosure; it does not touch a single number a valuation would use.**
- **The exposure is not what its caption sounds like** — inter-CCP collateral under SEBI's interoperability framework, a deliberate mutual-hostage design, **not a credit guarantee of a third party's debt.**
- **The reciprocal is disclosed and granular. NSE contributes ₹26,529 lakh — 21.52% — to the clearing subsidiary's core settlement guarantee fund, by contributor and by segment, tying exactly.** **A company hiding an exposure to its rival does not publish its rival's money to the rupee in the same set of accounts.**
- **The base rate is zero for the exposure class.** No Indian clearing corporation has defaulted since interoperability went live in June 2019; SEBI disposed of its interoperability show-cause against the subsidiary in **May 2025 without penalty.**

**Cutting back the other way, and it is the strongest argument against the benign reading: the clearing subsidiary did not contract** — total assets rose **+10.1%** and its own fund contribution rose **+7.3%**. **A guarantee that had simply unwound would more plausibly accompany a contracting balance sheet, and this one grew.**

**What would settle it:** `NOT-DISCLOSED -> the clearing subsidiary's own s.136 / MCA-filed FY2026 financial statements, and the SEBI-mandated risk disclosures on the BSE website.` **Both exist and both are public.**

> **The consequence is a named margin-of-safety requirement, not a terminal state: the balance sheet is complete only on the assumption that the ₹10,60,000 lakh never was a Group obligation, and that is an assumption the reader must make rather than verify.** **This is the highest-consequence monitorable in the report** (§19), and **a reasonable analyst could rule the other way on this one item.**

# BSE Limited — a forensic investigation

**What this is.** Not an earnings review. An investigation into where BSE's FY2026 earnings actually came from, whether they can persist, and what the published accounts do and do not let a reader see.

**As at** 8 September 2026 · **price** ₹3,409.80, close 4 September 2026 `[NSE Bhavcopy, 2026-09-04]` · **accounts** audited consolidated FY2026, and FY2017–FY2026 read from each year's own annual report rather than from later comparatives.

**Every claim below is graded.** The distinction is load-bearing and is marked at each turn:

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **REPORTED** | What the company published |
| **ESTABLISHED** | What the evidence proves, with the arithmetic shown |
| **INFERRED** | What follows from the evidence but is not itself disclosed |
| **UNCERTAIN** | What the evidence cannot settle — stated as such, never resolved by preference |

**Two bases travel together throughout**, because both have already produced errors in this work: standalone vs consolidated revenue (₹4,46,951 lakh vs ₹4,83,395 lakh) and issued vs paid-up shares (41,19,79,077 vs 40,78,84,077 — market capitalisation on paid-up, earnings per share on issued, which is the company's own audited basis). Unit is ₹ lakh throughout.

---

# PART I — THE APPARENT STORY

**REPORTED.** FY2026 was the best year in BSE's history. Revenue from operations rose **63.46%** to ₹4,83,395 lakh. Profit attributable rose **88.3%** to ₹2,49,698 lakh. Operating margin gained **10.06 points**. Return on equity reached **43.75%**. Cash flow from operations was ₹3,10,368 lakh against profit of ₹2,49,698 lakh — a conversion of **1.248**. The company describes a *"transformational increase in equity derivatives activity"* and *"a sustainable structural efficiency gain"* in clearing costs `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p75-76/pr64-65]`.

Read at face value, this is a company that grew, priced well, converted profit to cash better than one-for-one, and became structurally more efficient.

**Every one of those five claims is either incomplete or wrong, and the corrections do not all run the same way.** Two of them understate the company. This report tests each in turn.

The apparent story rests on three quantities. **None of the three is owned by BSE, none is priced by BSE, and two of them moved inside a single month during the year under review.** That is what the rest of this report is about.

# PART II — THE INVESTIGATION

## 1. Where the growth actually came from

**REPORTED.** Revenue rose ₹1,87,661 lakh. Management attributes it to *"a transformational increase in equity derivatives activity"* and, separately, states that transaction charges *"grew 88%… powered by"* that activity.

**ESTABLISHED — every rupee attributed, zero residual.**

| Driver | ₹ lakh | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Equity-derivatives premium turnover — activity alone** | **+1,66,261** | **88.60%** |
| All other activity growth | +6,915 | 3.68% |
| **Realisation, all lines combined** | **−1,215** | **−0.65%** |
| Everything else (colocation, data, listing, index, clearing billed outside the group) | +17,700 | 9.43% |
| **Total** | **+1,87,661** | **100.00%** |

`DERIVED: 5,622 − 783 − 6,054 = −1,215` — the realisation gain on derivatives was more than consumed by ₹60.5 cr lost on equity cash and ₹7.8 cr on the mutual-fund platform.

> **Management's account is true on 91.6% of the increase and false on the aggregate. Price was a drag on the year, not a contributor.**

**CONNECTED — the pool did not grow; the split moved.** Under identical rules, identical securities transaction tax and identical RBI directions, the competitor's total income **fell 2.4%** and its profit **fell 15.5%** while BSE's rose 59.1% and 88.3%. The two-venue income pool grew `DERIVED: 23,861.10 ÷ 22,413.31 = 6.46%`. BSE's share went **14.44% → 21.58%, +714 basis points.**

`DERIVED: (1,911.79 − 1,447.79 × 0.1444) ÷ 1,911.79 = 89.07%` — **nine-tenths of BSE's income increment came out of the competitor rather than out of market growth.**

**CONNECTED — the mechanism was a circular, not a product.** SEBI's framework of 1 October 2024 limited each exchange to **one weekly expiry on one benchmark index**, converting the competitor's advantage in the *number* of weekly products into a symmetric one-each allocation. Approximately **95% of BSE's premium sits in the two nearest weekly expiries**.

**CONNECTED — and management misread its own franchise for sixteen months.** The MD attributed Sensex's success to owning an expiry day that *"did not clash with any expiry"* `[Q4FY24 Concall 13May2024]`. BSE then **surrendered that exclusivity** on 1 September 2025 by moving onto the competitor's Thursday — and posted the fastest growth in the product's history. The real driver was premium per lot rising 180% on 35% *fewer* contracts: a mix shift authored by SEBI's November-2024 lot-size doubling.

**CONNECTED — and the calendar moved against BSE inside the same year.** Premium share fell to **32.4% in June 2026** for one reason: the competitor had an extra weekly expiry that month.

**INFERRED.** A share won on a regulator-allocated calendar is held on a regulator-allocated calendar. FY2026 was a **redistribution**, which is zero-sum and bounded above by a pool that grew 6.46% — not a market expansion.

**UNCERTAIN, and it cuts the other way.** BSE took that share **under identical rules**, which is a genuine competitive result and not luck. There is **no evidence in the corpus that the gain has reversed** on any measure other than one secondary-sourced month. A reader who believes the calendar will not move again is entitled to weight this heavily against everything above, and this report records that a reasonable analyst does so.

## 2. Where the margin actually came from

**REPORTED.** Operating margin gained 10.06 points. Clearing and settlement cost fell 19.45%, described as *"a sustainable structural efficiency gain"*.

**ESTABLISHED — half the margin gain is one line, and most of that line is not a cost improvement.**

| Component of the −5.31pp clearing contribution | Share |
|---|---:|
| The **revenue denominator** growing | **75.1%** |
| An **intra-group charge** growing and eliminating on consolidation | 10.6% of the *entire* margin gain |
| Actual external cost improvement, grossed up | −0.25pp |

**CONNECTED — one cost, three published magnitudes, and the MD&A quotes the middle one.** Consolidated **−19.45%** · standalone **−4.56%** · grossed up for the intra-group charge **−3.04%**.

**CONNECTED — and on the base management itself names as the cost driver, the cost went up.**

> **The word "efficiency" describes a unit cost. Per contract, grossed up, clearing cost rose 3.74%.** The rupee fall is entirely a **6.53% fall in the quantity being cleared**.

**CONNECTED — the quarterly data corroborates it independently.** Clearing cost went `5,550 (Jun-2025) → 9,028 (Jun-2026) = +62.7%` against revenue **+63.4%**. The line now tracks revenue almost exactly — consistent with the elimination explanation, inconsistent with structural efficiency.

**ESTABLISHED — what actually created the margin is a market-structure ratio.** BSE earns on **premium**, pays its regulatory levy on **notional**, and pays clearing cost per **contract**. In FY2026 those three moved **+117.45%, +69.11% and −6.53%** — a divergence of 124 percentage points. The premium/notional ratio rose **28.58%**.

**CONNECTED — so operating leverage has no single value, and the sign flips.**

| Activity base | FY2026 change | Ex-levy cost elasticity |
|---|---:|---:|
| Premium | +117.45% | **+0.094** |
| Notional | +69.11% | **+0.159** |
| **Contracts** | **−6.53%** | **−1.682** |

> **A 1.776-point spread including the sign, on one year's trades. Any single operating-leverage figure quoted for this company is a choice presented as a measurement.**

**CONNECTED — and the cost base only ratchets one way.** In all three windows where turnover actually fell, costs **rose**: −0.507, −0.750, −0.781. Costs contracted once in ten years — **in a year turnover rose 56.84%.** The claim that survives is narrower and is the one to publish: *costs have never fallen in a year turnover fell.*

**INFERRED — and this is the sharpest arithmetic in the report.** Only ₹15.48 of every ₹100 of revenue is discretionary spend. A 20% cut across **every** discretionary layer — deeper than anything on record — recovers **₹3.10 per ₹100**. A reversion of the wedge merely to the Q2FY26 level **already printed inside this cycle** removes **₹38.82 per ₹100**.

> **12.5 to 1. The entire discretionary cost lever, pulled harder than it has ever been pulled, covers one-twelfth of the hole.**

**UNCERTAIN, and it cuts the other way.** The wedge moved **in BSE's favour by ₹16,402 lakh** in FY2026 against a premium-base counterfactual, and the FY2026 levy increase decomposes 101.7% volume, −1.7% rate, **0% base mismatch**. The wedge is a **level** exposure, not a worsening trend — and reading it as a worsening trend was disconfirmed inside this analysis. The same ratio flattered both the margin and the levy, and it hands back both on reversion.

## 3. What happened to the cash

**REPORTED.** Cash from operations ₹3,10,368 lakh; conversion 1.248; ten-year cumulative conversion 1.272. Better than one-for-one.

**ESTABLISHED — 45.1% of this balance sheet is not the company's money**, and the classification ties to the reported total with **zero residual** in each of three years. Note 16 names the three buckets outright: Owned ₹24,992 lakh, Earmarked ₹26,004 lakh, **Clearing and settlement ₹3,55,423 lakh**.

**ESTABLISHED — separating own-account cash changes the answer and its sign.**

| | Ten-year cumulative |
|---|---:|
| Headline CFO ÷ PAT | **1.272** |
| **Own-account CFO ÷ PAT** | **0.712** |

Bounds tested: **0.609 · 0.667–0.757 · 0.712 · 0.825 · 0.874. None reaches parity.** The reconstruction closes on the balance sheet to **₹49 lakh — 0.02%**.

**CONNECTED — 95.1% of the year's largest cash inflow is other people's money.** Of the ₹72,767 lakh working-capital line carrying FY2026's cash flow: **₹24,251 lakh is members' float** and **₹44,937 lakh is tax collected for the government**. BSE's own working capital contributed **₹3,579 lakh — 4.9%.** A ratio of 20 to 1.

**CONNECTED — and the basis on which this is measured changed, by footnote.** The FY2025 cash-flow statement inserted eleven words — *"Movement in Core Settlement Guarantee Fund liabilities and related assets of subsidiary companies are not considered"* — with **no reclassification note and no amount**. It cut the FY2024 comparative by ₹19,857 lakh, in the year that figure fell 85.6%.

**CONNECTED — income on members' money is booked inside revenue.** ₹17,142 lakh of treasury income on the ₹3,55,423 lakh clearing float is credited to shareholders **as revenue from operations**, while income on earmarked deposits is credited to the liability and settlement-fund income to the fund. **No policy paragraph covers the third pool in either direction.** The classification follows the *entity*, not the economics.

**INFERRED.** The printed cash-flow number is not a claim on shareholders. A model built on it pays for ₹64.21 of cash per ₹100 of revenue where the shareholders' claim is ₹44.01 — a **1.459× overstatement**.

**UNCERTAIN — and it matters that this is a caption defect, not a fabrication.** Every input is traced in all ten annual reports and ties to the balance-sheet caption exactly each year. The disclosure to separate the float existed in Notes 11, 12 and 16 the whole time. **The correct number was always constructible; it was simply never presented.**

## 4. What the company is doing with the money

**REPORTED.** Return on equity 43.75%. Ten-year incremental return on capital 56.96%.

**ESTABLISHED — the reported return is a weighted average of two businesses 44× apart.**

| Pool | Share of shareholders' capital | After-tax return |
|---|---:|---:|
| Securities and bank deposits | **78.30%** | **4.42%** |
| The exchange itself | **16.53%** | **195.02%** |

**CONNECTED — and the weight on the deposit account is rising fast.** Own-account financial assets went from 83.88% of equity attributable to **92.06%** in one year, and **108.13% of FY2026's entire increase in shareholders' equity went into the securities book** — ₹6.68 of financial asset for every ₹1 of operating asset.

**CONNECTED — the payout regime inverted exactly when the money arrived.**

| Period | Own-account free cash flow | Returned | As % of free cash flow |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| FY2017–FY2023 | ₹6,854 lakh | ₹1,65,964 lakh | **24.21×** |
| FY2024–FY2026 | ₹3,30,683 lakh | ₹70,094 lakh | **21.20%** |

> **BSE distributed twenty-four times its free cash flow when it had none, and a fifth of it when it had plenty. There is no dividend policy in any of ten annual reports.**

**ESTABLISHED — the cost, sized.** One year's retention costs **1,129 basis points** of return; the accumulated pile costs **10,707 basis points** — `DERIVED: 36.60%` as retained against `143.67%` if distributed.

**CONNECTED — and every explanation offered has been tested and sized.**

| Explanation | Sized | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Capital trapped in the regulated clearing subsidiary | 5.1% of the year's retention | Explains one twentieth |
| Regulatory capital requirement | Floors ₹201 cr against equity of ₹6,795 cr — **97.04% surplus** | Explains nothing |
| The capital-expenditure cycle | Cash capex ₹35,932 lakh against a dividend of ₹41,198 lakh | **Distribution exceeded investment** |
| Legal capacity | 11.07× the proposed dividend | Not a constraint |

**CONNECTED — and management's denial of the premise is falsified by the documents it cites.** Asked why the company retains cash earning *"4% – 4.5% post-tax"*, the MD replied that *"the money is not earning 4.5%… it is earning much more than that… which is very much available in the balance sheet and profit and loss for you to see."* **The balance sheet and profit and loss give 4.42% after tax — inside the range put to him and 0.08 points from its midpoint.**

**INFERRED.** Over the decade ₹1 became more than ₹1 — but **almost none of it was a decision.** Both CDSL sell-downs were SEBI-directed and the residual 15% is a regulatory cap; the buyback route is closed at 2.49% take-up and would now require clearing 9.47× the last offer price.

> **On the capital management actually controls today, ₹1 is becoming ₹1.04 a year, inside a business earning 195%.**

**UNCERTAIN, and this is the strongest fact on the other side.** The buybacks are worth **+18.87% of today's share price** — ₹22,083 crore of value for continuing shareholders against ₹658 crore paid. But `DERIVED: 4,497 + 12,300 + 47,620 = 64,417` lakh of the ₹65,837 lakh was spent FY2018–FY2020, **before the money arrived.** **BSE was an outstanding allocator when it had nothing and has been a poor one since it has had plenty.**

## 5. What the accounts do and do not let a reader see

**This is the section most likely to be misread, so the conclusion goes first: the arithmetic inside BSE's accounts is trustworthy and the perimeter around them is not. Those are different findings and only one of them is about honesty.**

**ESTABLISHED — the arithmetic is sound, and the evidence is affirmative rather than an absence.** Five independent reconstructions of the FY2026 accounts close to between **0.02% and 0.13%**. Twenty-six restatement movements across ten annual reports contain exactly **two** earnings restatements, one of them mechanical. FY2020 through FY2023 and FY2025 are clean. Revenue recognition is point-in-time at a rate filed with the regulator, with **no variable consideration on 78.51% of revenue** — the least discretionary model available.

**And five defects run against the company's own interest**: the FY2024 cash-flow restatement **cut** reported cash flow by ₹19,857 lakh; the loss-allowance re-estimate is **net conservative by ₹879 lakh**; buildings moved to the **shorter** depreciable life; the earnings-per-share figure management leads with is the **lower** one (₹56.27 against an audited ₹60.61); and the voluntary settlement-fund policy **permanently reduces** profit.

> **A register of five conservative errors is not the register of a company managing earnings. Anyone declining BSE on accounting grounds is arguing against the evidence.**

**ESTABLISHED — the perimeter moves.** Seven separate disclosures narrowed:

| What stopped | The year it stopped | What the underlying item was doing |
|---|---|---|
| The ₹10,60,000 lakh inter-CCP guarantee | FY2026 — **the prior-year comparative was actively restated to ₹24 lakh** | Had grown every year for six |
| The "of which remote" grading on claims | FY2026 | Claims **+29.2%**, their first material rise in nine years |
| The SEBI regulatory-fee note | FY2026 | The fee **+70.8%** to ₹56,616 lakh |
| Bankex and every market-share metric | FY2025 onward | Bankex had fallen ~95%, with a public promise to rebuild |
| The market-price table and index charts | After FY2024 | A LODR Schedule V requirement, at the company that **sells market data** |
| Eight related parties, including two employee trusts | FY2026 | Their cash flows **12.3×** and **+182%** |
| The trading-member shareholding category | Sep-2025 | **20.57% reclassified to "public" with 0.29% of shares traded** |

**INFERRED — and the inference rests on a controlled comparison inside one company in one year.**

> **On accounting, the direction of error runs against the company. On disclosure, it runs with the company, in every case where the trajectory can be established. One organisation cannot be unbiased in its arithmetic and unbiased in its perimeter and produce that asymmetry by chance.**

It is also **not simply a capacity problem**: the pattern crosses two independent channels — the annual report (annual, assured, secretarial function) and the investor-relations channel (quarterly, unassured, different team). A staffing constraint in the first does not propagate into the MD's prepared remarks.

**UNCERTAIN — on three of the seven, process and intent cannot be distinguished**, and this report does not pretend otherwise: the governance penalty table is entity-scoped with two secretarial audits signed thirteen days apart; six of the eight deleted parties are legacy trusts with immaterial transactions; a section letter was reused and everything after it shifted up. **On two it can**: a comparative was *actively restated* by ₹10,60,000 lakh, and two trusts a standard makes related parties **by definition** were removed in the year their money multiplied.

**ESTABLISHED — and the base rate cuts the finding down hard.** The gap has cost a rupee **once cleanly** (the SEBI fee: note → contest abandoned → no note). Twice arguably — and in both, the loss was disclosed *first* and the silence followed. **Nowhere has the gap itself cost a rupee**: no restatement, no qualification, no non-disclosure action, no clearing-corporation default in India since 2019.

**And the disclosure record has a strong half that must be stated at full strength.** Every adverse event across **477** exchange announcements was disclosed the same day — a subsidiary's SEBI order at **22:43 the night it was passed**. FY2026 **added** disclosure as well as removing it. Colocation revenue was refused in August 2025 and then disclosed quarterly from November 2025 **and retrospectively**. **"Reducing transparency" is too strong as a general characterisation and is not adopted here.**

> **The verdict: the perimeter moves with the news while the arithmetic inside it does not — and in ten years that has cost the company approximately nothing and cost the reader a great deal.**

**INFERRED — and this is why it belongs in a valuation rather than a governance annexe.** Every series a reader would need to track the first three investigations in real time is **either withdrawn or was never disclosed**: contract counts, the audited product split, the intra-group clearing rate, market share, Bankex, the inter-CCP guarantee. **A thesis whose monitorables have been deleted cannot be falsified quickly. That is a margin-of-safety fact, not a moral one.**

**The single sentence the whole investigation reduces to:**

> **Every component of FY2026 — the revenue growth, the margin gain, the cash conversion and the return on equity — traces to two SEBI circulars and one market-structure ratio. Not one of the three is owned by BSE, priced by BSE, or forecastable by BSE's own account. And two of them moved against BSE inside the year under review.**

**The corollaries follow mechanically, and each was found by a different line of enquiry before they were connected:**

- The **margin** is the quotient of two exogenous ratios, so it is not a management achievement and cannot be defended by management action.
- **Operating leverage** has no single value because the three bases diverged by 124 points, so cost rows in any forward model are **assumptions, not derivations**.
- The **cost base cannot answer the revenue risk** — 12.5 to 1 against.
- The **cash** that arrived was 95.1% other people's money at the margin, so the conversion that looks better than one-for-one is 0.712 over a decade.
- The **capital** it became earns 4.42%, so the reported return on equity is a decaying number by construction.
- And the **series needed to watch any of this** have been withdrawn.

## 7. What the investigation does NOT establish

**Prioritising material issues means saying plainly where the evidence stops. Six things this report cannot settle, and it does not resolve them by preference.**

**1. Whether the share gain reverses.** BSE took 714 basis points **under identical rules**, and there is no evidence of reversal beyond one secondary-sourced month. The mechanism is a circular and circulars move — but "can move" is not "will move", and the report does not claim otherwise.

**2. Whether the ₹10,60,000 lakh guarantee ever was a Group obligation.** The comparative was actively restated with no note, which requires a positive act. But the exposure is inter-CCP collateral under a mutual-hostage design, **BSE publishes the competitor's ₹26,529 lakh contribution to its own clearing fund to the rupee** in the same accounts, and the base rate for the exposure class is zero. **The settling document is public and one filing away.** A reasonable analyst could rule either way, and this report says so rather than choosing.

**3. Which of the clearing subsidiary's two published profits is the right one** — ₹16,979 lakh on Form AOC-1 or ₹6,805 lakh on the Schedule III table 84 printed pages later. **The valuation's sizing of that subsidiary rests on the higher figure and would not stand on the lower.** No consolidation bridge exists in the report.

**4. What the BSE Institute disposal was sold for.** Four figures: ₹1,690 lakh, ₹6,590 lakh, ₹6,191 lakh, and a public "₹16.9 crores". Two of three documented figures say ₹1,690 lakh. **₹4,900 lakh of the parent's distributable profit turns on it, and the parent is the only entity that can pay a dividend.**

**5. Whether the disclosure narrowing is intent or process** on three of its seven instances.

**6. The pricing basis of any recurring related-party charge.** No rate, volume base or benchmark exists anywhere in the corpus. The intra-group clearing charge passes the only available proxy — the rate per rupee of transaction charge **fell**, 3.65% → 3.44% — but the assertion is unfalsifiable from the filings. **And the sharpest untested case is not the clearing subsidiary: BSE bills Hindustan Power Exchange 2.22× that associate's entire net profit.**

# PART III — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE PRICE

**The investigation above is about durability. This part is about what is being paid for it.**

## The multiple, on two bases that must travel together

| | |
|---|---:|
| Price × paid-up shares = market capitalisation | **₹1,39,08,031 lakh** |
| ÷ audited consolidated earnings per share ₹60.61 (issued capital) | **56.26×** |
| ÷ the same year's **own-account operating owner cash** of ₹1,18,779 lakh | **109.74×** |

> **The constraints do not halve the earnings and leave the multiple alone. They roughly double the multiple — `DERIVED: 109.74 ÷ 56.26 = 1.95×`.**

`DERIVED: ₹2,49,698 lakh of reported profit corresponds to ₹1,18,779 lakh of own-account operating owner cash — 47.57 paise in the rupee, a 52.43% haircut.` That step-down is the whole of what the investigation costs a valuation: float income out, own-corpus income out, associate out (the stake is valued separately), settlement fund at policy rather than at charge, a wedge reversion to a level **already observed**, then the 0.712 conversion.

## What the price implies

**ESTABLISHED.** Stripping the treasury at book and CDSL at market leaves **₹1,30,34,590 lakh — 93.72% of the price — on the operating exchange.** A no-growth capitalisation of own-account free cash flow is ₹13,79,400 lakh.

> **83.80% of BSE's market capitalisation is growth that has not been earned.**

Closing that gap requires own-account operating free cash flow to compound at **26.8% to 31.7% a year for ten years**. The company's demonstrated pre-windfall rate, on a consistent post-deconsolidation perimeter, is **13.89%** — a factor of **2.11×**.

**INFERRED — and only one thing can supply it.** The pool grew 6.46%.

| Required BSE growth | Pool at a generous 10%/yr | Pool at its actual 6.46% |
|---|---:|---:|
| **23.2%** (a 20× exit) | **67.0% of the pool** | **93.0% of the pool** |
| 15.7% (the base case) | 35.8% | 49.6% |

> **At the growth the price requires, BSE must become effectively the whole Indian exchange income pool within a decade — from 21.58% today and 14.44% two years ago. Even the base case requires 35.8%.**
>
> **And the variable that must compound for ten years is one a regulator sets and has already moved three times in twenty-one months.**

## The scenarios, and why they are asymmetric

| | FY2029 operating earnings | Implied multiple | The assumption that drives it |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Bear** | ₹98,787 lakh | **131.95×** | The wedge reverts to a level **already printed in this cycle**, and the cost base still rises |
| **Base** | ₹3,03,444 lakh | **42.96×** | The wedge holds flat; premium turnover +4.8%/yr beyond the level already achieved |
| **Bull** | ₹4,83,201 lakh | **26.98×** | The wedge holds at its **all-time-high quarterly level**, realisation flat not rising |

> **The bull case improves the multiple 1.59×. The bear case worsens it 3.07×. That asymmetry is not a modelling artefact — it is the cost ratchet meeting a revenue line whose activity a regulator allocates.**

Note what the bull case is *not* allowed to assume: rising realisation. **No disclosed realisation series at BSE is currently rising.**

## The finding that inverts the intuition

A company with 78% of shareholders' capital in securities looks defensive. **It is the opposite, and there are two independent proofs.**

**Arithmetic.** Strip the treasury at book and CDSL at market and the multiple gets **worse** — 66.59× on normalised operating earnings against 55.70× headline. **A pool earning 4.42% inside a 20.47× book multiple cannot be a discount.**

**Historical.** FY2019 → FY2021: profit attributable fell **37.0% on revenue that did not fall at all**, because the cost base is a ratchet **and** investment income fell 36.2% at the same time and through the same cause — rates come down when activity comes down.

> **The two shock absorbers fail together. And the same mechanism is live now: the own-corpus yield already fell 156 basis points, and total treasury income across both pools fell 2.28% on an earning base that grew 21.6%.**

**The retention gap, sized both ways.** Capitalised at its own return the treasury is worth ₹1,92,457 lakh rather than ₹5,22,509 lakh — a gap of **₹3,30,052 lakh: 49.46% of shareholders' equity and 2.37% of the market capitalisation.** That pair is why the balance sheet is decisive for the **quality** judgement and almost irrelevant to the **price** judgement.

## The margin of safety the evidence requires

**Pay on ₹1,18,779 lakh of own-account owner cash, not ₹2,49,698 lakh of reported profit**, on growth no faster than the demonstrated 13.89%, with the clearing subsidiary at a wider discount than the exchange.

Set by three things, each established above and none of them an opinion:

1. **Cash conversion of 0.712** over ten years, with no tested bound reaching parity.
2. **An operating leverage with no single value** — so the cost rows in any model are assumptions a reader is entitled to replace without contradicting a single disclosed fact.
3. **~70% concentration that is MD&A-only and unaudited** — the concentration cannot be verified from the audited statements at all.

**And a sensitivity that runs the other way, carried because it does.** The 0.712 is a decade figure; FY2026's own own-account conversion is **0.902**. On that basis the multiple is 86.62× rather than 109.74×. **The constraint stack removes and never adds, so it is a floor on owner cash rather than a central estimate.**

# PART IV — THE MOST DEFENSIBLE INTERPRETATION

**Stated as plainly as the evidence allows, and no further.**

**BSE is a toll booth attached to a rulebook it does not write.** It collects a fee on activity it cannot create, at a rate set inside a framework a regulator controls, on a product whose calendar that regulator allocates. In FY2026 the regulator handed it a symmetric expiry allocation and doubled the lot size; the resulting mix shift produced the best unit economics in the company's history in a year when the number of contracts it cleared **fell**. Nine-tenths of the income it gained came out of its competitor.

**The accounts recording all of this are trustworthy.** In ten years, **zero rupees of realised loss is attributable to an accounting channel.** Every realised loss came from a regulator, a counterparty, or a capital-allocation decision. The money risk at BSE is regulatory; the accounts are not where it lives. **The earnings-quality verdict is MODERATE, not because the arithmetic is doubtful, but because the most-used cash figure cannot be used as printed.**

**What management controls, it executes well and reports incompletely.** It delivers 65.2% of what it says it will build and 33.3% of the reassurances it offers about numbers it does not control — a 2.0× gap, and every one of the period's earnings shocks landed in the second category. It has never diluted a shareholder in nine years, has no promoter, pays its chief executive 7.4% of the statutory ceiling, and told the tape about an adverse order at 22:43 on the night it was passed.

**And what it does with the proceeds is the one thing wholly inside its control and the one thing it has not explained.** ₹1 is becoming ₹1.04 a year on 78% of shareholders' capital, inside a business earning 195%, with no dividend policy in ten annual reports and every offered explanation accounting for at most one twentieth of the retention.

**The price asks for none of that to matter.** At 56.26× reported earnings — 109.74× the cash those earnings actually represent — **83.80% of the market capitalisation is growth not yet earned**, requiring a decade at roughly twice the company's demonstrated rate, deliverable only by taking most of the competitor's remaining share, in a product whose expiry day a regulator assigns and has already reassigned three times in twenty-one months.

> **The defensible interpretation is not that BSE is a bad business or that its accounts are unreliable. It is that a genuinely excellent year, produced by variables the company neither owns nor prices, is being extrapolated for a decade — and that the disclosures a reader would need to detect the extrapolation failing early have been progressively withdrawn.**

**The honest counterweight, at full strength.** BSE beat its competitor under identical rules; the buybacks created ₹22,083 crore of value against ₹658 crore paid; the accounting errors run against the company; the levy moved in its favour; the credit loss is one named debtor; and there is no evidence the share gain has reversed. **A reader who believes the expiry calendar will hold is entitled to weight all of that heavily against everything above, and this report records that a reasonable analyst does.**

# PART VI — THE INTEGRITY RECORD

**This analysis made errors. They are listed here rather than in an annexe, because a reader weighing our arithmetic against BSE's is entitled to know how often we were wrong, in which direction, and whether we caught it ourselves.**

**Six process defects. Eighteen positions withdrawn or corrected. Three of the six were the orchestrator's own.**

**1. A factor-of-ten error, and it propagated.** The CDSL stake was published as *"25.2% of market capitalisation"*. It is **2.52%** — market capitalisation divided by ₹13,908 crore when it is ₹1,39,080 crore. **It reached a downstream task brief before being caught.** It inverts the finding's meaning: the stake is a large *balance-sheet* fact (45.03% of equity) and a **small valuation one**, because BSE trades at 20.47× book.

**2. A percentage that reproduced on no profit figure in the corpus, carried through six stages.** The retrospective SEBI levy was published as *"₹22,421 lakh — 21.8% of FY2024 PAT"*. **Both figures are withdrawn.** The settled amount is **₹7,347 lakh = 9.52%**, which the FY2024 note itself states. The ghost was later located: `16,977 ÷ 77,839 = 21.81%` — **a correct percentage struck on the FY2024 charge, then attached to the wrong numerator.**

**3. A position attributed to an analysis that had explicitly refuted it.** A benign reading of a missing cash flow was recorded as one analysis's position when its own text rated the chain BROKEN and refuted that reading. **A weaker position was paraphrased into a stronger claim while logging a contradiction.**

**4. A dependency violation.** The valuation was dispatched before the bear case it depended on had completed, and four of its constraints were incorporated only afterwards. **No conclusion reversed. The harness had no dispatch-time check, and one was added only after this was caught.**

**5. The bear case made the same class of error as (1) — and its own recompute caught it.** Market capitalisation written as ₹13,90,803 lakh in four places. Every ratio built on it was independently correct, so nothing propagated.

**6. The finished report could not be persisted by the process that wrote it.** The assembly step produced all nineteen sections and the harness refused to let it write the file. **It correctly refused to evade the block, and said so.** The content reached this document through two chat messages, one of which truncated and had to be re-requested.

**And one defect in the working material:** the management-questions list was mechanically assembled by pattern-matching, which truncated entries mid-sentence and swept in prose that was never a question. It was rebuilt from source.

**Eighteen corrections against 118 findings and 55 chain records. In every case the correcting evidence was already inside the corpus rather than newly fetched** — so the failure rate is in **citation and carry-forward**, not in extraction.

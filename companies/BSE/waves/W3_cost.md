### 1.2 The 1,243 residual — closed

W1_ledger's carve-out arithmetic reproduced independently and confirmed:
`33,884 − 4,177 − 218 − 2,083 − 235 − 9,170 = 18,001` against the FY2025 AR's restated FY2024
*Administration and other expenses* of **16,758** — residual **1,243** `[AR FY24, Note 28, PDF
p208-209/pr202-203]` and `[AR FY25, Note 29, PDF p196/pr185]`.

**It is the discontinued operation, and it ties to the rupee.** The FY2025 AR restated FY2024 onto a
continuing-operations basis. The income carved out is `1,61,790 − 1,59,588 = 2,202`; the discontinued
operation's PBT is **330**; therefore the expense carved out must be `2,202 − 330 = 1,872`
`[AR FY24, Consolidated P&L, PDF p157/pr151]` and `[AR FY25, Consolidated P&L, PDF p156/pr145]`.

The FY2024 expense lines that fell on restatement are exactly four:
`employee benefits 20,471 − 19,906 = 565` · `depreciation 9,544 − 9,493 = 51` ·
`technology 13,744 − 13,731 = 13` · `administration residual 1,243`.
`565 + 51 + 13 + 1,243 = 1,872`. **Exact.** The residual is not unexplained; it was simply never
labelled. Second, independent route: the line-by-line delta of the 24 surviving administration
sub-lines between the two presentations also nets to **−1,243** (largest movers *Miscellaneous*
−1,126, *Professional fees* −838, offset by *Postage and telephone* +421 and three sub-lines the
FY2024 note did not carry at all — printing and stationery 48, property taxes 234, repairs to other
assets 208).

> **Consequence.** RF-015 said a reader "is not comparing the same thing". Correct for *Administration
> and other expenses*. **Not correct for the regulatory levy**, which is the line that matters — see 1.3.

### 1.3 The like-for-like series, and why it survives both changes

Define **regulatory levy** = SEBI regulatory fees + contribution to SEBI + contribution to investors'
service fund + contribution to investors' protection fund. These four components are named identically
in every year of the window, on both sides of both presentation changes.

The invariance is provable, not asserted. FY2024 on the **as-first-reported** route:
`16,977 (P&L face) + 235 + 2,083 + 4,177 + 218 (inside Note 28) = 23,690`. FY2024 on the
**as-restated** route: `17,212 + 2,083 + 4,177 + 218 = 23,690`. **Identical.** The presentation change
moved 6,713 between captions and created nothing; and the discontinued operation carried **zero**
regulatory levy, which is why the 1,243 residual sits entirely in administration expenses.

| ₹ lakh, consolidated | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SEBI regulatory fees | 348 | 17,212 | 33,141 | 56,616 |
| Contribution to SEBI | 1,787 | 2,083 | 2,569 | 2,688 |
| Investors' service fund | 3,584 | 4,177 | 5,068 | 5,386 |
| Investors' protection fund | 189 | 218 | 268 | 279 |
| **Regulatory levy (like-for-like)** | **5,908** | **23,690** | **41,046** | **64,969** |
| YoY | — | +300.98% | +73.26% | +58.28% |
| Contribution to core SGF | 5,496 | 9,170 | 9,000 | 7,696 |
| **Total regulatory cost** | **11,404** | **32,860** | **50,046** | **72,665** |
| Levy ÷ revenue from operations | 7.24% | 17.28% | 13.88% | 13.44% |
| Total regulatory cost ÷ revenue | 13.98% | 23.97% | 16.92% | 15.03% |

Revenue bases: FY2023 81,553; FY2024 **1,37,104 restated** (as first reported 1,39,003 gives 17.28%
→ 17.04% and 23.97% → 23.64%, and the choice must be declared per `restatements.md`); FY2025 2,95,734;
FY2026 4,83,395. Sources `[AR FY24, Note 28, PDF p208-209/pr202-203]`, `[AR FY25, Note 28, PDF
p196/pr185]`, `[AR FY26, Note 27, PDF p212/pr201]`.

**Answer to the brief: yes, the series can be built, and it is the only one of the three regulatory
channels that survives both changes cleanly.** The two that do not are *Administration and other
expenses* (1,243 of discontinued-operation cost removed with no label) and *Contribution to core SGF*
(comparable in the P&L but **not** in the cash flow — see the Core SGF chain, L7).

## 5. The regulatory-function spend: real decline, wrong denominator

W2_gov_early measured spend on BSE's own regulatory function falling from **4.17% to 1.36%** of
standalone revenue, FY2019 → FY2026. **Verified independently at source, both years:**

- FY2019: "direct and indirect expenses amounting to ₹ 2,555 Lakh as per activity based accounting"
  `[AR FY2019, Board's Report §14, PDF p41/pr32]`; standalone total revenue 61,239
  `[AR FY2019, Board's Report, PDF p25/pr16]`. `2,555 ÷ 61,239 = 4.172%`.
- FY2026: "₹ 6,567 Lakhs as per activity-based accounting methodology" and 333 resources deployed
  `[AR FY26, Board's Report §14, PDF p32/pr21]`; standalone total revenue 4,83,634
  `[AR FY26, Board's Report, PDF p23/pr12]`. `6,567 ÷ 4,83,634 = 1.358%`.

**Is it a presentation artifact of §1's changes? No, and the reason is structural.** Both the numerator
and the denominator sit **outside** the financial statements. The numerator is a SECC activity-based-
accounting disclosure in the Board's Report; the denominator is standalone total revenue, which no note
reclassification touched. Neither the FY2025 Core SGF move nor the FY2026 SEBI-fee absorption can reach
either number. The two series are not the same measure and W2_gov_early said so explicitly.

**But the ratio is a denominator effect, and on a workload denominator it reverses.** Spend rose
`6,567 ÷ 2,555 = 2.570x` (+157.0%) while the revenue it is divided by rose `4,83,634 ÷ 61,239 = 7.898x`
(+689.8%). Applying denominators that actually proxy supervisory workload:

| Denominator | FY2019 | FY2026 | Spend per unit | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standalone total revenue (₹ lakh) | 61,239 | 4,83,634 | 4.172% → 1.358% | **−67.4%** |
| Listed companies (count) | 5,262 | 5,663 | `2,555 ÷ 5,262 = 0.486` → `6,567 ÷ 5,663 = 1.160` lakh | **+138.8%** |
| Equity cash ADT (₹ cr) | 3,127 | 7,950 | `2,555 ÷ 3,127 = 0.817` → `6,567 ÷ 7,950 = 0.826` lakh per ₹cr | **+1.1%** |

Listed-company counts `[AR FY2019, MD&A, PDF p75/pr66]` and `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p57/pr46]`.

> **The decline is arithmetically real and is not an artifact. It is also not evidence of
> under-resourcing on its own,** because the denominator that produces it — standalone revenue — grew
> 7.9x on a quantity that generates almost no incremental supervisory workload per rupee: index-option
> premium, ~95% of which sits in two weekly expiries on one index (RF-020). Per supervised issuer the
> spend rose 2.39x; per rupee of cash-market turnover it is flat.

## 6. Contradiction recorded, not resolved (doctrine §10)

**RF-019 sizes the retrospective re-basing at ₹22,421 lakh "charged across FY24–FY25".** That figure is
`16,977 + 5,444`. The two numbers are not both charges:

- 16,977 is the **FY2024 P&L charge**, and the note says it includes 7,347 "in respect of periods up to
  March 31, 2023" `[AR FY24, Note 46, PDF p233/pr227]`.
- 5,444 is the **provision balance at 31-Mar-2025**, printed in the provisions note beside 16,977 as the
  prior-year balance of the same provision `[AR FY25, Note 17, PDF p191/pr180]`. FY2025's own cash flow
  confirms it is a drawdown, not an addition: "Increase / (decrease) in provision **(10,793)**" against
  +17,035 in FY2024 `[AR FY25, Consolidated Cash Flow, PDF p158/pr147]`, and `16,977 − 5,444 = 11,533`
  of the FY2024 provision was settled during FY2025.

Adding them counts part of the FY2024 charge twice. **The independently derived retrospective element is
7,347 lakh** — 9.52% of FY2024 consolidated PAT of 77,166, not 21.8% — and the implied-rate series
corroborates it: `(17,143 − 7,347) ÷ 34,75,477.85 = 0.002819` against FY2025 0.002993 and FY2026
0.002972, three flat years, whereas leaving the 7,347 in gives FY2024 0.004933, 65% above FY2025.

Both positions stand. W2_industry's **event** is correct and its qualitative force is undiminished; only
the rupee sizing is disputed, and the dispute is on the record for W7.

## 7. Findings

### FINDING: The largest expense in the group is not a cost decision, and the FY2026 increase is 101.7% volume and −1.7% rate
S1 WHAT: Regulatory contribution rose 41,046 to 64,969, +58.28%, becoming 35.4% of total expenses and the single largest line in the consolidated profit and loss `[AR FY26, Consolidated P&L, PDF p164/pr153]`.
S2 WHY: The mechanism is a statutory levy assessed on options NOTIONAL turnover at a rate SEBI sets, applied to a base that grew 69.11%. It is not a price the company negotiates, not a resource it consumes, and since SEBI's letter of 14-Aug-2024 reiterating the notional methodology it is not even a basis the company can contest `[AR FY25, Note 46, PDF p217/pr206]`.
S3 EVIDENCE: Decomposed on the standalone, where the fee is actually assessed: 55,625 against 33,123, +22,502. Volume effect at constant rate `33,123 x 0.69114 = 22,893`; rate effect `22,502 − 22,893 = −391`, i.e. `−391 / 22,502 = −1.74%` of the increase. The implied rate is `55,625 / 1,87,15,375 = 0.002972` lakh per ₹cr of ADT against `33,123 / 1,10,66,674 = 0.002993`, −0.70% `[AR FY26, Standalone Note 27, PDF p287/pr276]` and `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p58/pr47]`. **The asymmetry inside the levy:** SEBI regulatory fees are 87.1% of the line but 98.1% of its increase (`23,475 / 23,923`), while the three fund contributions are 12.9% of the line and 1.9% of its increase — so a line management describes as one thing is in fact one turnover-linked item plus three near-fixed ones `[AR FY26, Note 27, PDF p212/pr201]`.
S4 CONNECTS: The base mismatch that RF-012 and RF-030 identify is a LEVEL exposure, not the FY2026 delta, and in FY2026 it moved in BSE's favour. Had the fee been assessed on the same base as revenue — premium, +117.45% — at the same effective yield, the FY2026 standalone fee would have been `33,123 x 2.17450 = 72,027` against an actual 55,625, i.e. **16,402 lakh more**. The notional base was worth 16,402 to BSE this year precisely because the premium-to-notional ratio rose 28.58% (`0.104315% against 0.081126%`). This is the disconfirming half of the standing narrative and it is recorded as such.
DOMAINS: IncomeStatement, Notes, MgmtCommentary, Industry
DISCONFIRM: Searched for and found. (i) The 16,402 counterfactual above cuts directly against reading the notional base as a FY2026 headwind. (ii) Management's claim that the levy "does not impact the operating efficiency of the business" is very nearly true this year — it moved the consolidated cost-to-income ratio by only −0.06pp `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p76/pr65]`. (iii) The levy fell as a share of revenue, 13.88% to 13.44%. What survives all three: the direction of the ratio is set by a market microstructure quantity, not by the company, and it can reverse without anything at BSE changing.
S5 MEANS: The single biggest line in BSE's cost base has zero management discretion in it after FY2024, moves one-for-one with a turnover number BSE does not bill on, and in FY2026 happened to move favourably — so a margin computed after it is a market-structure quotient rather than an operating result, and the direction of the next move is not a company variable.
BASE-RATE: Realised, at this company. The FY2024 charge of 17,212 was 49.5x the FY2023 charge of 348, and 7,347 of it was for conduct in FY2007-FY2023 under a different reading of the same rule `[AR FY24, Note 46, PDF p233/pr227]`. In the wider system NSE's regulatory fees FELL 17.27% in a year its revenue fell 3.15% (W2_peers), so the levy has demonstrably moved independently of revenue in both directions at both venues.
S6 IMPLICATION: Earnings durability is bounded by a cost that is 13.4% of revenue and is set by the quotient of two exogenous market ratios, so the FY2026 margin should not be extrapolated without the premium-to-notional ratio being carried as an explicit assumption. Monitorable: the quarterly premium and notional ADT disclosure — a reversion of the ratio to FY2025's 0.081126% at unchanged premium turnover would raise the standalone levy by roughly 55,625 x (0.104315 / 0.081126 − 1) = 15,900 lakh. Thesis-breaker: a further retrospective re-basing, or a rate change, neither of which is forecastable from any BSE disclosure.
SEVERITY: High

### FINDING: One clearing cost, three published magnitudes — the MD&A quotes the middle one and calls it structural efficiency (RF-025, verified and sharpened)
S1 WHAT: Clearing and settlement expense fell 34,805 to 28,035, −19.45%, in a year notional turnover rose 69.11%; the MD&A explains a −5% decline as "a sustainable structural efficiency gain" `[AR FY26, Consolidated P&L, PDF p164/pr153]` and `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p76/pr65]`.
S2 WHY: The mechanism is a counterparty mix shift plus a transfer price, not a rate change.

## 1. THE DRIVER MODEL

Doctrine §1f: the section leads with the conclusion. **BSE's earnings are the product of five quantities,
and BSE sets none of them.** Four are market or regulatory ratios; the fifth is a yield. The model below
is built from those five and from nothing else — no broker number, no consensus, no third-party estimate
enters this file at any point.

### 1.1 Driver 1 — premium average daily turnover, and BSE's share of it

Revenue is earned on **option premium**, not on notional and not on contracts. Management states the
mechanism plainly: *"the SEBI turnover fee is based on the notional trading, the clearing expense is based
on the number of contracts, and we earn revenue based on premium"* `[Q4FY26 Concall 13May2026, MD & CEO]`.

| | FY2025 | FY2026 | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Premium ADT (₹ crore) | 8,978 | **19,523** | `DERIVED: 19,523 ÷ 8,978 = 2.1745`, **+117.45%** |
| Equity-derivatives transaction charges, standalone (₹ lakh) | 1,41,554 | **3,13,437** | `DERIVED: 3,13,437 ÷ 1,41,554 = 2.2143`, **+121.43%** |

`[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p58/pr47]` and `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p75/pr64]`.

**The concentration this creates, and the counter-proportion that makes it a finding.** Equity derivatives
are `DERIVED: 3,13,437 ÷ 4,46,951 = 70.13%` of **standalone** revenue from operations and
`DERIVED: 3,13,437 ÷ 4,83,395 = 64.84%` of **consolidated** revenue from operations. Inside the *same*
transaction-charge line, equity derivatives rose **+121%** while equity cash fell **−18%** — so the growth
of the aggregate is not a statement about trading activity, it is one product. **Neither share alone says
that.** And per constraint 6, **all of it is unaudited**: the audited Note 22 stops immediately above the
split, at `DERIVED: 3,79,499 ÷ 4,83,395 = 78.51%` of revenue in a single line, while Auction charges of
₹60 lakh — `DERIVED: 60 ÷ 4,83,395 = 0.012%` of revenue — get their own audited caption
`[AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200]`. NSE, at a 0.14pp identical concentration, splits the identical line
five ways inside its audited note.
`NOT-DISCLOSED -> an Ind AS 115.114 disaggregation note in the FY2027 annual report; NSE's FY2026 Note 27 is the peer control.`

**The share, not the market, is what moved.** The two-exchange income pool grew
`DERIVED: 23,861.10 ÷ 22,413.31 = 1.0646`, **+6.46%**, in FY2026 while BSE's share of it went
`14.44% → 21.58%`, +714 basis points `[SECONDARY: NSE Q4FY26 results as reported, 2026-05-05]` against
`[AR FY26, Consolidated P&L, PDF p164/pr153]`. **Roughly nine-tenths of BSE's incremental income came out
of NSE rather than out of market growth.** That is the single most important fact for any growth
assumption in §4, and §2.3 turns it into an arithmetic test the price must pass.

### 2.2 The reverse discounted cash flow — what growth is embedded

**Base:** own-account operating free cash flow **₹1,65,528 lakh** (§1.5).
**Value to explain:** ₹1,30,34,590 lakh, i.e. **78.75×**.
**Structure:** ten explicit years compounding at *g*, then 4% in perpetuity (below nominal Indian GDP),
discounted at *r*. **Capex is held flat at ₹35,932 lakh throughout**, which is generous to the price —
a business compounding at these rates would reinvest more — so the required *g* below is if anything
understated.

**No beta is used and none can be** (§0.2). The discount rate is an explicit assumption and the answer is
shown across a band rather than at a point. A reader who disagrees with *r* can read the row.

| Discount rate *r* | Terminal growth | **Required 10-year growth in own-account operating FCF** |
|---|---|---:|
| **11%** | 4% | **26.8% a year** |
| **12%** | 4% | **29.3% a year** |
| **13%** | 4% | **31.7% a year** |

`DERIVED`, at r = 12%: with `x = (1 + g) ÷ 1.12`, the model is
`x(x¹⁰ − 1) ÷ (x − 1) + 13.0 × x¹⁰ = 78.75`, where `13.0 = 1.04 ÷ 0.08`. At g = 29.32%, x = 1.15464,
x¹⁰ = 4.2043, the explicit stage contributes 24.11 and the terminal 54.65, total **78.75** — the price.
At r = 11% the terminal factor is `1.04 ÷ 0.07 = 14.857` and g solves at 26.83%; at r = 13% it is
`1.04 ÷ 0.09 = 11.556` and g solves at 31.66%.

**The same question asked a way a reader can check without a perpetuity.** If the exchange is worth some
finite multiple of earnings in ten years and the shareholder requires 12%, what growth is needed?

| Exit multiple on FY2036 operating earnings | **Required 10-year growth** |
|---|---:|
| 15× | **26.2% a year** |
| 20× | **23.2% a year** |
| 25× | **20.9% a year** |
| 30× | **18.9% a year** |

`DERIVED`, at a 20× exit: `x = 1.232 ÷ 1.12 = 1.100` exactly, x¹⁰ = 2.5937; explicit stage
`1.100 × (2.5937 − 1) ÷ 0.100 = 17.53`; terminal `(20 × 1,95,739 ÷ 1,65,528) × 2.5937 = 23.65 × 2.5937 =
61.34`; total **78.87** against the required 78.75. The exchange must therefore compound its operating
earnings at **23.2% a year for a decade** even for a reader willing to pay 20× for it in 2036.

**The pool cannot supply it. Only share can.** The two-exchange income pool grew **+6.46%** in FY2026
(§1.1). Take a generous 10% a year for the pool. Then for BSE's revenue to compound at *g* for ten years,
its share of the pool must reach:

> **At the growth the price requires, BSE must become the larger of the two Indian equity exchanges by
> income within ten years — 67.0% of the pool on a generous pool assumption, and effectively the whole of
> it on the pool's actual FY2026 growth rate.** Even this file's base case requires BSE's share to reach
> 35.8%, from 21.58% today and 14.44% two years ago.

**RECONCILIATION with `W7_bearcase.md` §4.3 — the two files agree, and the residual is a denominator.**
That file reaches **75.7%** of the FY2036 pool where this one reaches 93.0%, and the gap is not a

### 3.3 What the sum-of-parts implies about the allocator, separately from the price

**The treasury is a quality problem, not a price problem, and the arithmetic separates the two.**

- At book it is 3.76% of the price. The market is paying approximately ₹1 for ₹1 of retained cash, so
  retention is roughly value-neutral **in market terms** at the margin.
- Its **intrinsic** worth if never distributed is the perpetuity of its own return against the cost of
  equity: `DERIVED: 4.42% ÷ 12% = 0.36833`, so `DERIVED: 5,22,509 × 0.36833 = 1,92,457` lakh — **₹47.18
  per share** rather than ₹128.10.
- **The gap is `DERIVED: 5,22,509 − 1,92,457 = 3,30,052` lakh — 49.46% of shareholders' equity
  (`DERIVED: 3,30,052 ÷ 6,67,301 = 49.46%`) and 2.37% of the market capitalisation
  (`DERIVED: 3,30,052 ÷ 1,39,08,031 = 2.37%`).**

> **The value forgone by permanently retaining the treasury at 4.42% is ₹3,30,052 lakh — roughly half of
> shareholders' equity and roughly two and a third percent of the share price.** That pair is why the balance
> sheet is decisive for the *quality* judgement and almost irrelevant to the *price* judgement, and why a
> sum-of-parts that stopped at "the cash is 3.76% of the price" would have missed the finding.

**The drag, as the accounts state it.** One year's retention costs **1,129 basis points** of return on
closing equity — `DERIVED: 47.89% − 36.60% = 11.29pp`, where 36.60% is `2,48,725 ÷ 6,79,550` as retained
and 47.89% is `(2,48,725 − 7,802) ÷ (6,79,550 − 1,76,486)` had it been distributed. The accumulated pile
costs **10,707 basis points** — `DERIVED: 143.67% − 36.60% = 107.07pp`. And the weight is moving the wrong
way: own-account financial assets were `DERIVED: 3,71,074 ÷ 4,42,373 = 83.88%` of equity attributable at
FY2025 and `DERIVED: 6,14,282 ÷ 6,67,301 = 92.06%` at FY2026;
**`DERIVED: 2,43,208 ÷ 2,24,928 = 108.13%` of FY2026's increase in shareholders' equity went into the
securities book.**

**Nothing management has offered explains it.** Regulatory net-worth floors are
`DERIVED: 20,100 ÷ 6,79,550 = 2.96%` of equity; capital trapped in the clearing subsidiary is
`DERIVED: 9,067 ÷ 1,76,486 = 5.1%` of the year's retention; corrected cash capex of ₹35,932 lakh is
**smaller** than the ₹41,198 lakh proposed dividend (`DERIVED: 41,198 ÷ 35,932 = 1.15×`); and the parent's
retained earnings are `DERIVED: 4,55,828 ÷ 41,198 = 11.07×` the proposed dividend, so legal capacity is not
a constraint `[AR FY26, Note 35, PDF p222/pr211]`, `[AR FY26, Standalone Note 15, PDF p280/pr269]`.
**There is no dividend policy in any of ten annual reports.**

### 3.3a The treasury is a dilutant, not a cushion — the two shock absorbers fail together (constraint 15)

**The intuition this section exists to kill.** A company with 78.30% of shareholders' capital in
securities looks as though it carries downside protection. **On this evidence it carries the opposite**,
and there are two independent proofs.

**Proof one, arithmetic — the strip test.** Removing the cash makes the multiple **worse**. §2.1 and §3.2
show it: the headline is `DERIVED: 1,39,08,031 ÷ 2,49,698 = 55.70×` on market capitalisation to PAT
attributable, and stripping the treasury at book and CDSL at market gives **60.61× on the bear case's
denominator and 66.59× on this file's** (§3.2a). A pool earning 4.42% after tax inside a 20.47× book
multiple cannot be a discount; it is the low-return fraction of a high-multiple whole.

**Proof two, historical — the absorbers are correlated, and the correlation is causal.** FY2019 to FY2021
is the precedent and it is in this corpus:

| ₹ lakh, consolidated | FY2019 | FY2020 | FY2021 | Movement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Revenue from operations | 45,030 | 45,051 | 50,137 | **flat, then up** |
| Investment income | 20,218 | 15,866 | 12,908 | `DERIVED: 12,908 ÷ 20,218 = 0.6384`, **−36.2%** |
| **PAT attributable** | 19,417 | 12,227 | 14,490 | `DERIVED: 12,227 ÷ 19,417 = 0.6297`, **−37.0%** |

`records/trend_10y.csv`, from `[AR FY2020, Consolidated P&L]` and `[AR FY2021, Consolidated P&L, PDF p146/pr141]`.

> **Profit fell 37.0% on revenue that did not fall at all**, because the cost base is a ratchet *and*
> investment income fell 36.2% at the same time and through the same cause — rates come down when activity
> comes down. **The treasury does not offset a downturn; it participates in one.** The same mechanism is
> live today in reverse: the own-corpus yield already fell **156 basis points** to 5.942% in FY2026 on a
> 43.9% larger corpus, and combined treasury income across both pools **fell 2.28%** on an earning base
> that grew 21.6% (§1.4).

**What this file does about it, stated so the modelling is checkable.** The base, bear and bull scenarios
in §4 value **the operating exchange only** and carry the treasury separately in §3 — not because the two
are independent, but precisely because they are not. Modelling them together at a blended growth rate
would let a mechanically-compounding retention pile disguise an operating decline, which is the exact
error FY2020 demonstrates. **No scenario in §4 credits the treasury with cushioning a revenue reversion,
and the ₹5,22,509 lakh is carried at book in every case rather than being grown or drawn down.**

### 3.4 The clearing subsidiary, valued separately (constraint 9)

W7 requires **a wider margin of safety on the clearing subsidiary's contribution to value than on the
exchange's.** Sized both ways, because the earnings answer and the tail answer are different:

**On earnings, the exposure is small and bounded.** ICCL's PAT is ₹16,979 lakh
`[AR FY26, Form AOC-1, PDF p312/pr301]`, of which the float treasury income after tax is ₹12,861 lakh
(W3_revenue establishes the entire ₹17,142 lakh arises outside the parent). So ICCL's contribution to the
*exchange's* normalised operating earnings is `DERIVED: 16,979 − 12,861 = 4,118` lakh, i.e.
`DERIVED: 4,118 ÷ 1,95,739 = 2.10%`. **Writing that contribution to zero costs 2.10% of the operating value
— `DERIVED: 1,30,34,590 × 2.10% = 2,73,726` lakh, 1.97% of the market capitalisation.**

**On the tail, it is not bounded at all, and that is the actual requirement.** The ₹10,60,000 lakh
inter-CCP bank guarantee was removed and the FY2025 comparative **actively restated to ₹24 lakh**, with no
Ind AS 1.41 reclassification and no Ind AS 8.49 error correction. Sized on the balance sheet of its own
date, it is `DERIVED: 10,60,024 ÷ 4,57,570 = 2.32×` total equity and
`DERIVED: 10,60,024 ÷ 10,34,165 = 102.5%` of total assets at 31-Mar-2025 — **an off-balance-sheet exposure
that exceeded the group's entire balance sheet** `[AR FY25, Consolidated Note 38 fn 3, PDF p209-210/pr198-199]`
→ `[AR FY26, Consolidated Note 37, PDF p227/pr216]`.
`NOT-DISCLOSED -> ICCL's own s.136 / MCA-filed FY2026 financial statements, and the SEBI-mandated ICCL risk
disclosures on the BSE website.`

> **The margin of safety on the clearing subsidiary is therefore not an earnings adjustment worth 2.10% —
> it is the requirement that the reader accept, without being able to verify it from the parent's filings,
> that ₹10,600 crore never was a Group obligation. That is an assumption, not a measurement, and the
> exchange's 93.72% of the price sits on top of it.**

## 5. THE MARGIN OF SAFETY REQUIRED

Doctrine §1c permits the margin of safety and the reason for it. **This section states a required margin,
not a price.**

### 5.1 The stack — what one rupee of reported profit is worth as owner cash

Every step is a constraint this run imposed, applied to the same FY2026 audited base:

| Step | ₹ lakh | Running | Constraint |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| PAT attributable, audited consolidated, total operations | 2,49,698 | **2,49,698** | — |
| less float treasury income after tax `DERIVED: 17,142 × 0.7503 = 12,861` | (12,861) | 2,36,837 | Map B / overlay §2 |
| less own-corpus investment income after tax `DERIVED: 29,030 × 0.7503 = 21,782` | (21,782) | **2,15,055** | second engine, valued separately |
| less CDSL associate | (6,842) | 2,08,213 | **constraint 4** |
| less Core SGF top-up to policy | (11,279) | 1,96,934 | **constraint 3** |
| less discontinued operations | (1,195) | **1,95,739** | non-recurring |
| less wedge reversion to the **observed** Q2FY26 level, after tax `DERIVED: 38,535 × 0.7503 = 28,914` | (28,914) | 1,66,825 | §1.2 — a level seen inside this cycle, not a forecast |
| **× ten-year own-account cash conversion of 0.712** | | **1,18,779** | **constraint 1 / 11** |

`DERIVED: 1,66,825 × 0.712 = 1,18,779`. And `DERIVED: 1,18,779 ÷ 2,49,698 = 47.57%`.

> **₹2,49,698 lakh of reported profit corresponds to ₹1,18,779 lakh of the exchange's own-account owner
> cash — 47.57 paise in the rupee — once the eleven constraints and one already-observed level of the
> valuation's most volatile driver are applied.**

**Against that, the price:** `DERIVED: 1,30,34,590 ÷ 1,18,779 = 109.74×`.

**The two numbers, side by side, are the margin-of-safety statement:** the shares change hands at
**56.26× audited consolidated earnings per share** and at **109.74× the same year's own-account operating
owner cash on this run's own constraints**. The required margin of safety is the distance between those two
numbers, and it is `DERIVED: 109.74 ÷ 56.26 = 1.95×` — **the constraints roughly double the effective
multiple.**

### 5.2 The three named sources of the requirement, sized

**(i) The cash conversion — 0.712, and no bound reaches parity.** Over a decade BSE converted roughly
seven-tenths of reported profit into cash its shareholders own, while the published statement said better
than one for one. `DERIVED: 44.0%` of a decade of headline CFO is float (35.5%) or off-basis Core SGF
(8.5%). The bounds tested were **0.609 · 0.667–0.757 · 0.712 · 0.825 · 0.874**, and **none reaches 1**.
Applied to the multiple: a model built on the published statement pays for **₹64.21** of cash per ₹100 of
revenue where the shareholders' claim is **₹44.01** — a `DERIVED: 64.21 ÷ 44.01 = 1.459×` overstatement of
the cash the price is being paid for.

**(ii) The operating leverage has no single value, and the sign flips.** The identical cost base against
the three simultaneously-true turnover measures for FY2025→FY2026 gives **+0.094** (premium), **+0.159**
(notional) and **−1.682** (contracts) — a spread of `DERIVED: 0.094 − (−1.682) = 1.776` elasticity points
**including the sign**. That is not an analyst's choice of denominator: it is a choice among three
quantities the company itself says drive three different lines of its own income statement, and they
diverged by `DERIVED: 117.45 − (−6.53) = 123.98` percentage points in one year. **A margin of safety cannot
be set from a leverage estimate here, because there is no leverage estimate — there is a range whose
endpoints have opposite signs.** The consequence for the model is that §4's cost rows are assumptions and
cannot be derived, and the reader must treat them as such.

**(iii) ~70% of revenue in a product a regulator allocates, and the 70% is unaudited.** ~95% of BSE premium
sits in the two nearest weekly expiries; **premium share fell to 32.4% in June 2026 solely because NSE had
an extra weekly expiry that month**; and the split that establishes the concentration exists only in the
MD&A, on which **no auditor has opined**, while 4 of 4 peers publish the Ind AS 115 disaggregation. **The
margin of safety demanded of that earnings stream must be set against unaudited evidence** — the
concentration cannot be verified from the audited statements at all.
`NOT-DISCLOSED -> an Ind AS 115.114 disaggregation note, or a reconciliation of the MD&A product split to the audited Note 22 total.`

### 5.3 What the requirement amounts to, stated as a range a reader can disagree with

The honest form of a margin-of-safety statement here is **not** a single discount, because the three
sources above are of different kinds — one is a measured conversion ratio, one is an unmeasurable
elasticity, one is an unaudited disclosure. Stated as the reader must use it:

| The requirement | Sized | Why it is a floor, not a ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| **On earnings** — the multiple must be struck on ₹1,18,779 lakh, not ₹2,49,698 lakh | `DERIVED: 1 − 1,18,779 ÷ 2,49,698 = 52.43%` haircut to the headline | The wedge step uses a level already observed; a level below Q2FY26 has not been ruled out |
| **On growth** — the price requires 26.8%–31.7% for ten years against a demonstrated 13.89% pre-windfall rate | `DERIVED: 29.32% ÷ 13.89% = 2.11×` | The demonstrated rate is measured on a consistent perimeter; the higher nine-year rates contain the share windfall |
| **On the base year** — FY2026 is peak-cycle and the next quarter grew **+0.16%** | The first out-of-sample observation removes 97.5% of the sequential profit increase from the operating engine | It is one quarter, and Q1 is seasonally distinct — this cuts the other way and is carried |
| **On the clearing subsidiary** — wider than on the exchange (constraint 9) | Earnings exposure 2.10% of operating value; **tail exposure 2.32× FY2025 total equity, unsizeable from the parent's filings** | The settling document exists and is public but has not been read in this run |
| **On voluntary disclosure** — every unaudited series should be treated as available only while favourable | Seven instances of the perimeter narrowing; the Core SGF policy, the colocation split, the market-share series and the inter-CCP guarantee are all voluntary | Realised cost of the pattern to date is **nil** — this widens the margin, it does not establish a loss |

> **The margin of safety this evidence requires is that a reader pay for the exchange on ₹1,18,779 lakh of
> own-account owner cash rather than ₹2,49,698 lakh of reported profit, on a growth rate no faster than the
> 13.89% the company demonstrated before a share windfall it did not price, and with the clearing
> subsidiary's contribution carried at a discount to the exchange's because one off-balance-sheet exposure
> larger than the entire balance sheet can no longer be sized from the parent's filings. At ₹3,409.80 the
> price does the opposite of all three: it capitalises the reported number, at roughly twice the
> demonstrated rate, on accounts that close one quarter before the first stall.**

**And the disconfirming half, carried at full strength.** The base rate is decisive in the company's
favour on the one question a margin of safety is usually built to answer: **in ten years, zero rupees of
realised loss at BSE is attributable to an accounting-quality channel.** Every realised loss came from a
regulator, a counterparty or a capital-allocation decision. Five accounting defects run *against* the
company's interest. The recognition model has almost no discretion in it — point in time, at a rate filed
with SEBI, no variable consideration, policy unchanged, on 78.51% of revenue. **The margin of safety
required here is a margin against the drivers, not against the accounts**, and that distinction is what
separates this from a case where a valuation should not be struck at all.

# Sector overlay — Market Infrastructure Institution (exchange + clearing)

**Doctrine §7 inserts a sector overlay where the standard revenue engine does not fit.** The standard
template assumes an industrial/product company; the bank overlay assumes a balance sheet that earns a
spread. An exchange-and-clearing group is neither: it earns a **toll on activity it does not own**, holds
a **balance sheet that is largely other people's money**, and is **priced by its own regulator**.

This overlay defines what L1–L8 must trace for BSE. It is inserted between the triage gate and wave 2.

---

## 1. The earnings engine — what replaces "volume × price"

Every revenue line traces to an activity metric the company does not control, times a rate the regulator
can change. Both halves must be established separately; a change in either explains a different thing.

| Revenue line | Activity driver (L3) | Rate driver (L3) | Counterparty (L4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Equity-derivatives transaction charges** | Premium turnover / notional turnover, contracts traded, expiry-day concentration, market share vs NSE | Charge per ₹ crore of premium turnover; SEBI "true-to-label" constraints | Trading members, prop vs client |
| **Equity-cash transaction charges** | Average daily turnover, delivery vs intraday | Charge per ₹ crore | Trading members |
| **StAR MF platform** | Order count, distributor count, SIP registrations | Per-order fee | AMCs / distributors |
| **Listing fees** | Listed-company count, main board vs SME, market-cap slabs | Annual fee slab, initial listing fee | Issuers — *whom BSE also regulates* |
| **Book building fees** | IPO count, issue size | Fee schedule | Issuers |
| **Colocation / data / index** | Racks, bandwidth, licence count, index AUM | Rate card | Members, data vendors, AMCs |
| **Investment income** | Own corpus **and** float balances | Yield, mark-to-market | — |

**Mandatory L3 rule for this sector.** A transaction-charge line must be decomposed into
**activity × realisation**, never left as a rupee total. "Revenue grew because volumes grew" is not an
L3 answer if realisation moved at the same time — and realisation is exactly what regulation changes.

**Mandatory L4 rule.** Listing and book-building fees are paid by **the companies BSE regulates as an
exchange**. That is a structural conflict inherent to the model, not a scandal — but it is an L4
counterparty fact and must be stated, not assumed away.

## 2. The balance sheet — most of it is not the company's money

This is the single biggest departure from the standard template, and it breaks L6 and L7 if ignored.

| Balance | Whose money | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Member deposits, margin money, settlement obligations | **Members'** | Moves through group cash; corrupts CFO |
| Core Settlement Guarantee Fund (core SGF) | **Restricted** — regulatory, cannot be distributed | Not available to shareholders; excluded from any equity-return measure |
| Investor Protection Fund / IPFT | **Restricted, earmarked** | Same |
| Own investment corpus | Company's | The only pool that is genuinely shareholders' |

**Mandatory L6/L7 rule.** Own-account cash must be separated from member and settlement float before any
cash-conversion statement is made. Where the disclosure does not permit the split, the honest L7 is
`NOT-DISCLOSED` and the conclusion is that conversion **cannot be measured** — never a headline CFO/PAT
ratio computed over the commingled total.

**Mandatory equity rule.** Return on equity computed on total equity overstates the return on
shareholders' capital to the extent restricted funds sit inside it. State which basis is used.

> **CORRECTION, 2026-09-08, from W3_balancesheet — the DIRECTION above is wrong for BSE.**
> This rule predicted total-equity ROE would **overstate** the shareholder return. At BSE it
> **understates** it, and the reason is structural: the **Core SGF (₹1,24,683 lakh) is presented as its
> own caption *between* equity and liabilities** and is therefore already outside the denominator. What
> *is* inside equity is **₹93,485 lakh of non-distributable reserves** (demutualisation capital reserve,
> business-combination reserve, FCTR, LES reserve, abeyance-earmarked general reserve), which pulls
> reported ROE **down by 8.95pp**.
>
> The overstatement channel the rule describes does exist, but it is narrow: it runs only through
> **Treasury income on clearing and settlement funds (₹17,142 lakh)** — income on members' money booked
> inside revenue — worth ₹12,861 after tax, 5.15% of PAT, **2.71pp of ROE**.
>
> **Net: reported ROE of 43.75% understates the return on distributable shareholders' capital by
> 6.24pp** (distributable-equity ROE **52.70%**).
>
> **The generalised rule, corrected:** do not assume the direction. Establish *where the restricted funds
> actually sit* — inside equity, or in a caption of their own — before choosing a basis. Both errors are
> available, and which one applies is a presentation fact, not a principle.

## 3. Costs that exist only in this sector

- **Regulatory contribution / SEBI fees** — scales with turnover, so it behaves like a revenue-linked
  levy rather than an operating cost. Rising with revenue is expected; rising *faster* is the finding.
- **Contribution to core SGF** — regulatory, driven by risk and volumes, charged below the PBT line in
  BSE's presentation. Its placement in the P&L changes what "operating margin" means; state the placement.
- **Clearing and settlement expense** — paid to the clearing corporation. If consolidated, largely
  intra-group; movement against volumes is diagnostic.
- **Cost of the regulatory function itself** — surveillance, inspection, member supervision. An exchange
  that under-funds this is buying near-term margin with regulatory risk. Track as % of revenue, not in
  rupees, and against penalty history.

## 4. Governance additions for an MII

1. **No promoter, by design.** A demutualised exchange has no controlling shareholder and SEBI caps
   individual holdings. Promoter tests have **no subject** — they are *inapplicable*, not *clean*.
   Recording them as passes would be a false clean.

   > **CORRECTION, 2026-09-08, from W1.5_triage.** This section originally read "**six** of the standard
   > eight-point screen's promoter tests have no subject". **That was wrong, and the error was mine.**
   > The six-item list it referred to (pledge · promoter RPTs · promoter salary · shareholding trend ·
   > cross-holdings · inter-corporate lending) is **not** the eight numbered screen points. Mapped
   > across, three of those six collapse into screen point 1, one into point 4, and the last two kill
   > only the *promoter limb* of points 2 and 6 — which survive on a substituted subject.
   >
   > **Correct position: 2 points wholly INAPPLICABLE (1, 4); 2 applicable on a substituted subject
   > (2, 6); 4 applicable as written (3, 5, 7, 8).**
   >
   > Marking six numbered points inapplicable would have been a **false gap** — the mirror image of the
   > false-clean error this rule exists to prevent, and just as misleading.
2. **The regulator appoints part of the board.** Public interest directors are SEBI-approved. Board
   independence must be assessed against that structure, not the ordinary listed-company test.
3. **The company regulates its own customers.** Listing fees come from entities BSE supervises.
4. **The regulator sets the prices.** Charge structures, expiry-day rules, position limits and
   true-to-label requirements are SEBI's to change. Pricing power sits with the regulator, not the
   company — which is where the industry analysis (L3 rate driver) must locate it.
5. **Penalties are a supervision signal, not a cost.** The rupee amount is usually trivial against PAT;
   the pattern and the function they come from are the finding.

## 5. What the standard template would get wrong here, if applied unmodified

| Standard step | Failure on an MII | Overlay replacement |
|---|---|---|
| Operating leverage from variable/fixed cost split | Costs are largely fixed; the swing factor is exogenous market volume | Leverage to **turnover**, both directions, plus the regulated rate |
| Capital intensity per ₹1 of revenue | Near-zero physical capital; capital is regulatory (SGF, net worth) | Regulatory capital per ₹1 of revenue |
| Working-capital cycle (inventory + receivable − payable days) | No inventory; "payables" include member money | Own-account working capital only |
| CFO/PAT conversion | Commingled with settlement float | Own-account CFO only, or NOT-DISCLOSED |
| ROE on total equity | Includes restricted funds | ROE on distributable equity, stated separately |
| Promoter tests | No promoter exists | Mark inapplicable; assess regulator-administered risk instead |

> **Correction to §4.4, from W2_industry (2026-09-07).** The overlay said "the regulator sets the
> prices" and named SEBI. Two errors:
>
> **(a) Incomplete.** The rate is set by **SEBI, RBI and the Finance Ministry** — the overlay named only

>
> **(b) Wrong on share.** Pricing power is **two powers sitting in two places**. The *rate* is the
> regulator's, near-absolutely. The *share* is contestable, and it moved decisively. In FY2026, under
> identical rules, identical STT and identical RBI directions, NSE's total income fell 2.4% and PAT
> 15.5% while BSE's consolidated total income rose 59.1% and PAT 88.3%; the combined pool grew only
> 6.5% (₹22,413 → ₹23,861 cr) and BSE's share of it went 14.44% → 21.58%. **Roughly nine-tenths of
> BSE's incremental income came out of NSE, not out of market growth.** SEBI prices a unit of activity;
> it does not decide whose venue the unit occurs on.
>
> The one node BSE prices freely is the index, bought back from S&P Dow Jones effective 1-Jun-2024.

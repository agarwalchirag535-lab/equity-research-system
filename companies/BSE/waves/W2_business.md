# W2 — Business understanding: BSE Limited

Wave 2 artifact (doctrine §7). **Deliberately contains no financial figures of any kind** — no revenue,
no margin, no ratio, no growth rate, no market-share percentage, no rupee amount. Where a number would
normally carry the point, the mechanism carries it instead. Sizing is waves 3–4's job; this file exists
so that those waves know what they are sizing.

## 1. One page a non-finance reader can follow

**BSE does not buy or sell securities. It sells access to a place where other people do, and it sells the
by-products of running that place — the record of what happened there, the right to sit close to it, and
the right to be listed in it.** Everything it charges for is one of those three things, or the mirror of
them in a second market it also runs (mutual fund orders instead of share orders).

**Conclusion first: BSE is a toll booth attached to a rulebook.** The toll is charged on activity BSE does
not own and cannot create — somebody else decides to trade, to list, to buy a mutual fund unit — and the
size of the toll is set inside a framework the regulator controls, not by BSE. The rulebook half is not
optional decoration: BSE is required to police the very people who pay it, and it says so in its own
words, calling itself a *"First Level Regulator"* with published notices, circulars and guidelines for the
entities under its purview [AR FY26, MD&A §11.B, PDF p71/pr60].

Eight lines, for a reader with no finance background:

1. **BSE runs marketplaces.** An order-matching system for shares, share derivatives, bonds, exchange-traded
   funds and a few other instruments, plus a separate order-routing system for mutual fund purchases called
   StAR MF [AR FY26, MD&A §3, PDF p57–59/pr46–48].
2. **It charges brokers a small fee every time a trade happens on it.** That fee is recognised the instant
   the transaction occurs, at a specified rate [AR FY26, consol Note 2.3.10, PDF p177/pr166].
3. **It charges companies for being listed on it** — an annual fee for staying listed, and separate fees for
   the software and machinery of raising money (book building, buy-backs, reverse book building)
   [AR FY26, MD&A §4.B, PDF p63/pr52].
4. **It sells the trading record.** Basic price data is given away free to investors and to members; the
   enhanced, faster, deeper feed is sold — domestically by BSE, internationally by Deutsche Börse under a
   partnership [AR FY26, MD&A §4.C, PDF p63/pr52; Concall 13Aug2024 filing (call 07Aug2024), MD & CEO].
5. **It rents physical proximity.** Colocation is rack space in BSE's own data centre so that a trading firm's
   computers sit next to the matching engine. Management's account is that it was built as market
   infrastructure and became a profit centre incidentally
   [AR FY26, MD&A §8.A.III, PDF p66–67/pr55–56; Concall 13Feb2026 (call 09Feb2026), MD & CEO].
6. **It licenses its indices.** The SENSEX and the rest are designed and maintained by a wholly owned
   subsidiary and licensed to fund managers, insurers and other exchanges for products that track them
   [AR FY26, MD&A §8.B.III, PDF p67/pr56].
7. **The money it holds is mostly not its money.** Members' margins and deposits, clearing bank deposits,
   settlement obligations, issuers' security deposits and restricted regulatory funds all sit on the group
   balance sheet [AR FY26, consol Note 16, PDF p206/pr195].
8. **It is priced, supervised, and partly staffed by its regulator.** SEBI approves every director, appoints
   Public Interest Directors, sets the minimum net worth, and levies a fee on BSE calculated on turnover
   [AR FY26, CG Report §2.A, PDF p78/pr67; AR FY26, consol Note 35, PDF p222/pr211; AR FY25, consol Note 46,
   PDF p217/pr206].

**The single sentence a non-finance reader should leave with:** BSE earns a fraction of a fraction of other
people's transactions, is paid by intermediaries rather than by the people whose money is moving, and is
obliged to spend part of what it earns policing the people who pay it.

## 2. Everything BSE charges for, who pays, and what triggers the payment

**Conclusion: there are four genuinely distinct revenue engines — activity tolls, tenancy fees, information
rents, and float — and they are triggered at four different moments in the customer relationship.** That
matters more than the list itself: the activity tolls are recognised the instant a trade occurs and cannot be
banked in advance, while the tenancy fees are collected before the service is delivered and sit as a
liability until earned [AR FY26, consol Note 19 *Income received in advance* / *Advance from customers*,
PDF p208/pr197].

| What is sold | Who writes the cheque | The problem it solves for them | What triggers the obligation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Equity-derivatives transaction charge** | Trading member (broker), for its own book or its client's | Access to a matched, guaranteed, anonymous market in index and stock derivatives | Point in time, as the transaction occurs, at a specified rate [AR FY26, consol Note 2.3.10, PDF p177/pr166] |
| **Equity-cash transaction charge** | Trading member | Same, for shares; and access to the exclusive scrips that only BSE lists | Same trigger. Charged at a *special rate* for exclusive stocks and a *normal rate* for stocks traded on both bourses [Concall 13May2026 (call 07May2026), MD & CEO; Investor Presentation filed 07Aug2025] |
| **StAR MF order charge** | **NOT DISCLOSED** — see §8 | A single pipe from any distributor to any asset manager, in demat or statement-of-account mode, for lump sums, SIPs, switches and withdrawals [AR FY26, MD&A §3.B.III, PDF p57–58/pr46–47] | Per order, as the order is processed; management has confirmed the investor is not charged and has treated charging the investor as a hypothetical suggestion [Concall 13Aug2024 filing (call 07Aug2024), MD & CEO] |
| **Annual listing fee** | The listed issuer — equity, debt and mutual fund schemes | Continuous access to public capital, price discovery, liquidity, and a compliant channel for disclosure to its own shareholders [AR FY26, BRSR §19.c, PDF p108/pr97] | Recognised straight-line over the period it relates to; cash collected ahead of that period [AR FY26, consol Note 2.3.10, PDF p177/pr166] |
| **Book building fee / other issue-related fees** | The issuer raising money | The bidding machinery of an IPO, an offer for sale, an offer to buy, a buy-back, a reverse book build | Point in time, on completion of the book building process [AR FY26, consol Note 2.3.10, PDF p177/pr166] |
| **Membership admission and annual subscription / processing fees** | Trading members | The right to be a member at all, under the Deposit Based Membership scheme | Straight-line over the period; membership fees recognised the same way as listing fees [AR FY26, MD&A §4.A, PDF p63/pr52; consol Note 2.3.10, PDF p177/pr166] |
| **Colocation and connectivity charges** (incl. throttle) | Trading members and algorithmic firms | Physical latency — a rack next to the matching engine, and the bandwidth to use it | Over the contract period, calculated on specified charges for data-centre racks [AR FY26, consol Note 2.3.10, PDF p177/pr166] |
| **Data services** | Domestic data customers directly; international customers via Deutsche Börse, which is the licensor outside India | A machine-readable, low-latency, enhanced feed. Basic data is free to investors and to members; the enhanced product is the thing sold | Recognised as the performance obligation is satisfied [AR FY26, MD&A §4.C, PDF p63/pr52; Concall 13Aug2024 filing (call 07Aug2024), MD & CEO] |
| **Index licensing and subscription** | Asset managers, insurers, other exchanges, global institutions | A rule-based, independently administered benchmark they can build a product on or be measured against | Straight-line over the period the fee relates to [AR FY26, consol Note 2.3.10, PDF p177/pr166; MD&A §8.B.III, PDF p67/pr56] |
| **Technology / software solutions and services** | Members and third parties buying IT support, development, customisation, maintenance | Systems they would otherwise build | Over the period the service is performed; third-party software passed through at gross [AR FY26, consol Note 2.3.10, PDF p177/pr166] |
| **Clearing and settlement fees** (earned by the clearing subsidiary) | Clearing members | Removal of counterparty risk — see §4 | Point in time as the transaction completes, per the fee scales [AR FY26, consol Note 2.3.10, PDF p177/pr166] |
| **Auction charges** | Members who fail to deliver | Resolution of a failed delivery | **NOT DISCLOSED** as a mechanism — a labelled line in the revenue note with no explanatory text [AR FY26, consol Note 22, PDF p211/pr200] |
| **Treasury income from clearing and settlement funds** | Nobody — this is earned *on* money held, not charged *to* anyone | It solves nothing for a customer; it is the return on balances the group holds because it clears trades | **NOT DISCLOSED** — see §8. It sits inside *revenue from operations*, not investment income [AR FY26, consol Note 22, PDF p211/pr200] |

**The asymmetry inside this list is the finding.** Two streams are charged per event and cannot be
pre-sold (transaction charges, book building). Four are charged per period and are collected before the
period runs (listing, membership, colocation, index subscription). One is not charged to anyone at all
(treasury income on clearing and settlement funds). A business described as "transaction-driven" therefore
contains a block of pre-collected annuity revenue and a block of return-on-float that no customer ever
authorises — and only the first block is what the risk section calls exposed to trading activity
[AR FY26, MD&A §11.A, PDF p70/pr59].

**How the money physically arrives.** The documents describe the cash mechanics only obliquely, and the
description that exists is a risk disclosure rather than a process note: the group states it manages credit
risk *"by collecting deposits, advance collections, and collaterals in form of cash and other securities
for trading, clearing and listing services"* [AR FY26, consol Note 34, PDF p219/pr208]. Read with the
*Income received in advance* and *Advance from customers* balances [AR FY26, consol Note 19, PDF p208/pr197]
and the members' deposits in Note 16 [PDF p206/pr195], the picture is that BSE is substantially pre-funded
and collateralised across all three of its main relationships. Credit exposure is described as limited
*"given the regulated nature of operations"*, with one customer large enough in the receivable book to be
flagged as a concentration [AR FY26, consol Note 34, PDF p219/pr208].

**What is not explained: the billing cycle itself.** No document in this set states when a transaction-charge
bill is raised, over what period charges are aggregated, whether they are debited from a member's settlement
account or invoiced separately, or what happens on non-payment. **NOT DISCLOSED.** What would answer it: the
Exchange's own notices and circulars on transaction-charge billing, and the member Rules, Bye-laws and
Regulations — neither of which is in the annual-report or concall corpus.

## 3. The walk: one retail trade, from the app to BSE's revenue line

**Conclusion: BSE touches the retail investor at no point, is paid by neither the investor nor the investor's
counterparty, and its revenue is fixed by a variable — the option premium — that is decided by market
volatility rather than by BSE, by the investor, or by the broker.** That last point is management's own
framing, and it is the single most important sentence about this business model:

> *"the SEBI turnover fee is based on the notional trading, the clearing expense is based on the number of
> contracts, and we earn revenue based on premium"*
> — [Concall 13May2026 (call 07May2026), MD & CEO]

Three different quantities, three different parties, one trade. The walk, end to end:

**Step 1 — The investor.** A retail investor opens a broking app and places an order. The investor has no
relationship with BSE. The investor is identified inside the system by a unique client code registered by
the broker; the exchange knows the code, not the person [Investor Presentation filed 07Aug2025, equity
derivatives slide].

**Step 2 — The trading member (broker).** The broker is the only party in this chain with a contract with
BSE. It is a *member*, admitted under the Deposit Based Membership scheme, and it has posted a deposit with
the exchange before it was allowed to send a single order [AR FY26, MD&A §4.A, PDF p63/pr52; consol Note 16
*Deposits received from trading members*, PDF p206/pr195]. The broker runs its own risk check, then routes
the order.

**Step 3 — The routing decision (where BSE's revenue is won or lost).** The broker decides *which* exchange
receives the order. For a stock listed on both venues, that decision is a competitive one; for a
BSE-exclusive scrip it is not, and BSE charges a *special rate* on those [Concall 13May2026 (call
07May2026), MD & CEO]. Smart Order Routing — splitting one order across venues to get the best price —
is the mechanism that would make the broker venue-agnostic. Management's stated position is that BSE has
cleared the SOR applications and that they remain pending at the competing exchange, and that this is what
it infers to be impeding its cash-market share [Concall 13May2026 (call 07May2026), MD & CEO]. **A single
routing decision, taken inside a broker's software, is the gate on an entire revenue line.**

**Step 4 — The matching engine.** The order reaches BSE's trading system. Firms that want to reach it
fastest rent a rack in BSE's colocation facility; that rent is itself a revenue line, and it exists because
latency is a competitive input for the firms trading, not because BSE wanted a landlord business
[AR FY26, MD&A §8.A.III, PDF p66–67/pr55–56; Concall 13Feb2026 (call 09Feb2026), MD & CEO]. Pre-trade risk
controls sit here too — Limit Price Protection validates limit-order prices against a reference band in the
derivatives segment [AR FY26, MD&A §6.C, PDF p64/pr53].

**Step 5 — The trade exists, and revenue is recognised.** The order matches. At that instant, and not
before, BSE recognises a transaction charge measured at the specified rate [AR FY26, consol Note 2.3.10,
PDF p177/pr166]. **This is the moment the revenue line moves.** For an index option, the base of the charge
is the premium — the price of the option — not the notional value of the contract it controls.

**Step 6 — Novation to the clearing corporation.** The trade is passed to Indian Clearing Corporation
Limited (ICCL), which steps in as *central counterparty to eligible trades* [AR FY26, consol Note 34,
PDF p218/pr207]. The original buyer and seller no longer face each other; each faces ICCL. ICCL charges a
clearing fee, recognised at the point the transaction completes, per its fee scales [AR FY26, consol
Note 2.3.10, PDF p177/pr166]. At standalone level, BSE pays a *clearing and settlement expense* to the
clearing corporations; on consolidation the intra-group part eliminates
[AR FY26, MD&A §V.5, PDF p76/pr65].

**Step 7 — Margin and collateral.** The clearing member's exposure is already covered: members must
*"maintain prefunded collateral with conservative haircuts based on VaR"*, with netting arrangements and the
Settlement Guarantee Fund behind that, and daily stress testing across all segments under SEBI methodology
[AR FY26, consol Note 34, PDF p219/pr208]. Those margins sit on the group balance sheet as
*Deposit and margins from members* — a liability, not equity [AR FY26, consol Note 16, PDF p206/pr195].

**Step 8 — The clearing banks and the depository.** Cash moves through clearing banks, which have themselves
posted deposits with the group [AR FY26, consol Note 16 *Deposit from clearing banks*, PDF p206/pr195].
Securities move in the depository system: on the sell side, securities are blocked for pay-in in the client's
own demat account when a pledge is invoked or released, and the broker must sell them the same day so that
they do not accumulate in the broker's demat account [AR FY26, MD&A §5.E, PDF p63/pr52]. One of the two
depositories, CDSL, is an associate of BSE — not a subsidiary
[AR FY26, consol Note 2.2 *Particulars of subsidiaries and associates*, PDF p171/pr160].

**Step 9 — Settlement.** Obligations net down and settle; the unsettled leg sits as *Settlement obligation
payable* [AR FY26, consol Note 16, PDF p206/pr195]. The settlement cycle has been shortened to T+1, with an
optional same-day cycle introduced for qualified stock brokers [Investor Presentation filed 07Aug2025,
milestones slide; AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p64/pr53 area — SEBI circular on optional T+0].

**Step 10 — The levies flow back out.** On the same trade, BSE owes SEBI a regulatory fee computed on annual
turnover, and SEBI has advised that for option contracts that turnover is the **notional** value
[AR FY25, consol Note 46, PDF p217/pr206]. It also owes a contribution to the Investors' Services Fund
computed off listing fees, a contribution to the Investor Protection Fund, and a separate contribution to
SEBI [AR FY26, consol Note 27, PDF p212/pr201]. Separately, it contributes to the Core Settlement Guarantee
Fund, which once contributed is non-refundable even if the fund later exceeds its required corpus
[AR FY26, consol Note 2.3.12, PDF p178/pr167].

**The walk's punchline.** Between Step 5 and Step 10, three different bases are in play on one trade —
premium (BSE's revenue), notional (SEBI's fee), contract count (the clearing cost). Management states
explicitly that the relationship between them cannot be predicted, because premium is a function of
volatility and volatility is driven by events outside the company
[Concall 17Nov2025 (call 11Nov2025), MD & CEO; Concall 13May2026 (call 07May2026), MD & CEO].
**The company's own account is that a mechanical wedge sits inside its most important revenue line and that
the wedge moves for reasons nobody at BSE controls.**

## 4. The entity map, in words

**Conclusion: BSE Limited is a holding company that operates one of its regulated functions itself (the
exchange) and is required to hold the other in a separately capitalised subsidiary (the clearing
corporation) — and the separation is a regulatory command whose rationale the documents assert but never
explain.**

**BSE Limited** — the exchange. Established 1875; corporatised and demutualised under the SEBI-notified
BSE (Corporatization and Demutualization) Scheme, 2005, with demutualisation completed in 2007, *"separating
ownership and management"* [AR FY26, consol Note 1, PDF p169/pr158]. It has no promoter by construction; its
own shares are listed on the competing exchange, NSE, and it confirms in its own corporate-governance report
that it has paid NSE the annual listing fee, like any other issuer
[AR FY26, consol Note 1, PDF p169/pr158; AR FY26, CG Report, General Shareholder Information, PDF p92/pr81].
**BSE is simultaneously a competitor of, a customer of, and a supervised peer alongside, the exchange on
which its own equity trades.** It must maintain a
minimum net worth prescribed by the SECC Regulations, 2018 [AR FY26, consol Note 35, PDF p222/pr211].

**Indian Clearing Corporation Limited (ICCL)** — wholly owned, the group's only *material* subsidiary
[AR FY26, Board's Report §11.E, PDF p32/pr21]. Principal activity: clearing and settlement
[AR FY26, consol Note 2.2, PDF p171/pr160]. Management's own description is that ICCL manages *"Collateral,
Risk Management, Clearing, and Settlement functions"* across several segments and a diverse product range
[Investor Presentation filed 07Aug2025, ICCL slide]. Its capital is regulated separately by SEBI, with its
own minimum net-worth floor under the SECC Regulations, 2018, computed as the higher of a stated minimum
and a risk-based calculation [AR FY26, consol Note 35, PDF p222/pr211].

**Why they must be separate — what the documents support and what they do not.** Three structural facts are
documented and they point the same way, without the rationale ever being stated in words:

1. **Separate regulatory capital.** ICCL's net worth is set by SEBI under a different regulation from the
   exchange's, and is risk-sensitive rather than flat [AR FY26, consol Note 35, PDF p222/pr211]. Capital
   that absorbs settlement failure is therefore ring-fenced inside the clearing entity.
2. **A default fund that only the clearing entity holds.** The Core Settlement Guarantee Fund is established
   *by ICCL*, per segment, *"to guarantee the settlement of trades executed in respective segment"*, and
   contributions to it are non-refundable once made [AR FY26, consol Note 41, PDF p231/pr220; consol
   Note 2.3.12, PDF p178/pr167]. The exchange contributes to it but does not hold it — and the standalone
   accounts contain no Core SGF note at all, only the policy and the expense (W1 docmap §6 #9).
3. **The fund is multi-exchange, which only works if the clearer is a separate institution.** ICCL's Core
   SGF disclosure shows contributions arriving from ICCL itself, from BSE, and from other exchanges
   including NSE and MSE, plus an "other" category comprising an amalgamation transfer, penalties
   transferred by BSE at SEBI's direction, and fines collected from members
   [AR FY26, consol Note 41, PDF p231–232/pr220–221]. **Trades executed on one exchange can be cleared at
   another group's clearing corporation.** That interoperability is only coherent if clearing is a distinct,
   separately capitalised institution rather than a department of a trading venue.

The one place the documents come close to the *reason* is the risk note: the group is exposed to
*"clearing and settlement risk arising from role of its clearing corporations as central counterparty"*, and
capital is held *"to maintain investor, clearing member, creditor and market confidence for orderly
settlement"* [AR FY26, consol Notes 34 and 35, PDF p218 and p222/pr207 and pr211]. **The rationale for
mandatory legal separation — that a member default must not reach the exchange's balance sheet or its
shareholders — is NOT DISCLOSED in these documents;** it is implied by the capital, fund and
interoperability structure but never argued. What would answer it: the SECC Regulations, 2018 themselves and
SEBI's rationale documents accompanying them, neither of which is in this corpus.

**The rest of the group.** BSE Technologies Private Limited — IT support services, and the vehicle for the
StAR NPS platform and for the stake in the power exchange [AR FY26, consol Note 2.2, PDF p171/pr160; MD&A
§8.B.I and §8.B.IV, PDF p67–68/pr56–57]. BSE Index Services Private Limited — index design, maintenance,
licensing and subscription, and an RBI-authorised financial benchmark administrator
[AR FY26, MD&A §4.D, PDF p63/pr52; consol Note 35, PDF p222/pr211]. India International Exchange (IFSC)
Limited and India International Clearing Corporation (IFSC) Limited — the GIFT City exchange and clearer,
majority- but not wholly-owned, regulated by IFSCA rather than SEBI, with their own net-worth floors
[AR FY26, consol Note 2.2, PDF p171/pr160; consol Note 35, PDF p222/pr211]. India INX Global Access IFSC
Limited — an intermediary for trading on overseas exchanges [AR FY26, consol Note 2.2, PDF p171/pr160].
CDSL is an **associate**, equity-accounted, not consolidated — which is why the group's segment disclosure
lost the Depository segment entirely after FY2017 (W1 docmap §5.4).

### Who the customers are, and whether they are the same people as the payers

**Conclusion: they are not, and the gap between the two lists is the structural fact about this business.**

BSE's own customer taxonomy names six groups: listed companies, trading members and brokers, investors,
SMEs and startups, mutual fund distributors and investors, and data customers
[AR FY26, BRSR §19.c, PDF p108/pr97]. Set that against who actually pays:

| Named as a customer | Writes BSE a cheque? |
|---|---|
| Listed companies / SMEs / startups | **Yes** — annual listing fee, book building, issue-related fees. And they are simultaneously the entities BSE supervises, delists and sanctions (§7) |
| Trading members and brokers | **Yes** — transaction charges, membership and subscription fees, colocation, deposits and margins. They are also the entities BSE inspects (§7) |
| Investors (retail and institutional) | **No.** They generate every rupee of transaction revenue and pay none of it. They receive basic data free, receive investor-awareness programmes funded out of BSE's own regulatory levies, and have a grievance mechanism run at BSE's cost [Concall 13Aug2024 filing (call 07Aug2024), MD & CEO; AR FY26, MD&A §7.A.IV, PDF p64–65/pr53–54] |
| Mutual fund distributors and investors | **Payer NOT DISCLOSED** — see §8 |
| Data customers | **Yes**, but domestic customers pay BSE directly while international customers are sold to, and licensed by, Deutsche Börse under a partnership in which Deutsche Börse owns sales and marketing outside India [AR FY26, MD&A §4.C, PDF p63/pr52] |
| Index clients (AMCs, insurers, other exchanges) | **Yes**, via the index subsidiary [AR FY26, MD&A §4.D, PDF p63/pr52] |

**Three consequences fall out of this table.**

- **The revenue-generating population and the paying population are separated by one intermediary in every
  case except listing.** BSE's demand signal reaches it filtered through a broker's routing software, a
  distributor's platform choice, or a fund manager's benchmark decision. It never reaches it directly from
  the person whose money is moving.
- **BSE's two largest paying constituencies are also its two supervised constituencies.** Issuers pay listing
  fees and are compulsorily delisted by BSE for non-compliance [AR FY26, MD&A §6/§7.B.III, PDF p66/pr55].
  Members pay transaction charges and are inspected by BSE, including thematic inspections of their
  technology governance [AR FY26, MD&A §7.A.II, PDF p64/pr53]. This is the MII overlay's structural conflict
  (W1.7 §1, §4.3), and it is inherent rather than aberrant.
- **The one internationally-sold product is sold by somebody else.** For market data outside India, the
  customer relationship belongs to Deutsche Börse, not BSE [AR FY26, MD&A §4.C, PDF p63/pr52].

## 5. The named drivers — conditions, not metrics

**Conclusion: only one of the eight conditions below is inside BSE's control, and it is the one management
talks about most.** Each is stated as a condition that must hold, per doctrine §2 L8.

**D1 — Brokers must be willing and able to route orders to BSE.** Not "market share must rise" — the
condition is that a broker's order-routing software is permitted, configured and approved to send orders to
BSE at all. Management's stated diagnosis of its cash-market position is that Smart Order Routing approvals
have not completed at the other venue and that clients therefore cannot be exchange-agnostic
[Concall 13May2026 (call 07May2026), MD & CEO; Concall 13Feb2026 (call 09Feb2026), MD & CEO]. Common contract
notes and a single VWAP were implemented as the enabling infrastructure
[Investor Presentation filed 07Aug2025, ICCL slide]. **This is the only driver BSE has been actively trying
to move, and its own account is that it is blocked outside the company.**

**D2 — Someone must want to trade at all.** BSE's own risk section leads with dependence on the volume and
value of trades, the number of orders on the mutual fund platform, the number of active investors, and the
number of new listings and capital raised — and states that many of the factors driving these are beyond
its control [AR FY26, MD&A §11.A, PDF p70/pr59; §9.A, PDF p68/pr57].

**D3 — Volatility must be present, because the derivatives toll is charged on premium.** Premium is a
function of volatility; volatility is driven by internal and external events; BSE earns on premium while its
levies are computed on notional and its clearing cost on contract count
[Concall 17Nov2025 (call 11Nov2025), MD & CEO; Concall 13May2026 (call 07May2026), MD & CEO]. **A calm market
is not merely a low-volume market for this business; it is a market where the base of the charge itself
shrinks relative to the base of the levy.**

**D4 — Companies must keep choosing to list, and to stay listed.** Both the initial event (book building,
listing processing) and the recurring annuity (annual listing fee) depend on it, and the annuity is the
straight-lined, pre-collected part of the business [AR FY26, MD&A §4.B, PDF p63/pr52; consol Note 2.3.10,
PDF p177/pr166]. The SME platform, the startup platform and the migration path to the main board are the
funnel that feeds it [AR FY26, MD&A §3.B.II and §3.C.IX, PDF p57 and p59/pr46 and pr48].

**D5 — The regulator must leave the charge structure alone, or change it in BSE's favour.** BSE's own threats
section says that regulatory decisions on ownership structure, *"associated fees and levies, and restrictions
on how Exchanges distribute their profit"* will continue to affect its competitiveness
[AR FY26, MD&A §9.B, PDF p68/pr57]. The overlay's hypothesis — that pricing power sits with SEBI rather than
with BSE (W1.7 §6) — is consistent with BSE's own framing. Note also that a levy basis can be changed
retrospectively: SEBI advised BSE to compute regulatory fees on notional value for option contracts
*"from the year 2006-07 onwards"* [AR FY25, consol Note 46, PDF p217/pr206].

**D6 — The systems must not go down, and must scale on the worst day.** MD&A treats trading-system capacity,
order-handling headroom, peak-load management, disaster recovery and uptime as core, and the auditor made
*Information Technology systems and controls* a Key Audit Matter in FY2024 and again in FY2026 (absent in
FY2025) [AR FY26, MD&A §8.A.III, PDF p66–67/pr55–56; W1 docmap §5.2]. Cybersecurity is named as a standalone
threat, with the point made that a breach damages brand, customer trust and investor confidence — i.e. the
loss channel is reputational and therefore volume-destroying, not just remedial cost
[AR FY26, MD&A §9.C, PDF p68/pr57].

**D7 — Members must remain solvent, and the default machinery must hold.** The clearing entity is central
counterparty; members post prefunded collateral with VaR-based haircuts; netting and the Settlement
Guarantee Fund stand behind that; daily stress testing runs across all segments under SEBI methodology
[AR FY26, consol Note 34, PDF p218–219/pr207–208]. The scenario sources are named explicitly: clearing
member default, inter-clearing-corporation default, and clearing bank failure [same note].
**A failure here is not a margin event; it is an existence event for the clearing subsidiary.**

**D8 — The regulator must keep approving the board, and the board must keep being approvable.** Under the
SECC Regulations the governing board is composed of Public Interest Directors (SEBI-approved, representing
investors), Non-Independent Directors representing shareholders who are not trading or clearing members, and
the MD/EDs; SEBI may appoint up to three nominee directors, and **all appointments of directors have been
approved by SEBI** [AR FY26, CG Report §2.A, PDF p78/pr67]. There is no promoter to backstop this
(W1.7 §4.1). The regulatory function itself runs under a Chief Regulatory Officer reporting to the MD & CEO
*and* to the Regulatory Oversight Committee [AR FY26, Board's Report §14, PDF p32/pr21].

## 6. What would have to be true for BSE to stop earning money

**Conclusion: this business does not have one kill switch; it has one slow bleed and three fast breaks, and
the slow bleed is the one the documents describe most carefully.** No forecast is offered below — each item
is a stated condition and its documented mechanism.

**The slow bleed — order flow re-routes and does not come back.** BSE competes for listings, clearing,
trading and settlement volumes, and distribution of financial products, and says so
[AR FY26, MD&A §11.A, PDF p70/pr59]. The mechanism by which flow moves is not price alone; it is where a
broker's software is configured and approved to send orders (D1). The corollary is uncomfortable and follows
from BSE's own account: **the same mechanism that has kept BSE's cash-market flow constrained would work in
reverse, and neither direction is inside BSE's control.**

**Fast break 1 — the regulator changes the base, the rate, or the product.** Three documented instances of
regulatory action reshaping a market's economics sit inside this corpus. (i) A regulatory clarification
reduced one whole segment's activity to nothing, and the associated settlement-guarantee corpus could not
simply be released and redeployed because that too requires regulatory discussion
[Concall 13Aug2024 filing (call 07Aug2024), MD & CEO]. (ii) SEBI re-specified the turnover base for
regulatory fees on options to notional value, retrospectively [AR FY25, consol Note 46, PDF p217/pr206].
(iii) Expiry-day scheduling for index derivatives has been changed repeatedly, and management's own analysis
ties the concentration of volume on expiry days — and therefore the premium-to-notional relationship — to
those changes [Investor Presentation filed 07Aug2025, milestones slide; Concall 13May2026 (call 07May2026),
MD & CEO]. **BSE's own threats section names "associated fees and levies" and "restrictions on how Exchanges
distribute their profit" as live regulatory variables** [AR FY26, MD&A §9.B, PDF p68/pr57].

**Fast break 2 — a settlement failure at the clearing corporation.** Named scenario sources: clearing member
default, inter-clearing-corporation default, clearing bank failure [AR FY26, consol Note 34, PDF p219/pr208].
The buffers are prefunded collateral, netting, the Core SGF and stress-tested margin. Contributions to the
Core SGF are permanently non-refundable [AR FY26, consol Note 2.3.12, PDF p178/pr167] — which means the cost
of a larger buffer is a permanent transfer out of shareholder reach, not a reversible provision.

**Fast break 3 — a technology or cyber failure.** Named as a threat in its own right, with the loss channel
stated as brand, customer trust and investor confidence rather than as direct cost
[AR FY26, MD&A §9.C, PDF p68/pr57]. The auditor's IT-systems Key Audit Matter appeared in FY2024, vanished in
FY2025 and returned in FY2026 (W1 docmap §5.2).

**Substitutes — what could replace BSE, and what could not.** For a stock traded on both venues, the
substitute already exists and is one routing table away. For a BSE-exclusive scrip there is no substitute,
which is why those carry a *special rate* [Concall 13May2026 (call 07May2026), MD & CEO]. For clearing, a
substitute exists structurally — interoperability means an exchange's trades can clear elsewhere, which is
visible in the multi-exchange composition of ICCL's Core SGF contributions
[AR FY26, consol Note 41, PDF p231–232/pr220–221]. For the mutual fund platform, the substitutes are the
other exchange-based platform and the direct, off-exchange route. For indices, the substitute is any rival
benchmark a fund manager chooses to track. **Only the exclusive-listing franchise and the SENSEX brand have
no ready substitute in these documents** [AR FY26, MD&A §8.A.I, PDF p66/pr55].

**Disconfirming evidence, sought and recorded.** Against the "flow can leave" thesis: the group's revenue is
not purely transactional. Listing fees and index and colocation subscriptions are straight-lined over
periods and pre-collected [AR FY26, consol Note 2.3.10, PDF p177/pr166; consol Note 19, PDF p208/pr197];
treasury income on clearing and settlement funds does not depend on any customer's decision to pay
[AR FY26, consol Note 22, PDF p211/pr200]; the company carries no borrowings, so a volume shock has no debt
covenant behind it (W1 docmap §6 #2; AR FY26, consol Note 35, PDF p222/pr211). Against the "regulation is
purely a threat" framing: several documented regulatory changes have run in BSE's favour or been neutral —
joint annual inspection by MIIs replacing separate assessments, common taxonomy adoption in which BSE's XBRL
work became the industry standard, and BSE's recognition as the administration and supervisory body for
Investment Advisers and Research Analysts [AR FY26, MD&A §5.D, PDF p63/pr52; §7.B.II, PDF p65–66/pr54–55;
§7.A.III, PDF p65/pr54].

## 7. The regulatory function versus the commercial function

**Conclusion: BSE performs a genuine state-delegated policing function over the very customers who pay it,
funds that function partly out of a levy carved off its own listing fees and partly out of its own P&L, and
accounts for the cost of it on an activity-based methodology that no other expense in the report uses.**

### What is regulatory, not commercial

BSE describes itself as a *"First Level Regulator"* responsible for the orderly functioning of the securities
market, and publishes notices, circulars and guidelines binding on the entities under its purview
[AR FY26, MD&A §11.B, PDF p71/pr60]. Concretely, the following are regulatory functions performed by a
listed company:

- **Market surveillance and investigation.** Automated alerts are generated, escalated to snap
  investigations, then to preliminary or detailed investigations, and reports are forwarded to SEBI
  [AR FY26, MD&A §7.A.I, PDF p64/pr53]. Surveillance frameworks — the Enhanced Surveillance Measure and a new
  Additional Measure for unusual price movement — are calibrated in joint meetings with SEBI and applied by
  BSE to listed scrips, including reduced price bands and restricted trading frequency
  [AR FY26, MD&A §6.A and §6.B, PDF p64/pr53].
- **Member oversight.** Routine inspections, joint inspections with SEBI, other exchanges and depositories,
  and thematic inspections covering technology governance, systems and cyber security, plus inspections of
  authorised persons, branches and remisiers [AR FY26, MD&A §7.A.II, PDF p64/pr53].
- **Supervision of investment advisers and research analysts.** SEBI granted BSE recognition as Investment
  Adviser Administration and Supervisory Body and Research Analyst Administration and Supervisory Body for a
  fixed term; BSE inspects them and handles complaints against them
  [AR FY26, MD&A §7.A.III and §7.A.IV, PDF p64–65/pr53–54].
- **Investor grievance handling.** BSE runs Common Investor Service Centres jointly with the competing
  exchange under a SEBI initiative — one shared centre per location serving both exchanges and SEBI. It
  redresses complaints against trading members, listed companies, registrars and transfer agents, advisers
  and analysts, routed through SEBI's SCORES system, and refers unresolved disputes into the SEBI Online
  Dispute Resolution mechanism which replaced BSE's own arbitration mechanism
  [AR FY26, MD&A §7.A.IV, PDF p64–65/pr53–54].
- **Listing compliance and enforcement.** Running the Corporate Announcement Filing System through which
  listed companies disclose to the market; building and maintaining the XBRL taxonomy that SEBI directed
  other exchanges to adopt as the common national standard; and **compulsorily delisting companies** that
  remain suspended for non-compliance [AR FY26, MD&A §7.B, PDF p65–66/pr54–55].
- **Investor education.** Investor awareness programmes run through the Investor Protection Fund and the
  Investors' Services Fund, regional seminars conducted jointly with SEBI, and participation in SEBI's own
  public campaigns [AR FY26, Board's Report §16.F, PDF p35/pr24; MD&A §11.B, PDF p71/pr60].

### Who pays for it

**Four distinct funding routes, and the payer differs in each — that is the finding, not the aggregate cost.**

1. **The Investors' Services Fund is funded by issuers.** Under a SEBI directive standing since 1996-97, BSE
   must set aside a fixed proportion of the annual listing fees it receives into the ISF; the balance rolls
   forward with contributions in, investment income credited to it, and expenses incurred on its behalf
   charged out, and investments are earmarked against the liability
   [AR FY26, consol Note 19(a), PDF p209/pr198]. **Listed companies therefore fund the investor-services
   function that supervises listed companies.**
2. **The Investor Protection Fund is a separate earmarked pool** with a contribution payable sitting as an
   earmarked liability [AR FY26, consol Note 19, PDF p208/pr197] and a contribution expense inside Regulatory
   contribution [AR FY26, consol Note 27, PDF p212/pr201]. Its stated purpose is claim settlement for
   investors against defaulting members, alongside awareness work [AR FY26, BRSR, PDF p154/pr143].
   **The precise funding formula is NOT DISCLOSED** — see §8.
3. **SEBI is paid out of turnover.** *Regulatory contribution* carries both a *Contribution to SEBI* and
   *SEBI regulatory fees* as separate lines [AR FY26, consol Note 27, PDF p212/pr201]; the latter is levied
   on annual turnover, with option turnover measured at notional value per SEBI's advice
   [AR FY25, consol Note 46, PDF p217/pr206]. **Traders therefore fund the regulator, through BSE, on a base
   larger than the one BSE itself charges on.**
4. **The rest comes out of BSE's own profit and loss.** BSE runs a dedicated Regulatory Division of
   specialised departments serving investors, members, advisers and analysts, issuers and regulators, under a
   Chief Regulatory Officer reporting to both the MD & CEO and the Regulatory Oversight Committee, and it
   separately states the direct and indirect expense of that function **computed on an activity-based
   accounting methodology** [AR FY26, Board's Report §14, PDF p32/pr21].

**The accounting placement is itself a disclosure choice with consequences.** Regulatory contribution became
its own P&L line and note only at FY2025; before that its substance sat elsewhere, and the separately visible
SEBI regulatory-fee note disappeared entirely in FY2026, its substance now netted inside Regulatory
contribution (W1 docmap §5.8 #1). Separately, *Contribution to core settlement guarantee fund* was moved
below a new subtotal, *Profit before contribution to core settlement guarantee fund*, from FY2025 onward
(W1 docmap §5.8 #10). **Two of the four regulatory-cost channels have changed presentation inside the deep
window; a reader comparing the cost of the regulatory function across FY2024 and FY2026 is not comparing the
same thing.** That is flagged here for W3_cost and W3_revenue, and sized nowhere in this file by design.

**The conflict, stated plainly and without alarm.** BSE inspects its members and delists its issuers, and
both groups are its paying customers [AR FY26, MD&A §7.A.II and §7.B.III, PDF p64 and p66/pr53 and pr55].
The structural mitigants the documents name are the SEBI-approved board with Public Interest Directors
representing investors, the Regulatory Oversight Committee to which the CRO independently reports, and
SEBI's power to appoint nominee directors [AR FY26, CG Report §2.A, PDF p78/pr67; Board's Report §14,
PDF p32/pr21]. **The class of error those mitigants cannot catch is under-investment in the regulatory
function itself** — a decision taken at the level of resourcing rather than at the level of a specific
enforcement outcome, and therefore invisible to a committee that reviews cases rather than budgets. The
activity-based regulatory-expense figure is the only disclosure in the corpus that bears on it, and it is a
single annual number with no comparative methodology note.

# 0. THE VERDICT, BEFORE THE EVIDENCE

> ## GATE VERDICT: **PROCEED**
>
> **No unresolved Critical flag survives adjudication.** All four Critical candidates are re-run
> against this gate's own question — *does anything here stop the process?* — and not against W5's
> question. All four are settled at **High**. Valuation proceeds, under the seven constraints W5 set
> and the four this gate adds (§7).

**The reasoning in one paragraph.** Seventy-eight register entries is a long file, and a long file
creates pressure toward a dramatic ending. The discipline that governs this gate is §1a-ii: **in ten
years, zero rupees of realised loss at BSE is attributable to an accounting-quality channel.** Every
realised loss in the window came from a regulator, a counterparty, or a capital-allocation decision.
Three of the four Critical candidates are **disclosed, quantified and priceable** — a levy, a
peak-cycle quarter, a cash-conversion caption whose correction already exists and closes on the balance
sheet to ₹49 lakh. The fourth, the guarantee, is the only one where the exposure genuinely stopped
being measurable — and even there the recognised balance sheet did not move, the settling document is
named and obtainable, and the exposure class has never produced a rupee of loss at this company or in
the Indian clearing system. A gate exists to stop a process that cannot be run honestly. This one can
be run honestly, provided it is run on the corrected numbers and states what it cannot see.

**What the gate does not say.** It does not say the register is noise. Thirty-six distinct issues
survive consolidation, **twenty-three of them at High**. The correct output of this gate is not a clean bill —
it is a **PROCEED with a named margin-of-safety requirement on the clearing subsidiary, a mandatory
substitution of the own-account cash series for the printed one, and a peak-cycle flag on the accounts
the valuation will be struck on.**

## 3.1 The retrospective SEBI levy: ₹22,421 lakh against ₹7,347 lakh

**Position 1** — W3_notes, W3_revenue, and RF-019 as written by the orchestrator: **₹22,421 lakh**,
reasoning `16,977 + 5,444`, stated as *"21.8% of FY2024 PAT"*
`[AR FY25, consolidated P&L, PDF p191/pr180]`.

**Position 2** — W3_cost: **₹7,347 lakh**, reasoning that 5,444 is a *provision balance* drawn down,
not a charge, and that the retrospective element is the amount the FY2024 note itself attributes to
prior periods `[AR FY24, Note 46, PDF p233/pr227]`.

### RULING: Position 2. The retrospective element is **₹7,347 lakh = 9.52% of FY2024 consolidated PAT.**

Settled against Note 17 and the FY2025 CFO provision line, exactly as the brief directs. Five
independent tests, and they all point the same way:

**(i) Note 17 prints 5,444 as a closing balance beside 16,977 as its opening balance.**
`[AR FY25, Note 17, PDF p191/pr180]`. A provision note reports balances. Adding a closing balance to
the charge that created it counts the same money twice — `16,977` is the charge and `5,444` is what
remained of it eleven months later.

**(ii) The FY2025 cash-flow provision line is `(10,793)` — a decrease.**
`[AR FY25, Consolidated Cash Flow, PDF p158/pr147]`. A year in which a further ₹5,444 lakh was
*charged* would show a provision **increase**. `DERIVED: 16,977 − 5,444 = 11,533` of drawdown on the
SEBI provision alone; the net line is `(10,793)`, so all other provisions rose
`DERIVED: 11,533 − 10,793 = 740`. The FY2024 comparator is the mirror image: `+17,035` against a charge
of `17,212`, i.e. the FY2024 levy was almost entirely non-cash
`[AR FY24, Consolidated Cash Flow, PDF p158/pr152]`.

**(iii) The FY2026 report proves it structurally, by moving the number.** The FY2025 comparative CFO
provision line was restated from `(10,793)` to `740`, with other liabilities restated from `(25,155)` to
`(36,688)` — an exact offset of `DERIVED: 36,688 − 25,155 = 11,533`, with cash generated from operations
unchanged at 73,812 `[AR FY25, PDF p158/pr147]` → `[AR FY26, PDF p165/pr154]`. And the FY2025 5,444 was
reclassified into other current liabilities in the FY2026 comparative:
`DERIVED: 47,947 − 42,503 = 5,444`, to the rupee
`[AR FY26, Notes 17 and 19, PDF p207-208/pr196-197]`. **A number that can be reclassified from
provisions to other liabilities at a balance-sheet date is a balance. A charge cannot be reclassified;
it has already gone through the P&L.**

**(iv) The implied levy rate corroborates it independently.**
`DERIVED: (17,143 − 7,347) ÷ 34,75,477.85 = 0.002819` for FY2024, against `0.002993` (FY2025) and
`0.002972` (FY2026) — three flat years. Leaving the 7,347 in gives
`DERIVED: 17,143 ÷ 34,75,477.85 = 0.004933`, i.e. `0.004933 ÷ 0.002993 = 1.648` — **65% above** the
following year on a rate that is set by circular and did not change
`[AR FY26, Note 27, PDF p212/pr201]`.

**(v) The FY2024 note says so in terms.** ₹16,977 lakh was provided *"including ₹7,347 lakhs in respect
of periods up to March 31, 2023"* `[AR FY24, Note 46, PDF p233/pr227]`. The retrospective element is
disclosed. It was never 22,421.

## 3.2 The guarantee sizing: 2.32× / 102.5% against 1.56× / 78.8% against 2.396×

`[AR FY25, Note 38, PDF p209-210/pr198-199]`.
**Position 2** — W3_balancesheet: **1.56× total equity, 78.8% of total assets**
`[AR FY26, Note 37, PDF p227/pr216]`.
**Position 3** — W6: **2.396× consolidated net worth** `[AR FY26, Consolidated Note 40, PDF p228/pr217]`.

### RULING: all three are arithmetically correct. Publish **Position 1**. The cause is exactly what the brief suspected — different years and different denominators.

**Position 1 uses the FY2025 balance sheet.**
`DERIVED: 10,60,024 ÷ 4,57,570 = 2.3167 = 2.32×` total equity;
`DERIVED: 10,60,024 ÷ 10,34,165 = 1.0250 = 102.5%` of total assets `[records/balance_cash_10y.csv]`.

**Position 2 uses the FY2026 balance sheet.**
`DERIVED: 10,60,000 ÷ 6,79,550 = 1.5599 = 1.56×`;
`DERIVED: 10,60,000 ÷ 13,44,614 = 0.7883 = 78.8%`.

**Position 3 uses FY2025 equity attributable to owners of the parent**, which the statement of changes
in equity gives as the opening balance: `DERIVED: 4,57,570 − 15,197 = 4,42,373`, where 15,197 is the
non-controlling interest `[AR FY26, Statement of Changes in Equity, PDF p167-168/pr156-157]`. So
`DERIVED: 10,60,000 ÷ 4,42,373 = 2.396×`.

**Position 1 is the right basis, for one reason.** The guarantee existed at **31-Mar-2025**, and it is
the **31-Mar-2025 comparative** that was restated. A number must be sized against the balance sheet of
its own date. Between those dates equity grew `DERIVED: 6,79,550 ÷ 4,57,570 = 1.485`, **+48.5%**, and
total assets grew `DERIVED: 13,44,614 ÷ 10,34,165 = 1.300`, **+30.0%** — so measuring a FY2025 exposure
against FY2026 denominators understates it by roughly a third. Position 2 is not wrong as a sentence
("if that exposure were live today it would be 1.56× today's equity"); it is the wrong denominator year
for a **restatement** finding, and it must not be the headline.

**Position 3 is on the right year and is arguably the sharper denominator** — the shareholders' claim
rather than total equity — and it is retained as the variant to quote when the point is what the
guarantee is worth against the owners' capital specifically.

**Publication rule.** Size it as **2.32× total equity and 102.5% of total assets at 31-Mar-2025**, i.e.
an off-balance-sheet exposure that **exceeded the group's entire balance sheet**. Quote 2.396× of
owners' equity where the shareholder claim is the point. Do not quote 1.56× / 78.8% as the size of the
restated item. All three positions stay on the record.

**One corroboration neither position used.** The restated line is the only one in Note 37 that moved
down: every other line grew — income tax `11,170 → 13,496 (+20.8%)`, indirect tax
`2,210 → 5,930 (+168.3%)`, claims not acknowledged `10,504 → 13,567 (+29.2%)`, total non-guarantee
contingent liabilities `DERIVED: 32,993 ÷ 23,884 = +38.1%`. **The one line that vanished is 32× larger

## 3.3 The CDSL dividend — confirming W4_cash's settlement is correctly recorded

**Position A** — as logged in the register against W3_balancesheet: *"a normal consolidation mechanic —
the associate dividend eliminates against the equity-method carrying value. Not a missing cash flow."*
**Position B** — W3_group_rpt: the consolidated cash flow omits it in FY26 and FY25, **₹7,368 lakh over
two years, overstating consolidated CFO.**
**Settlement** — W4_cash, split decision.

### RULING: W4_cash's settlement is CORRECT and is correctly recorded. Confirmed on all three limbs, with one attribution corrected.

**Limb 1 — the omission is real, and is eight years, not two.**
`DERIVED: 1,058 + 1,209 + 941 + 1,881 + 3,135 + 2,508 + 3,449 + 3,919 = 18,100` lakh across
FY2019–FY2026, against W3_group_rpt's ₹7,368 lakh — `DERIVED: 18,100 ÷ 7,368 = 2.46×` larger. The proof
is structural rather than arithmetic: in **all ten years** the investing line "Dividend received" equals
the operating "Dividend income" adjustment **to the rupee** — `1,720 / 1,633 / 1,994 / 1,302 / 31 / 11 /
– / 26 / 1 / 1`. It is a mechanical mirror of P&L dividend income, and under the equity method an
associate's dividend never enters that income, so the line **cannot** carry it in any year, by
construction. FY2023 is the clean case: ₹3,135 lakh received `[AR FY2023, Note 16A, PDF p191/pr184]`,
a **dash** printed `[AR FY2023, Consolidated Cash Flow, PDF p163/pr156]`.

**Limb 2 — the "consolidation mechanic" defence fails on the standard.** CDSL is an equity-accounted
associate and therefore **outside** the consolidated reporting entity; elimination applies to intra-group
flows because both sides sit inside it. Ind AS 7.37 requires the investor to report cash flows between
itself and the investee; 7.31 requires dividends received to be shown separately; 7.21 forbids netting.
The carrying-value reduction is a balance-sheet mechanic with **no cash-flow counterpart**.

# 4. THE CRITICAL ADJUDICATION

**This gate's question is not W5's.** W5 asked *"can the numbers be relied on?"* and answered MODERATE.
W6's 8-point screen asked *"is there a governance kill?"* and found nothing reaching Critical. **This
gate asks: does anything here stop the process?** A Critical flag under §8 is one that makes the company
**uninvestable on trust grounds** — meaning no valuation could be honestly struck, at any price, because
either the evidence base cannot be relied on or an exposure that could impair the equity cannot be
bounded from any obtainable document.

Each candidate is re-run below against that test. **W5's verdict is not inherited.** Where the answer
agrees with W5, the reasoning is stated independently; where it would not, this file says so.

## 4.1 RF-008 — the ₹10,600 cr guarantee restated away. **HIGH, not Critical. Agree with W5, and it is the closest of the four.**

**The case for Critical is real and must be stated at full strength.** An off-balance-sheet exposure of
**2.32× total equity and 102.5% of total assets** (§3.2) stopped being quantified, and the prior year's
number was **actively changed**. That is not an omission — an omission leaves last year alone. Under Ind
AS 1.41 a reclassification of comparatives requires disclosure of the nature, amount and reason; under
Ind AS 8.49 an error correction requires the nature and the amount. **Neither is disclosed.** W6's
pattern analysis found this one of only two instances where the evidence is sufficient to distinguish
intent from process, precisely because somebody changed last year's number. And what replaced it is
prose: a footnote asserting collateral *"is adequate to meet potential obligations arising from Clearing
Member defaults"*, with no figure.

**Four things stop it reaching Critical at this gate, and the fourth is decisive.**

**(i) No recognised amount is affected, in any year.** Core SGF `1,24,683` and clearing-and-settlement
liabilities `3,55,423` both **grew**; the guarantee never appeared in the cash flow in FY2024, FY2025 or
FY2026 `[AR FY26, PDF p163/pr152]`, `[AR FY26, Note 16, PDF p206/pr195]`. This bounds the completeness of
the *risk disclosure*; it does not touch a single number a valuation would use.

**(ii) The exposure is not what its caption sounds like.** It is **inter-CCP collateral under SEBI's
interoperability framework** — each clearing corporation holds the other's collateral, a deliberate
mutual-hostage design. Its economic content is a collateralised operating arrangement in the normal
course, not a credit guarantee of a third party's debt. The reciprocal is disclosed and granular: **NSE
contributes ₹26,529 lakh — `26,529 ÷ 1,23,264 = 21.52%` — to ICCL's Core SGF**, by contributor and by
segment, tying exactly `[AR FY26, Consolidated Note 41, PDF p232/pr221]`. A company hiding an exposure to
its rival does not publish its rival's money to the rupee in the same set of accounts.

**(iii) The unknown has a named settling document, and it is obtainable.** §1a-iii's test for whether an
unknown is a finding or an unasked question:
`NOT-DISCLOSED -> ICCL's own s.136 / MCA-filed FY2026 financial statements, and the SEBI-mandated ICCL
risk disclosures on the BSE website.` Both exist and both are public. An exposure a reader can still
size from a named document one filing away is not an unbounded exposure.

**(iv) The base rate is zero, and it is zero for the exposure class, not just for this company.** No
Indian CCP has defaulted since 2019. No contingent claim has crystallised at BSE in nine years. SEBI
disposed of its interoperability show-cause against ICCL in **May 2025 without penalty**. In ten years
this company's realised losses came from a regulator, a counterparty and a capital-allocation decision —
never from an off-balance-sheet item. §1a-ii bars asserting that something matters without establishing
whether it has ever mattered, and here it has not.

**Disconfirming evidence against the alarming reading, sought and found.** ICCL did **not** contract over
the period — its total assets rose `5,67,395 ÷ 5,15,542 − 1 = +10.1%` and its own Core SGF contribution
rose `60,896 ÷ 56,751 − 1 = +7.3%`, which is the strongest argument that the guarantee did *not* simply
unwind, and it cuts **against** the company. Against that: a change in SEBI's collateral mechanism, or a
reclassification to a funded collateral line, is a change of *substance* rather than of candour, and
that reading cannot be excluded from the parent's disclosure alone.

**RULING: High.** The consequence is a **named margin-of-safety requirement**, not a terminal state: the
balance sheet is complete only on the assumption that the ₹10,60,000 lakh never was a Group obligation,
and that is an assumption the reader must make rather than verify. **W8 must apply a wider margin of
safety to the clearing subsidiary's contribution to value than to the exchange's.**

**And this gate records that a reasonable analyst could rule the other way on this one item.** The
thesis-breaker is precise and dated: **confirmation from ICCL's own FY2026 accounts that the guarantee
remains outstanding while the parent's note reads ₹23 lakh.** If that is found, this flag becomes
Critical and the gate must be re-run. Next check: ICCL's MCA filing, and the FY2027 consolidated
contingent-liability note.

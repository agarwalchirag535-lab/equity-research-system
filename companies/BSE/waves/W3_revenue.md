**Wave 3. Evidence, not conclusions about the investment case** (doctrine §7). Every transaction-charge
line is decomposed into **activity × realisation** as W1.7 §1 mandates; no rupee total is left standing
as its own explanation.

**Source of record.** The audited consolidated **Note 22, `[AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200]`**, not the
P&L face page. Its four components tie exactly: `3,79,499 + 51,903 + 17,142 + 34,851 = 4,83,395`
`[AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200]`. Prior year ties too:
`2,02,993 + 48,995 + 21,778 + 21,968 = 2,95,734` `[AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200]`.

**Sub-line count settled at source: 11.** Transaction charges (1, no split) · Listing services (3) ·
Treasury income (1) · Other operating income (6) = 11 named lines
`[AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200]`. The register's adjudication stands; W1_ledger's prose figure of
twelve is the count that is wrong.

**Restatement warning applied.** `records/restatements.md` governs every multi-year rate below. FY2017
revenue restated −27.8% and FY2024 −1.4%; **no growth rate in this file crosses FY2017 or FY2024**, and
the two FY2025-boundary restatements found here (receivables) are quantified in §5.3 rather than
smoothed.

**Unit throughout: ₹ lakh**, as printed `[AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200]`. Turnover figures are
₹ crore, as printed `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p58/pr47]`.

## 1. THE REALISATION SERIES — activity × realisation, per line

Method, applied identically everywhere: **realisation index = revenue index ÷ activity index.** It is
day-count-neutral because the trading-day count cancels in the ratio. Where an absolute per-unit rate is
quoted, the trading-day assumption is named and the item is `ESTIMATE`, not `DERIVED`.

### 1.1 Equity derivatives — the revenue base is premium

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transaction charges, standalone (₹ lakh) | 17,625 | 1,41,554 | 3,13,437 |
| Options premium ADT (₹ cr) | 2,133 | 8,978 | 19,523 |
| Realisation: ₹ lakh of charge per ₹1 cr of premium ADT | 8.263 | 15.767 | 16.055 |
| Realisation index, YoY | — | ×1.908 | **×1.0183** |

FY2026 and FY2025 charges `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p75/pr64]`. FY2024 charges `DERIVED`:
`1,41,554 − 1,23,929 = 17,625`, the FY2025 MD&A stating the increase was 1,23,929
`[AR FY25, MD&A, PDF p70/pr59]`. Premium ADT FY2026 and FY2025 `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p58/pr47]`;
FY2024 `[AR FY24, MD&A, PDF p53/pr47]`. Realisation `DERIVED`:
`17,625 / 2,133 = 8.263` · `1,41,554 / 8,978 = 15.767` · `3,13,437 / 19,523 = 16.055`.

**FY2026 decomposition.** Revenue index `3,13,437 / 1,41,554 = 2.214253`; activity index
`19,523 / 8,978 = 2.174538`; realisation index `2.214253 / 2.174538 = 1.018264`, **+1.83%**
`[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p75/pr64; AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p58/pr47]`. **Growth is 96.7% activity and 3.3%
price** (§3).

**FY2025's ×1.908 is not a price rise and must not be read as one.** BSE's Sensex derivative was
relaunched 15 May 2023 and Bankex 16 October 2023 `[AR FY24, MD&A, PDF p53/pr47]`, so FY2024 is a
part-year of charging against a full-year denominator. The FY2024 column is an `ESTIMATE` for shape
only; **the only comparable pair in the series is FY2025→FY2026.**

**Trading-day sensitivity, declared.** BSE's own disclosure implies `19,60,000 / 7,950 = 246.5` trading
days in FY2026 and `19,30,000 / 7,766 = 248.5` in FY2025 `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p58/pr47; AR FY25, MD&A,
PDF p52/pr41]`. Those totals are printed to three significant figures, so the day count carries roughly
±1%. The **index** above is immune (days cancel); an absolute per-unit rate is not, and none is used to
carry a conclusion.

## 2. THE THREE-BASE WEDGE (RF-012, RF-019, RF-030) — quantified as far as disclosure allows

> *"The SEBI turnover fee is based on the notional trading, the clearing expense is based on the number
> of contracts, and we earn revenue based on premium."* `[Q4FY26 Concall 13May2026, MD & CEO]`

| Base | What it drives | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 | FY26 growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Premium** ADT (₹ cr) | **Revenue** | 2,133 | 8,978 | 19,523 | **+117.45%** |
| **Notional** ADT (₹ cr) | **SEBI regulatory fee** | 34,60,747 (EDX total) | 1,10,66,674 | 1,87,15,375 | **+69.12%** |
| **Contracts** traded | **Clearing expense** | NOT-DISCLOSED | NOT-DISCLOSED | NOT-DISCLOSED | — |

Premium and notional `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p58/pr47]`; FY2024 `[AR FY24, MD&A, PDF p53/pr47]`.

**Internal tie-out that validates the split.** The FY2025 AR reports an EDX daily average of
1,10,66,881.25 `[AR FY25, MD&A, PDF p52/pr41]`; the FY2026 AR splits the same year into options notional
1,10,66,674 plus futures 207 `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p58/pr47]`. `1,10,66,674 + 207 = 1,10,66,881` —
exact. The two disclosures tie and the split is safe to use.

### 2.1 Bases one and two are proven, not assumed

**Revenue tracks premium.** `(3,13,437 / 19,523) ÷ (1,41,554 / 8,978) = 16.055 ÷ 15.767 = 1.0183` —
revenue per unit of premium moved **1.83%** in a year the line grew 121%
`[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p75/pr64 and PDF p58/pr47]`.

**The levy tracks notional.** SEBI regulatory fees `56,616 / 33,141 = 1.7083` against options notional
ADT `1,87,15,375 / 1,10,66,674 = 1.6912`; levy per unit of notional `1.7083 / 1.6912 = 1.0102`, **+1.02%**
`[AR FY26, Note 27, PDF p212/pr201; AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p58/pr47]`. **Two different quantities, each
tracked by its own line to within 2% in the same year.** That is what makes the wedge a measurement
fact rather than an assertion.

> **Basis conflict resolved.** W2_industry computed +67.9% on **standalone** SEBI fees of 55,625 / 33,123
> `[AR FY26, Standalone Note, PDF p287/pr276]`; W2_peers computed +70.83% on **consolidated** 56,616 /
> 33,141 `[AR FY26, Note 27, PDF p212/pr201]`. Both are right on their own basis. **This file uses the
> consolidated note**, because §0's revenue denominator is consolidated. Note 27 sits at PDF p212/pr201,
> not p213/pr202 as W2_peers cited — the page header reads printed 201.

### 2.2 The wedge itself

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Premium ÷ notional | 0.06163% | 0.081126% | **0.10432%** |
| Notional per ₹1 of premium | 1,622× | 1,233× | **959×** |
| YoY change in the ratio | — | +31.63% | **+28.58%** |

`DERIVED` from the table above: `2,133 / 34,60,747 = 0.06163%` · `8,978 / 1,10,66,674 = 0.081126%` ·
`19,523 / 1,87,15,375 = 0.10432%`; `0.10432 / 0.081126 = 1.2858`.

**Revenue base grew 48.33 percentage points faster than levy base — `117.45 − 69.12 = 48.33`.** That
gap is implied volatility and moneyness on the Sensex option, and nothing in it is a BSE decision.
Management says so: *"whether we can predict this trend, it is, of course, not predictable"*
`[Q4FY26 Concall 13May2026, MD & CEO]`.

**What it was worth in FY2026.** SEBI regulatory fees absorbed `33,123 / 1,41,554 = 23.40%` of

derivatives revenue: `3,13,437 × 0.0565 = 17,709` lakh — **₹177 crore of FY2026 profit that a reversion
to FY2025's premium/notional ratio would hand back to the regulator.**

### 2.3 The third base cannot be closed, and its two proxies disagree

Contract counts appear nowhere in the FY2024, FY2025 or FY2026 annual reports, and nowhere in the
concall corpus as an annual figure. Two proxies exist and they point in opposite directions:

| Proxy for contract-driven clearing cost | FY2026 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| ICCL charge to BSE, related party, per-contract in nature | 12,607 | 7,111 | **+77.29%** |
| Consolidated clearing and settlement expense (external) | 28,035 | 34,805 | **−19.45%** |

ICCL charge `[AR FY26, Standalone Note 35, PDF p298/pr287]`; consolidated expense
`[AR FY26, Consolidated P&L, PDF p164/pr153]`. **The two proxies for one base differ by 96.74
percentage points** (`77.29 − (−19.45)`).

The intra-group proxy also fails an internal test. If ICCL's per-contract rate were unchanged, +77.29%
would be contract growth — against notional +69.12%, implying average notional per contract **fell**
`1.6912 / 1.7729 = 0.9539`, **−4.61%**. SEBI raised index-derivative lot sizes from November 2024, which
moves notional per contract the other way. Either the intra-group rate changed, or the charge is not
purely per-contract. Both readings say the same thing: **the base management names as the cost driver
is not measurable from published information.**

`NOT-DISCLOSED -> BSE's own monthly business-growth filings to the exchanges carry segment contract
counts; neither the annual report nor the investor presentations reproduce them, and ICCL's standalone
financial statements (made available under s.136 Companies Act, not printed in the AR) would give the
clearing-fee-per-contract rate.`

**Consequence, recorded not interpreted.** With one of three bases unquantifiable, the residual between
them — which is the margin — cannot be attributed. This is RF-012's stated resolution condition
failing: *"if they cannot [be separately quantified], operating leverage for this company is not
measurable in the conventional sense."*

### 3.1 The three sentences this table produces

**(a) One line, one product, one base.** Item 1 alone is **88.60%** of the year's revenue increase, and
it is the growth of a market quantity BSE does not own. Adding item 2, equity derivatives account for
`1,71,883 / 1,87,661 = 91.59%` of the increase.

**(b) Across all three transaction-charge lines, realisation *subtracted* from revenue.** Items 2, 4 and
6 sum to `5,620 − 783 − 6,054 = −1,215` lakh. Management's headline is *"a transformational increase in
equity derivatives activity"* and a realisation gain of +1.83% `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p75/pr64]`; **the
₹56.2 cr realisation gain on derivatives was more than consumed by ₹60.5 cr lost on equity cash and
₹7.8 cr on StAR MF.** The counter-proportion is the finding: activity contributed
`1,73,176 / 1,87,661 = 92.28%` of the increase while realisation contributed **−0.65%**.

**(c) The two lines that went backwards are both rate, not volume.** Item 6 (equity cash, −6,054) sits
on turnover that rose; item 12 (treasury yield, −6,231) sits on float that rose. Together they cost

## 5. RF-026 RESOLVED — the receivable break is timing, and the live cycle has shortened

### 5.1 The headline that generated the flag

Gross trade receivables `65,109 / 34,802 = +87.09%` and unbilled `50,637 / 22,184 = +128.26%` against
revenue +63.46% `[AR FY26, Note 10 and 10.5, PDF p199/pr188]`. Doctrine §2c break 3 on its face.

### 5.2 The ageing table decomposes it, and the break is entirely in one bucket

| Consolidated gross receivables (₹ lakh) | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 | FY26 change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unbilled | 11,679 | 22,184 | 50,637 | **+128.26%** |
| Not due | 2,328 | 800 | 1,836 | +129.50% |
| Invoiced, less than 6 months | 7,126 | 2,244 | 3,092 | +37.79% |
| **Live book (unbilled + not due + <6m)** | **21,133** | **25,228** | **55,565** | **+120.25%** |
| Invoiced, over 6 months (stale) | 2,795 | 9,574 | 9,544 | **−0.31%** |
| **Gross** | **23,928** | **34,802** | **65,109** | +87.09% |
| Impairment allowance | 2,841 | 6,024 | 9,793 | +62.57% |
| **Net** | **21,087** | **28,778** | **55,316** | +92.22% |

FY2026 and FY2025 `[AR FY26, Note 10 and 10.5, PDF p199-200/pr188-189]`; FY2024 `[AR FY25, Note 10, PDF
p186/pr175]`. Ties: `50,637 + 1,836 + 3,092 + 520 + 2,922 + 4,228 + 1,874 = 65,109` ✓ ·
`22,184 + 800 + 2,244 + 3,113 + 5,315 + 329 + 817 = 34,802` ✓ ·
`11,679 + 2,328 + 7,126 + 630 + 553 + 833 + 779 = 23,928` ✓.

**The invoiced current book tracks revenue almost exactly.** Not-due plus under-6-months:
`(1,836 + 3,092) / (800 + 2,244) = 4,928 / 3,044 = +61.89%` against revenue **+63.46%**. A 1.57pp gap.

**The stale book did not grow at all.** `9,544 / 9,574 = −0.31%` in a year revenue rose 63.46%.

**So nothing in the invoiced book outran revenue.** The entire §2c signature sits in unbilled.

### 5.3 Is the unbilled build timing, a billing change, or recognition?

**Test 1 — normalise for the quarter that generates it.** Consolidated revenue from operations by
quarter: Q4FY26 1,56,351 · Q4FY25 84,664 · Q4FY24 48,837
`[Q4FY26 Results 07May2026, consolidated quarterly filing]`,
`[Q4FY25 Results 06May2025, consolidated quarterly filing]`,
`[Q4FY24 Results 08May2024, consolidated quarterly filing]`.

| Measured in days of Q4-run-rate revenue (`balance ÷ (Q4 revenue / 90)`) | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unbilled | 21.5 | 23.6 | **29.1** |
| Invoiced and current | 17.4 | 3.2 | 2.8 |
| **Live book, total** | **38.9** | **26.8** | **32.0** |

`DERIVED`: `11,679 ÷ (48,837 / 90) = 21.52` · `22,184 ÷ (84,664 / 90) = 23.58` ·
`50,637 ÷ (1,56,351 / 90) = 29.15`; `21,133 ÷ 542.6 = 38.9` · `25,228 ÷ 940.7 = 26.8` ·
`55,565 ÷ 1,737.2 = 32.0` `[AR FY26, Note 10.5, PDF p199/pr188; Q4FY26 Results 07May2026]`.

**The live receivable cycle is 32.0 days in FY2026 against 38.9 days in FY2024 — it has shortened by
6.9 days over two years**, and is 5.2 days longer than the FY2025 trough. The same shape holds on an
annual-average basis: `21,133 ÷ (1,39,003 / 365) = 55.5` days · `25,228 ÷ (2,95,734 / 365) = 31.1` ·
`55,565 ÷ (4,83,395 / 365) = 42.0` `[AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200]`. Two bases, same conclusion.

**Test 2 — size the part that is genuinely a lengthening.** Had unbilled grown with Q4 revenue,
`22,184 × (1,56,351 / 84,664) = 22,184 × 1.846713 = 40,968`. Actual 50,637.
`50,637 − 40,968 = 9,669` lakh is the lengthening; `40,968 − 22,184 = 18,784` is volume. **66.0% of the
+128.3% is volume; 34.0% is a 5.6-day extension (`29.15 − 23.58 = 5.57` days)**, worth `9,669 / 4,83,395 = 2.00%` of revenue and
`9,669 / 1,87,661 = 5.15%` of the year's revenue increase.

**Test 3 — recognition.** The Ind AS 115 policy is unchanged between FY2025 and FY2026: transaction
charges are recognised *"at a point in time as an when the transaction occurs… measured as per the
specified rate"* `[AR FY26, Note 2.3.10, PDF p176-177/pr165-166]`. There is no estimate of variable
consideration, no over-time measurement, and no judgement in the trigger — the trade either occurred on
the platform or it did not. The unbilled balance carries a **0% expected loss rate**, and the ageing
table's 15% blended rate is entirely borne by the invoiced 22.2%
`[AR FY26, Note 10.5, PDF p199/pr188]`.

**Test 4 — collection.** Invoiced-and-current days fell 17.4 → 3.2 → 2.8. Bad debts written off fell
`827 → 452 → 256` `[AR FY26, Note 10.4, PDF p199/pr188; AR FY25, Note 10.4, PDF p186/pr175]`. The
impairment charge for the year was 4,025, `4,025 / 4,83,395 = 0.83%` of revenue.

**VERDICT: TIMING.** Specifically, a lengthening of the interval between trade and invoice, not a change
in when revenue is recognised and not a deterioration in collection. **Directionally it began in FY2025,
not FY2026** — unbilled as a share of the gross book went `11,679 / 23,928 = 48.81%` → `22,184 / 34,802
= 63.74%` → `50,637 / 65,109 = 77.77%`, a two-year, 28.96pp migration of the book from invoiced to
unbilled. The FY2026 chain state for revenue is therefore **downgraded from BROKEN at L1/L6 to TRACED at
L6 with a Watch**, and the two things that keep it a Watch are in §5.4.

### 5.4 Two disconfirming facts that survive the verdict

**(a) BSE has already restated an unbilled balance, with no note.** FY2025 gross was first reported as
36,991 with unbilled 24,373 `[AR FY25, Note 10 and 10.5, PDF p186/pr175]`; the FY2026 comparative shows
34,802 with unbilled 22,184 `[AR FY26, Note 10, PDF p199/pr188]`. `36,991 − 34,802 = 2,189` and
`24,373 − 22,184 = 2,189` — **the entire reduction is unbilled, every other ageing bucket is identical
to the rupee**. On the as-first-reported base, unbilled grew `50,637 / 24,373 = +107.75%`, not +128.26%.
Neither AR explains it. Unbilled is a measured estimate that has already been revised once.

**(b) The same restatement moved 21,765 of receivables out of "secured".** FY2025 as first reported:
secured 21,775, unsecured 13,367, credit impaired 1,849 `[AR FY25, Note 10, PDF p186/pr175]`. The FY2026
comparative for the same date: secured **10**, unsecured 32,895, credit impaired 1,897
`[AR FY26, Note 10, PDF p199/pr188]`. `21,775 − 10 = 21,765` — **58.86% of the FY2025 gross book was
presented as collateral-backed and is now presented as unsecured** (`21,775 / 36,991 = 58.86%` against
`10 / 34,802 = 0.03%`). Net of the reclass the three lines still tie: `−21,765 + 19,528 + 48 = −2,189`.
No note in either annual report addresses it.

### FINDING: FY2026's revenue growth is 92.3% activity and minus 0.65% price — realisation subtracted from the top line in the year management called realisation a strength
S1 WHAT: Consolidated revenue from operations rose 1,87,661 lakh to 4,83,395 [AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200]. Decomposing every rupee of that increase into activity and realisation across the three disclosed transaction-charge lines gives activity +1,73,176 and realisation −1,215. The company grew on volume it does not control and lost money on price.
S2 WHY: BSE earns a regulated toll on activity it does not own. Realisation moved in three directions at once for three different regulator-authored reasons: derivatives realisation rose 1.83% because SEBI's expiry-day reallocation raised option premium relative to notional, equity-cash realisation fell 19.48% in the first full year of SEBI's true-to-label uniform per-unit charging effective 1 October 2024, and StAR MF realisation fell 2.67% alongside a SEBI circular of 8 August 2025 on transaction charges paid to mutual-fund distributors. None of the three is a BSE pricing decision.
S3 EVIDENCE: Equity derivatives: premium ADT 19,523 cr vs 8,978 cr = x2.174538 against revenue 3,13,437 vs 1,41,554 = x2.214253, realisation index 1.018264, worth +5,622 lakh [AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p58/pr47 and PDF p75/pr64]. StAR MF: orders 84.1 cr vs 66.3 cr = x1.268476 against revenue 28,490 vs 23,077 = x1.234571, realisation index 0.973270, worth −783 lakh [AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p57/pr46]. A share of growth stated for derivatives alone hides that the aggregate price effect is negative.
S4 CONNECTS: The Industry evidence puts the activity itself outside BSE's control — the two-exchange income pool grew only 6.46% while BSE's share went 14.44% to 21.58%, so roughly nine-tenths of the increment came out of NSE under identical rules and identical STT. DOMAINS: IncomeStatement, Notes, MgmtCommentary, Industry
DISCONFIRM: Sought and partly found. Derivatives realisation genuinely did rise 1.83%, so the claim of a favourable price effect is true on the line that dominates the mix; a reader looking only at 91.6% of the increase would reach management's conclusion honestly. Colocation and data services also grew well ahead of any volume measure, and data services is the one line where BSE explicitly says it raised a price [AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p63/pr52]. What cuts against those: colocation's prior-year rack count is not disclosed, so its realisation cannot be tested at all, and the two priced-up lines together are 23,872 / 4,83,395 = 4.94% of revenue.
S5 MEANS: BSE's FY2026 result is a market-share transfer measured in premium turnover, not an improvement in the terms on which the company sells anything; the one line where realisation genuinely rose is 10.74% of revenue (listing) and the one where it collapsed is priced by the same regulator that priced the one where it rose.
BASE-RATE: Realised, twice, at this company. Currency derivatives went from 9,733.67 cr ADT in FY2024 to 1,525.34 cr in FY2025 to nil in FY2026 on RBI's 5 January 2024 ETCD directions, and interest-rate and commodity derivatives are at nil or near-nil — a 40% four-year segment-extinction rate, none of it competitive. Separately, SEBI's 26 April 2024 letter re-based the regulatory fee to notional retrospective to FY2007 with 15% interest, costing 22,421 lakh across FY2024 and FY2025, 21.8% of FY2024 PAT attributable. Regulator-authored changes to this company's revenue and cost bases have produced realised losses in each of the last three years.
S6 IMPLICATION: The durability of the FY2026 earnings step depends on a market-share position and two exogenous ratios rather than on anything priced or contracted, so the base-case driver assumption should be premium turnover and share, not a revenue growth rate; the monitorable is the monthly premium-turnover share against NSE, next checked at the Q2FY27 disclosure, and the thesis-breaker is a quarter in which BSE's premium share falls while its own premium turnover also falls, which would separate share loss from pool contraction.

### FINDING: The equity-cash decline is a 19.5% repricing on rising volume, explained by management with a cause its own document refutes seventeen pages earlier
S1 WHAT: The MD&A states that equity-cash transaction charges "moderated by 18% to ₹25,021 Lakh, consistent with industry-wide reduction in average daily turnover value" [AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p75/pr64]. Seventeen pages earlier the same MD&A reports that BSE's own average daily equity turnover ROSE to 7,950 cr from 7,766 cr, "a Y-o-Y increase of about 2.36%" [AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p58/pr47]. The stated cause is contradicted by the company's own disclosure in the same document.
S2 WHY: The fall is a rate event, not a volume event. FY2026 is the first full year of SEBI's true-to-label uniform per-unit charging effective 1 October 2024, which removed the slab structure under which BSE's exclusive small-cap groups carried higher charges; BSE's own historic disclosure names those groups as the source of cash-segment charge increases in an earlier cycle. Overlaid on that, revised ESM from 28 July 2025 and the Additional Surveillance Measure from 10 November 2025 put a 1% price band and once-a-week trading on certain BSE-exclusive securities — the exact scrips that carry the special rate. Management's own vocabulary confirms the two-rate structure: a "special rate" for exclusive stocks and a "normal rate" for dual-listed stocks.
S3 EVIDENCE: Revenue index 25,021 / 30,356 = 0.824251 against activity index 7,950 / 7,766 = 1.023693; realisation index 0.824251 / 1.023693 = 0.805177, −19.48% [AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p58/pr47 and PDF p75/pr64]. Sized: at unchanged realisation the line would have been 30,356 x 1.023693 = 31,075 and the shortfall is 31,075 − 25,021 = 6,054 lakh, ₹60.54 crore — 6,054 / 4,83,395 = 1.25% of consolidated revenue and 6,054 / 3,29,914 = 1.83% of PBT [AR FY26, Consolidated P&L, PDF p164/pr153]. The asymmetry inside the same MD&A table: equity cash −18% against equity derivatives +121% in the same period, so the aggregate transaction-charge growth is not a statement about trading activity generally. And the repricing is not general either — derivatives realisation ROSE 1.83% and listing realisation rose 4.01% in the identical year, so a market-wide rate compression is excluded by two of BSE's own lines. Neither the −18% nor the +2.36% says any of this alone.
S4 CONNECTS: The Industry record shows the rate authority sits with SEBI, not BSE, and that the true-to-label reform was a regulatory event with a date, so the mechanism is available and management chose not to name it. The Notes confirm no accounting-policy change could account for it — transaction charges are recognised at a point in time at the specified rate, unchanged [AR FY26, Note 2.3.10, PDF p176-177/pr165-166]. This is the second verified instance in one document of the MD&A attributing a movement to a cause the company's own numbers refute; the first is the "structural efficiency" claim on clearing costs, where 81.2% of the consolidated fall is an intra-group charge that rose 77.3% and eliminated on consolidation.
DOMAINS: MgmtCommentary, IncomeStatement, Notes, Industry
DISCONFIRM: Sought. The industry claim is defensible at the market level even though it is false for BSE — market-wide cash ADT did soften in FY2026 while BSE's rose, because BSE gained share from roughly 6% to the 7-8% band. So the sentence is not fabricated; it is a market fact applied to a company for which it does not hold, in a paragraph explaining that company's revenue. Also weighing against a pure-repricing reading: the mix within BSE's own turnover between exclusive and dual-listed scrips is not disclosed, so part of the 19.48% could be mix rather than rate, and that split cannot be established. The direction is not in doubt; the composition of the 6,054 is.
S5 MEANS: On the only revenue line where BSE has genuine pricing differentiation, that differentiation was removed by regulation and the removal cost more than the entire realisation gain on the product management describes as transformational; and the annual report explains it with a cause that the same annual report disproves.
BASE-RATE: Realised, and at this company. The 6,054 lakh is not a projection — it is a completed FY2026 loss of revenue against an unchanged-rate counterfactual on volume that grew. In the system, the closest precedent is the same reform's effect at the peer level: NSE's cash-market transaction charges fell 7.93% in FY2026 [NSE AR FY26, Note 27, PDF p159-160/pr314-317], so the rate event is visible at both venues; what is BSE-specific is the 19.48% magnitude, which is 2.5 times the peer's decline and is the measure of how much of BSE's cash revenue depended on the exclusive-scrip slab.
S6 IMPLICATION: A management commentary that explains a rate event with a volume cause is a fact about the reliability of the narrative layer, so the equity-cash line should be modelled off BSE's disclosed turnover and a separately estimated rate rather than off management's growth language; the monitorable is the equity-cash realisation index recomputed each half-year from ADT and the charge line, and the thesis-breaker for the wider case is the same index turning negative on the derivatives line, which would show the true-to-label logic spreading to the 78.51% component.
SEVERITY: High

### FINDING: RF-026 resolves to timing — the entire receivable break is unbilled, and the live receivable cycle is shorter than it was two years ago
S1 WHAT: Gross receivables rose 87.09% and unbilled 128.26% against revenue of 63.46%, the classic L6-outruns-L1 signature [AR FY26, Note 10, PDF p199/pr188]. Reading the ageing table dissolves it: the invoiced current book grew 61.89%, the stale invoiced book fell 0.31%, and the live receivable cycle measured in days of fourth-quarter run-rate revenue is 32.0 days against 38.9 days in FY2024.
S2 WHY: The mechanism is the gap between the moment revenue is recognised and the moment an invoice is raised. Transaction charges are recognised the instant the trade occurs, at a specified rate, while billing is periodic; when the fourth quarter is 32.3% of the year's revenue and 84.67% larger than the prior fourth quarter, the balance sitting between recognition and invoice grows with it. What is not the mechanism: recognition, because the policy is unchanged and the trigger carries no estimate; and collection, because invoiced days fell.
S3 EVIDENCE: Gross 65,109 decomposes as unbilled 50,637 + not due 1,836 + under six months 3,092 + over six months 9,544 [AR FY26, Note 10.5, PDF p199/pr188]; prior year 22,184 + 800 + 2,244 + 9,574 = 34,802. The invoiced current book grew (1,836 + 3,092) / (800 + 2,244) = 4,928 / 3,044 = +61.89% against revenue +63.46% — a 1.57pp gap. Normalising unbilled on the quarter that creates it: 22,184 x (1,56,351 / 84,664) = 40,968 expected against 50,637 actual, so 18,784 of the 28,453 increase is volume and 9,669 is a genuine lengthening, 9,669 / 4,83,395 = 2.00% of revenue [Q4FY26 Results 07May2026, consolidated quarterly filing]. The counter-proportion that decides it: unbilled is 50,637 / 65,109 = 77.77% of the gross book while the invoiced-and-current portion is only 4,928 / 65,109 = 7.57%, and it is the small portion that tracks revenue exactly while the large one does not — the reverse of what a collection or recognition problem looks like, where the invoiced tail is what swells.
S4 CONNECTS: The Cash Flow confirms the conversion cost is modest — trade receivables absorbed 30,595 against 12,196, leaving 4,83,395 − 30,595 = 4,52,800 or 93.67% converted against 95.88%, a 2.21pp slip on a 63% revenue increase [AR FY26, Consolidated Cash Flow, PDF p165/pr154]. The Notes give the recognition test: point in time as the transaction occurs, no policy change [AR FY26, Note 2.3.10, PDF p176-177/pr165-166]. Business context closes it — BSE is substantially pre-funded, taking deposits, advance collections and collateral for trading, clearing and listing services, so the counterparty on the unbilled balance has already posted money with the exchange [AR FY26, Note 34, PDF p219/pr208].
DOMAINS: BalanceSheet, IncomeStatement, CashFlow, Notes, Business
DISCONFIRM: Two facts cut the other way and are not dismissed. First, unbilled carries a 0% expected loss rate at 77.77% of the gross book, so the entire ECL discipline in the ageing table applies to 22.23% of receivables; the blended 15% rate is arithmetically real and analytically empty. Second, on the invoiced book alone the provision is 9,793 / 14,472 = 67.67% of gross against 6,024 / 12,618 = 47.74% a year earlier and 2,841 / 12,249 = 23.19% two years earlier — BSE now provides against two-thirds of everything it has actually invoiced and not collected, and provisioning on the over-six-month bucket went 5,143 / 9,574 = 53.72% to 9,394 / 9,544 = 98.43%. That escalation is consistent with a stale tail being written down rather than with new deterioration, since the tail itself is flat, but it is the strongest available argument against the timing verdict and it is recorded as such.
S5 MEANS: The receivable signature that triggered the flag is a composition effect inside a shortening cycle, not an earnings-quality event; the balance-sheet residue of BSE's revenue is growing because the fourth quarter is, and the part of the book that has actually been invoiced is both smaller relative to revenue and collected faster than in either prior year.
BASE-RATE: No realised loss found on this pattern at this company. Bad debts written off fell in each of the last two years, 827 to 452 to 256, and the impairment charge for FY2026 of 4,025 is 0.83% of revenue [AR FY26, Note 10.4, PDF p199/pr188; AR FY25, Note 10.4, PDF p186/pr175]. The over-one-year buckets are 100% provided, so the loss that would have been realised has already been taken. The absence of a realised loss is what distinguishes this from a genuine L6 break and is recorded explicitly rather than assumed.
S6 IMPLICATION: The FY2026 revenue chain should be carried forward at L6 as TRACED with a Watch rather than as BROKEN, which removes one input from the earnings-quality gate; the monitorable is unbilled expressed in days of fourth-quarter run-rate revenue, which has gone 21.5 to 23.6 to 29.1 and should be re-measured at FY2027, and the thesis-breaker is that ratio passing roughly 40 days or the invoiced-and-current book growing materially slower than revenue, either of which would move the diagnosis from timing to something else.
SEVERITY: Medium

### FINDING: BSE restated its FY2025 receivable book twice with no note, moving 21,765 lakh out of "secured" and 2,189 lakh out of "unbilled"

### FINDING: The third base of the three-base wedge is undisclosed, and its two available proxies disagree by 96.7 percentage points
S1 WHAT: Management states the mechanism precisely — "the SEBI turnover fee is based on the notional trading, the clearing expense is based on the number of contracts, and we earn revenue based on premium" [Q4FY26 Concall 13May2026, MD & CEO]. Two of the three bases can be quantified from published data and each is confirmed to within 2%. The third, contract count, appears nowhere in the FY2024, FY2025 or FY2026 annual reports, and the two proxies for it move in opposite directions.
S2 WHY: Contract counts are a market-microstructure quantity that no accounting standard requires and that BSE has not disclosed since FY2019. The two proxies fail for identifiable reasons rather than by accident. The consolidated clearing-and-settlement expense is contaminated by consolidation: 81.2% of its 19.45% fall is the intra-group ICCL charge rising and eliminating, not external cost falling. The intra-group charge itself is a related-party price set inside the group, so its 77.29% rise measures whatever ICCL and BSE agreed, not necessarily contracts.
S3 EVIDENCE: Revenue tracked premium to within 1.83% — (3,13,437 / 19,523) / (1,41,554 / 8,978) = 16.055 / 15.767 = 1.0183 [AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p75/pr64 and PDF p58/pr47]. The levy tracked notional to within 1.02% — SEBI regulatory fees 56,616 / 33,141 = 1.7083 against notional ADT 1,87,15,375 / 1,10,66,674 = 1.6912, ratio 1.0102 [AR FY26, Note 27, PDF p212/pr201]. Against those two sub-2% confirmations, the third base offers the ICCL intra-group charge at 12,607 / 7,111 = +77.29% [AR FY26, Standalone Note 35, PDF p298/pr287] and the consolidated external clearing expense at 28,035 / 34,805 = −19.45% [AR FY26, Consolidated P&L, PDF p164/pr153] — a spread of 96.74 percentage points on one quantity. The proxy also fails its own internal test: if +77.29% were contract growth, average notional per contract fell 1.6912 / 1.7729 = 0.9539, −4.61%, in a year SEBI's November 2024 lot-size increase pushed it the other way. The asymmetry that matters: two bases are pinned to within 2% and one is unbounded, so the wedge between them — which is the margin — has a measured numerator and an unmeasurable denominator.
S4 CONNECTS: The Industry evidence supplies the consequence — the levy base was 959 times the revenue base in FY2026 and 1,233 times in FY2025, and that ratio moved 28.58% in one year on implied volatility and moneyness, so 5.65pp of derivatives revenue changed hands between BSE and SEBI on a quantity neither party set. The Notes show BSE pays 11.71% of revenue in turnover-linked regulatory fees against NSE's 4.80% — 8.54 rupees of revenue per rupee of levy against 20.85 — with the cause being the same wedge. Management commentary concedes the unforecastability directly: "the correlation between the clearing and settlement expenses versus the premium cannot be predicted" [Q4FY26 Concall 13May2026, MD & CEO]. The Governance angle is that the clearing corporation is wholly owned, so the one price inside the wedge that BSE does control is a related-party price it does not publish a rate for.
DOMAINS: IncomeStatement, Notes, MgmtCommentary, Industry
DISCONFIRM: Sought. A reader could argue the third base does not matter because clearing cost is small — 28,035 is 28,035 / 4,83,395 = 5.80% of revenue, down from 11.77%. That is true on the reported line and false on the mechanism: the standalone clearing charge is 37,191, 8.32% of standalone revenue, and it is the standalone that carries the contract exposure before consolidation nets it. Also weighing against: the peer set shows the wedge is not sectoral. IEX's levy base and revenue base are the same physical quantity, so it carries no wedge at all, and its realisation was flat at −0.21% in FY2026 [IEX AR FY26, Note 27, PDF p273/pr269] — which confirms the wedge is BSE-specific rather than a feature of exchanges generally, and therefore is a company-level exposure rather than an industry constant.
S5 MEANS: BSE's operating margin is the residual of three exogenous ratios of which one cannot be measured at all from published information, so the FY2026 margin expansion cannot be attributed between market structure and operating performance even in principle — and RF-012's stated resolution condition has now failed on the evidence rather than for want of looking.
BASE-RATE: Realised, and large. The 26 April 2024 SEBI letter re-based the regulatory fee to notional option value retrospective to FY2006-07 with 15% interest; 22,421 lakh was charged across FY2024 and FY2025, 16,977 of it in FY2024 alone, which is 16,977 / 77,839 = 21.81% of that year's PAT attributable — a completed cash cost arising purely from which base the levy runs on. The wedge has already produced a loss of that size once, for activity conducted years earlier under a different understanding of the same rule.
S6 IMPLICATION: Operating leverage at BSE is not measurable in the conventional sense and should not be modelled as a fixed-cost gearing effect, since the largest variable input runs on a base that is not published; the base case should carry the premium/notional ratio as an explicit exogenous assumption with a stated range rather than as an implicit constant, the monitorable is that ratio recomputed each quarter from the disclosed premium and notional ADTs, and the thesis-breaker is a quarter in which premium turnover grows while the ratio falls, which would show revenue and levy decoupling in the unfavourable direction.
SEVERITY: High

### FINDING: 3.55% of revenue is earned on money the group does not own, sits inside revenue from operations, and is the only revenue line with no recognition policy
S1 WHAT: "Treasury income from clearing and settlement funds" of 17,142 lakh is a named top-level component of audited revenue from operations, down 21.29% from 21,778 [AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200]. It is earned on member and settlement balances, it has no supporting note, and the Ind AS 115 policy paragraph that lists every other revenue stream does not name it.
S2 WHY: The group consolidates its clearing corporation, so member deposits, margins and settlement obligations sit on the group balance sheet — 3,55,423 lakh in Note 16 bucket (C) — and the return on those balances has to be presented somewhere. BSE presents it in the top line. The two adjacent pools of other people's money are treated differently and the differences are specified: income on earmarked amounts is credited to the liability rather than to income, and Core SGF investment income is credited directly to the fund and never enters the profit and loss. Three pools, three treatments, and the one that lands in revenue is the one with no policy paragraph.
S3 EVIDENCE: 17,142 against 21,778, −21.29%, on a float of 3,55,423 against 3,31,172, +7.32% [AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200; AR FY26, Note 16, PDF p206/pr195]. Implied yield on the average balance 17,142 / 3,43,298 = 4.99% against 21,778 / 3,44,669 = 6.32%, so the balance contributed +1,595 and the yield −6,231, total −4,636 — the entire decline is rate. The asymmetry: this line is 17,142 / 4,83,395 = 3.55% of revenue but −4,636 / 1,87,661 = −2.47% of the year's revenue change, and the float it rests on is 3,55,423 / 5,37,049 = 66.18% of the group's total current liabilities. A line that is one twenty-eighth of revenue sits on two-thirds of the liability side. The whole 17,142 arises outside the parent — the standalone Note 22 has no such caption and runs A+B+C only [AR FY26, Standalone Note 22, PDF p285/pr274] — so 100% of it is subsidiary income booked in group revenue.
S4 CONNECTS: The Balance Sheet supplies the counterparty composition: bucket (C) is deposit and margins from members 2,06,976 + others 87,714 + settlement obligation payable 42,357 + deposit from clearing banks 18,376 [AR FY26, Note 16, PDF p206/pr195]. The Cash Flow shows the group's own footnotes strip the parent's earmarked movements and the subsidiaries' Core SGF movements but say nothing about this pool, so the 3,55,423 moves through group cash unmarked [AR FY26, Consolidated Cash Flow, PDF p166/pr155]. Business logic confirms nobody is charged: the revenue-recognition framework asks when a performance obligation to a customer is satisfied, and here there is no customer and no obligation — the group earns because it holds. Management's own presentation adds a narration point: the line is printed in all three 2026 decks with an explicit −21% column, so it is disclosed, but it disappeared from the managing director's spoken remarks.
DOMAINS: IncomeStatement, BalanceSheet, CashFlow, Notes, Business
DISCONFIRM: Sought and found. Presenting float income inside revenue is defensible for a clearing corporation, because holding and investing member collateral is not incidental to the service — it is the service, and SEBI mandates both the collateral and the investment norms. The line is also fully labelled and quantified in the audited note with a prior-year comparative and printed in every investor deck, so this is not concealment. And the register's original framing that the line "sits inside revenue with no note" was itself wrong and was corrected: it is a named audited caption. What survives disconfirmation is narrower and firmer — the recognition policy paragraph names eleven revenue streams and omits this one, while specifying the treatment of the two neighbouring pools.
S5 MEANS: BSE's audited top line contains a block of return-on-float that no customer authorises, that arises entirely in subsidiaries, that fell 21% while its base grew 7%, and whose boundary against the two adjacent pools credited to liabilities is set by a policy the annual report does not print — which makes the split between revenue and liability on member money a management allocation rather than a disclosed rule.
BASE-RATE: No realised loss found. The line has never been restated, never been qualified by the auditor, and never appeared in a SEBI order. What has happened in the system is regulatory redirection of exactly this kind of pool: SEBI's segregation and monitoring framework for client collateral and its investment-norm circulars have repeatedly changed what clearing corporations may hold and where, and RBI's 13 February 2026 capital-market-exposure directions live from 1 July 2026 bear on the bank guarantees that sit alongside it. The pool has been re-regulated before; the income on it has not yet been.
S6 IMPLICATION: Any earnings-quality view should carry this line separately from customer revenue and should not extend an operating multiple to it, since it is a rate on a restricted balance rather than a claim on a customer; the monitorable is the implied yield recomputed each year from the Note 22 line over the Note 16 bucket (C) average, next checked at FY2027, and the thesis-breaker is a SEBI or RBI direction changing the permitted instruments for clearing-corporation collateral, which would move the whole 17,142 without any change in BSE's business.
SEVERITY: Medium

### FINDING: 12,551 lakh of audited transaction charges is earned by the clearing subsidiary from members and appears in none of the product numbers management publishes
S1 WHAT: Audited consolidated transaction charges are 3,79,499 [AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200]; standalone are 3,66,948 [AR FY26, Standalone Note 22, PDF p285/pr274]; the MD&A's three product sub-lines sum to the standalone figure exactly. The 12,551 lakh difference is real audited revenue that carries no product label anywhere in the annual report.
S2 WHY: The group's clearing corporation bills clearing members directly for clearing and settlement, separately from the transaction charge BSE bills trading members, and it also bills BSE itself for clearing-house services. The intra-group leg eliminates on consolidation; the member-facing leg survives and lands in the consolidated transaction-charge caption. The MD&A splits only the parent's revenue, so the surviving leg has no product line to sit in.
S3 EVIDENCE: The bridge closes exactly across all four Note 22 components: 12,551 + 10 + 17,142 + 6,741 = 36,444 = 4,83,395 − 4,46,951 [AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200 and Standalone Note 22, PDF p285/pr274]. Identification uses AOC-1 subsidiary turnover and the related-party note, and the method is validated on a line where it ties to the rupee: BSE Index Services turnover 3,876 less index management fees billed to the parent 215 gives 3,876 − 215 = 3,661, exactly the consolidated index-services line [AR FY26, AOC-1 Part A, PDF p312/pr301; AR FY26, Standalone Note 35, PDF p298/pr287]. Applied to the clearing corporation: turnover 42,464 less the 12,607 billed to BSE and eliminated leaves 29,857 external, less treasury income 17,142 and auction charges 60 leaves 12,655 against the observed 12,551 — a residual of 104 lakh, 0.83%. The proportion and its counter-proportion: the 12,551 is 3.31% of audited transaction charges and 0% of every product figure management publishes, while the MD&A simultaneously describes transaction charges as "82% of total revenue from operations", which is 3,66,948 / 4,46,951 = 82.10% standalone but 3,66,948 / 4,83,395 = 75.91% on the audited consolidated base the reader is looking at.
S4 CONNECTS: The Notes explain why no reconciliation exists — the group reports a single operating segment, "Facilitating Trading in Securities and other related ancillary services", with only a geographic split, so no subsidiary or business-line revenue is published [AR FY26, Note 39, PDF p228/pr217]. Governance supplies the related-party dimension: the clearing corporation is wholly owned and its charge to the parent rose 12,607 / 7,111 = +77.29% in one year, a related-party price whose rate basis is not disclosed. Management commentary compounds it by quoting the standalone percentage against a consolidated headline elsewhere in the same document. The peer control is decisive: NSE publishes a three-segment note with Clearing Services revenue of 1,762.37 cr shown separately from Trading Services [NSE AR FY26, Note 33(c), PDF p163], so the disclosure is available to an Indian exchange group with the same regulator, the same statute and the same auditor.
DOMAINS: IncomeStatement, Notes, MgmtCommentary, Governance
DISCONFIRM: Sought. The gap is small — 12,551 is 2.60% of consolidated revenue — and it is arithmetically visible to any reader who compares the two Note 22s, so it is not hidden, merely unlabelled. The single-segment presentation is also defensible under Ind AS 108, which keys off how the chief operating decision maker reviews the business, and BSE states the segment is what is presented to the managing director. What survives: a peer with an identical structure discloses it anyway, so the standard permits the disclosure and BSE has chosen not to make it; and the effect is that the growth of the group's clearing revenue, +56.77% (12,551 / 8,006), is invisible in every published product number.
S5 MEANS: The product picture BSE gives investors is a picture of the parent, not of the group whose results are reported, and the difference is a clearing revenue stream growing at 57% a year that no published line names — so any product mix or concentration ratio taken from the MD&A is measured on a base 7.54% smaller than the audited one.
BASE-RATE: No realised loss. The gap has never been misstated, and both sides of it are audited. The base rate that matters is a disclosure one: the register already records that BSE's largest single group penalty, the 5.05 cr clearing-corporation order, appeared in exactly one place in the FY2025 report while a penalty twenty times smaller received three slots. Subsidiary-level facts at this group have a demonstrated history of appearing in fewer places than parent-level facts of the same size, and this is the revenue instance of that pattern.
S6 IMPLICATION: Concentration and mix ratios should be recomputed on the audited consolidated base rather than taken from the MD&A, which moves equity derivatives from 70.1% to 3,13,437 / 4,83,395 = 64.84% of revenue and transaction charges from 82.10% to 75.91%; the monitorable is the consolidated-minus-standalone transaction-charge gap, recomputed from the two Note 22s each year, and the thesis-breaker is that gap growing faster than group revenue for two consecutive years, which would mean the clearing corporation rather than the exchange is driving the group.
SEVERITY: Medium

### FINDING: Listing services is the only line where realisation rose, and the audited note gives the only external handle on any BSE fee base
S1 WHAT: Listing services grew 5.94% to 51,903 [AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200]. Within it, the recurring annual listing fee grew 8.04% to 242 cr on a listed-company count that grew 3.87%, giving a realisation index of 1.040104 — a genuine 4.01% rise in fee per listed company [AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p57/pr46 and PDF p63/pr52]. It is the only revenue line at BSE whose realisation rose in FY2026.
S2 WHY: The annual listing fee is a slab charged per issuer and reset by the exchange within SEBI's framework, not a per-transaction rate set by a true-to-label rule. It is the one BSE revenue stream where the customer is a captive counterparty in a contractual relationship rather than an order flow that can move venue in a millisecond, and it is the one that is pre-collected and straight-lined rather than recognised at a point in time.
S3 EVIDENCE: Listed companies 5,663 against 5,452 = +3.87% and against 5,198 two years earlier [AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p57/pr46; AR FY25, MD&A, PDF p52/pr41]; annual listing fees 242 cr against 224 cr = +8.04%; realisation index 1.080357 / 1.038701 = 1.040104 [AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p63/pr52]. Independent audited corroboration exists and is the only one in the document: SEBI has required BSE since 1996-97 to set aside 20% of annual listing fees received into the Investors' Services Fund, and the ISF roll-forward is audited — contribution received 5,386 against 5,068, so fees received back out to 5,386 / 0.20 = 26,930 against 5,068 / 0.20 = 25,340, +6.27% [AR FY26, Note 19(a), PDF p209/pr198]. The asymmetry inside the caption: the recurring annual fee is 24,200 of the 38,861 standalone listing-fee line, 62.27%, and it grew 8.04%; the remaining 14,661 of one-time and initial fees grew 14,661 / 12,928 = +13.41% on 146 SME listings against 78, +87.18% — so the fast-growing half is the transactional half and the durable half is the slower one. The share of listing revenue that is contractual and recurring is falling even as the line grows.
S4 CONNECTS: Governance supplies the structural fact the overlay mandates be stated: these fees are paid by the entities BSE regulates as an exchange, and under the same SEBI directive 20% of them funds the Investors' Services Fund that supervises those entities — listed companies fund their own supervision [AR FY26, Note 19(a), PDF p209/pr198]. The Balance Sheet confirms the pre-collection: income received in advance and advance from customers total 9,129 against 8,337, growing 9.50% against listing revenue's 5.94%, the direction a straight-lined pre-collected fee should move on a growing book [AR FY26, Note 19, PDF p208/pr197]. Business context sets the ceiling — 5,663 listed companies is the largest count of any exchange in the world, and 1,484 companies were delisted between 1 April 2016 and 31 March 2026, so count growth is a net figure over substantial attrition [AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p66/pr55].
DOMAINS: IncomeStatement, Notes, BalanceSheet, Governance, Business
DISCONFIRM: Sought and found. The line has an unexplained internal inconsistency that cuts against the precision claimed: the MD&A reports annual listing fees of 242 cr on one page and an audited listing-fee line of 38,871 lakh, 388.71 cr, on another, a 146.71 cr difference the document never labels [AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p63/pr52; AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200]. The ISF back-out also does not tie to the rupee — 26,930 against a recognised 242 cr leaves 2,730 lakh, and 25,340 against 224 cr leaves 2,940 — so the two measures are on different bases, and the stability of the gap is what makes the corroboration usable rather than an exact reconciliation. And listing services is only 10.74% of revenue, so a 4.01% realisation gain here is worth roughly 2,025 lakh against the 6,054 lost on equity cash.
S5 MEANS: The one BSE revenue line with genuine pricing power is the one whose customers are the entities it regulates and cannot easily leave, which locates the company's residual pricing power in the regulatory relationship rather than in the trading franchise — and even there, the growth is tilting toward the one-time issuance component rather than the recurring annuity.
BASE-RATE: No realised loss on this line at this company; the annual listing fee has risen in each disclosed year and the count has risen in each of the last three. The relevant systemic base rate is that listing fee slabs are subject to SEBI approval and have been reset before, and that the count is exposed to delisting cycles — 1,484 delistings over ten years against a net count rise of 465 over the last two, so gross attrition runs many times the net addition and a single delisting wave would show up in the annuity directly.
S6 IMPLICATION: Listing services deserves a different durability treatment from transaction charges in any driver framework, being contractual, pre-collected and priced by BSE rather than per-transaction and priced by SEBI, which argues for splitting the revenue forecast rather than growing a single top line; the monitorable is annual listing fees per listed company, recomputable each year from the MD&A count and fee figures and cross-checkable against the audited ISF contribution, and the thesis-breaker is that ratio falling while the count rises, which would mean slab migration toward smaller issuers is offsetting the fee.
SEVERITY: Watch

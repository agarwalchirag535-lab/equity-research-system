## 0. THE BOTTOM LINE, BEFORE THE EVIDENCE

The standing rule governs this file: *"good governance" is not "good investment"*. What follows
establishes only **how much trust the numbers deserve** and **how management behaves with minority
capital**. It reaches no view on the shares.

**On trust: moderate, and the reason is unusual.** BSE's numbers are clean for a structural reason that
has nothing to do with virtue — there is no promoter, no director holds a share
`[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p80/pr69]`, there has never been an ESOP, and under the SECC framework the
MD & CEO is barred from stock options entirely `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p90/pr79]`. Every ordinary
incentive to manage earnings has been removed by regulation. `W5_earnings_quality.md` confirms the
result from the other end: five separate defects run **against** the company's interest, and in ten
years **zero rupees** of realised loss at BSE is attributable to an accounting-quality channel.

**On behaviour with minority capital: the money is not the problem; the perimeter is.** The same
regulatory design that removes the incentive to misstate also removes the incentive to *inform*. The
board and the CEO are paid in cash and are structurally indifferent to the share price. What moves at
BSE is not the number — it is the boundary of what gets published. Five separate instances of that
boundary narrowing are now on the record, and §4 adjudicates them.

**The single dated governance event on this file** is not any of the five. It is that
**S.R. Batliboi & Co. LLP's term ends at the 22nd AGM in 2027** — FY2018 to FY2027 is exactly the ten
consecutive years s.139(2) permits — and **no successor process is disclosed anywhere**
`[AR FY26, Board's Report, PDF p28/pr17]`. The firm that has issued nine consecutive unmodified
opinions has to go, in the year after it narrowed its own investment-valuation Key Audit Matter from
53.69% of standalone total assets to 11.36% `[AR FY25, Auditor's Report, PDF p220/pr209]`;
`[AR FY26, Auditor's Report, PDF p241/pr230]`.

## 1. THE TWELVE POINTS

### Point 1 — BOARD

**Verdict: the strongest independence structure in the framework, attached to the weakest challenge
capacity, and the weakness is designed in rather than chosen.**

Six of eight directors are Public Interest Directors at 31-Mar-2026 — **75.0% independent** — the
Chairperson must be a PID, and Chair/CEO separation has been continuous across every year read from
FY2017 to FY2026 and is mandated, not elected `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p78/pr67]`;
`[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p95/pr84]`. The Audit Committee is 3 PIDs + 1 NID = **75% independent**, met
six times in FY2026 with attendance 6/6, 6/6, 6/6 and the NID at 5/6, and is chaired by the one PID
whose SECC skills matrix carries both *Finance and Accountancy* and *Risk Management*
`[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p83/pr72]`; `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p81/pr70]`. PIDs met separately **four**
times against a SECC minimum of two, at full attendance `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p86/pr75]`. No
director holds any equity share and none are inter-se related `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p80/pr69]`.

Against that structure, four functional weaknesses, each sized:

1. **Tenure.** Mean PID tenure at 31-Mar-2026 is `(3.87 + 3.87 + 3.12 + 1.19 + 1.00 + 0.21) ÷ 6 =
   13.26 ÷ 6 = 2.21 years` against the SECC 3+3 maximum of six — **36.8%** of the permitted maximum
   `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p99/pr88]`. The Chairperson and Justice (Retd.) Vazifdar were appointed on
   the same day, 19-May-2022, and therefore exhaust their terms **on the same day in May 2028**.
2. **Chair churn.** Seven chairpersons in nine years; two served under 300 days (Dhirendra Swarup 94
   days, Pramod Agrawal 296 days); two chair-less interregnums of 11 and 13 days; and a **163-day**
   MD & CEO vacancy inside GAP-001 `[AR FY24, Consolidated Note, PDF p296/pr290]`.
3. **Expertise fit.** Capital markets — the business the company is in — is claimed by **three of eight**
   directors plus the MD `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p81/pr70]`. The board is heavy on administration and
   accountancy, thin on markets. Mitigant: a mandatory seven-day annual director training programme
   under SECC `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p80/pr69]`.
4. **The one committee that does not follow the independence logic.** The Stakeholders' Relationship
   Committee — the body that handles **minority-shareholder grievances** — is chaired by the
   Non-Independent Director, the nominee of Life Insurance Corporation, which is the largest single
   holder at **5.57%** `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p94/pr83]`. Permitted under LODR, which requires only a
   non-executive chair. It is nonetheless the one committee where the largest holder's representative
   presides over the smallest holders' complaints.

**And the structural gap that outranks all four.** The SEBI-mandated Executive Directors for Vertical 1
(Critical Operations), Vertical 2 (Regulatory, Compliance, Risk Management and Investor Grievances) and
Vertical 3 (Business) were **still unappointed** at the Corporate Governance Report date of 7-May-2026 —
the report says only that the company *"has initiated the process"* — leaving the Chief Regulatory
Officer reporting to the Managing Director & CEO `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p79/pr68]`;
`[AR FY26, Board's Report, PDF p32/pr21]`. Every SEBI penalty the group has ever paid came out of that
function (point 11). The same annual report's cover matter, printed 23-Jul-2026, lists Shri Gopalan S
Raghavan as *"Executive Director – Vertical 2"* with no appointment date and no other mention
`[AR FY26, Board of Directors, PDF p4 — cover matter, no printed folio]`. **Both statements stand** (§7.2, contradiction 3).

**Does the board challenge management?** No instance of a visible overruling appears in any of the nine
annual reports read. The one transaction that tested the board on minority capital is disconfirming and
must be read as such: the FY2022 retrospective *"re-compensation"* of **₹99,18,750** to the outgoing
MD & CEO was published with the full arithmetic (eligibility ₹1,19,02,500 less increment allowed
₹19,83,750 = ₹99,18,750), applied as a firm-wide policy, made conditional on SEBI's approval, deferred
50% of the variable component for three years under SECC, and **put to a shareholder vote**
`[AR FY22, AGM Notice, PDF p23/pr14]`. Sized: `99.19 ÷ 19,512 = 0.51%` of that year's standalone PAT.
That is the machinery working.

### Point 2 — PROMOTER

**Verdict: NO SUBJECT. Not clean — inapplicable.** See §2, bucket 2, for what replaces it and why the
substitute must not be scored on the same scale.

The company states it in both sets of accounts: *"There are no promoters identified for the Company"*
`[AR FY26, Standalone Note 49, PDF p310/pr299]`; `[AR FY26, Consolidated Note 52, PDF p240/pr229]`.
Corroborated three ways — all **22** NSE quarterly shareholding records from 30-Sep-2021 to 30-Jun-2026
carry `promoter and promoter group = 0` and `employee trusts = 0` without exception
`[NSE SHP, shareholding.json]`; the FY2019 statutory annual-return promoter tables read *"NOT
APPLICABLE"* `[AR FY19, MGT-9, PDF p60/pr51]`; and no director holds a share
`[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p80/pr69]`. This is a legal consequence of the corporatisation-and-
demutualisation scheme of 20-May-2005, whose residue is still live in the property notes — land held by
*"The Trustees of the Bombay Stock Exchange"*, expressly *"Not as promoter, director, relative or
employee of the Company"* `[AR FY26, Consolidated Note, PDF p308/pr297]`.

Concentration is genuinely low and nobody is near a SECC cap in any year read: largest holder LIC
**5.57%**, tenth-largest **1.17%** at 31-Mar-2026 `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p94/pr83]`. The register has
institutionalised — mutual funds + insurance + FPIs went `0.46 + 6.14 + 12.77 = 19.37%` (FY22) to
`8.57 + 8.89 + 13.01 = 30.47%` (FY24) to `12.52 + 11.88 + 19.42 = 43.82%` (FY26)
`[AR FY22, CG Report, PDF p123/pr114]`; `[AR FY24, CG Report, PDF p94/pr88]`;
`[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p93/pr82]`. For a company with no promoter, that is the substitute discipline
arriving.

**One ownership fact the annual report does not explain, and it is not small.** RF-035: **20.57%** of
the company — 8,35,59,827 shares held by Trading Members and Associates of Trading Members — moved into
the "public" category between 30-Jun-2025 and 30-Sep-2025 while the total share count moved only
`40,72,99,077 − 40,61,29,077 = 11,70,000`, i.e. **0.29%**. The shares did not trade; the category
changed `[NSE SHP, shareholding.json]`. Apparent free float rose `40,72,99,077 ÷ 32,25,69,250 = 1.263`,
**26.3%**, with nothing bought or sold. The annual reports never state the SECC numeric caps at all —
the FY2026 AGM notice only tells shareholders to *"adhere to the prescribed shareholding limits"*
without naming them `[AR FY26, AGM Notice, PDF p14/pr3]`. `NOT-DISCLOSED -> SEBI (SECC) Regulations
2018, Regulations 19–21, and BSE's quarterly shareholding-pattern XBRL filings.` **After the
reclassification there is no disclosed figure anywhere for how much of BSE is owned by the brokers BSE
regulates.**

### Point 4 — REMUNERATION

**Verdict: passes on every ceiling with room to spare; the only defect is a disclosure whose two halves
point in opposite directions. This is the least contested of the twelve points.**

| FY | MD & CEO pay | Ratio to median | Permanent employees | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2024 | ₹5,40,78,629 | 48.39× | 518 | `[AR FY24, Annexure A, PDF p33/pr27]` |
| FY2025 | +35.93% YoY (implied ₹7,35,10,000) | 84.46× | 771 | `[AR FY25, Annexure A, PDF p36/pr25]` |
| FY2026 | ₹8,64,94,321 (+17%) | 79.16× | 850 | `[AR FY26, Annexure A, PDF p37/pr26]` |

- **Against the statutory ceiling.** `864.94 ÷ 2,33,416 = 0.371%` of standalone PAT, against the s.197
  single-managerial-person cap of 5% of net profits — **7.4% of the permitted cap**
  `[AR FY26, Board's Report, PDF p23/pr12]`.
- **Against profit.** MD pay rose `8,64,94,321 ÷ 5,40,78,629 = +60.0%` over two years while standalone
  PAT rose `2,33,416 ÷ 75,339 = +209.8%` over the same two years — pay grew at 28.6% of the rate of
  profit `[AR FY24, Board's Report, PDF p18/pr12]`; `[AR FY26, Board's Report, PDF p23/pr12]`.
- **Variable pay and equity.** Half the MD's variable pay is deferred three years under SECC
  `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p90/pr79]`. There has **never** been an ESOP in any year read; the SEBI
  (Share Based Employee Benefits and Sweat Equity) Regulations are recorded as *not applicable* in every
  secretarial audit `[AR FY26, Secretarial Audit, PDF p42/pr31]`; and the MD, as KMP of an exchange, is
  **barred** from stock options `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p90/pr79]`.
- **Directors.** PIDs receive **sitting fees only** — 0.66× to 3.41× median in FY2026 — and *"None of
  the Directors were in receipt of any Commission from the Company or any remuneration from its
  subsidiaries"* `[AR FY26, Annexure A, PDF p37/pr26]`. Total directors' sitting fees
  `37 + 33 + 30 + 12 + 21 + 34 + 24 + 7 = 198` lakh, **+23.0%**
  `[AR FY26, Standalone Note 35, PDF p300/pr289]`.
- **Pay-for-performance linkage: absent by design, in both directions.** With no equity, no options and
  no commission, there is no alignment mechanism at all. That removes every dilution risk and every
  earnings-management-for-options incentive — and it removes the upside alignment too. For a minority
  holder this cuts both ways and must be scored both ways.
- **The one defect.** The two Rule 5(1) figures sitting adjacent in the same annexure move in opposite
  directions and neither states its population. Implied median pay, derived from the annexure's own two
  numbers: `540.79 ÷ 48.39 = 11.18` (FY24), `735.10 ÷ 84.46 = 8.70` (FY25), `864.94 ÷ 79.16 = 10.93`
  (FY26) — a two-year move of `(10.93 − 11.18) ÷ 11.18 = −2.2%` against the same annexure's stated
  median increases of +47%, +22% and +26%, which compound to `1.47 × 1.22 × 1.26 = 2.26×`, i.e.
  **+126%**. `NOT-DISCLOSED -> AR FY2025 Annexure A printed folio 36 and AR FY2026 Annexure A printed
  folio 37, read as rendered pages rather than extracted text; neither annexure states the population
  each Rule 5(1) figure is computed on.` Severity low — 0.371% of PAT is not a governance exposure —
  but a disclosure whose two halves contradict each other is a fact about the filing.

**Peer comparison for this point could not be run.** `W2_peers.md` benchmarks disclosure, revenue
structure, clearing capital, float and board structure across NSE, MCX, CDSL and IEX, and does not carry
a remuneration comparison. → **the peers' own Annexure A / Rule 5(1) disclosures in their FY2026 annual
reports** would settle pay-versus-peers, and were not read by this run.

### Point 7 — CAPITAL ALLOCATION *(hand-off consumed: `W4_capalloc.md`. Not re-derived.)*

> **Over the decade ₹1 became more than ₹1 — and almost none of it was a decision.**
> (`W4_capalloc.md` §0 and RF-064; underlying tags `[AR FY26, Consolidated Cash Flow, PDF p165/pr154]`
> and `[AR FY24, Board's Report, PDF p20/pr14]`.)

- **Neither CDSL step was discretionary.** Both sell-downs were SEBI-directed and the 15% residual is a
  regulatory cap. The buyback route is closed, not paused: **2.49% take-up** in the FY2024 tender.
- **On the capital management actually controls today, ₹1 → ₹1.04 a year** — 4.42% after tax on the
  treasury book and 4.21% on subsidiaries at cost, inside a business earning **195.0%**.
- **Retention drag: 1,129 basis points** of return on closing equity for one year's retention;
  **10,707 basis points** on the accumulated pile. `108.13%` of FY2026's increase in shareholders'
  equity went into the securities and deposit book.
- **Best against worst.** BSE Index Services: ₹3,001 lakh, **89.97% of the purchase price returned in
  dividends within 24 months**. India INX: **₹20,919 lakh, ten consecutive loss years, nil ever
  returned**, carried at **2.59×** its net-asset share. `20,919 ÷ 3,001 = 6.97×` more capital went into
  the failure than into the success.
- **The governance fact inside the capital-allocation fact:** *"there is no dividend policy, no target
  payout ratio and no capital-allocation framework in any of the ten annual reports"*. The Dividend
  Distribution Policy that **is** published still carries the FY2017-18 text describing itself as a
  *"progressive Policy"* while the payout fell from **95.1%** (FY22) to **17.65%** (FY26)
  `[AR FY26, Annexure E, PDF p50/pr39]`; `[AR FY26, Board's Report, PDF p24/pr13]`. The policy's test is

### 2.1 The Promoter bucket has no subject — what replaces it, and why it is not the same animal

**The subject is absent, not clean.** Recording pledge, promoter-entity RPTs, promoter salary versus
profit, promoter shareholding trend, promoter-group cross-holdings and inter-corporate lending to
promoter entities as *passes* would be a false clean; recording six numbered screen points as
inapplicable would be a false gap (`W1.7_mii_overlay.md` §4.1 CORRECTION). Both errors are available and
both have been avoided.

**What replaces controlling-shareholder risk is regulator-administered risk. It must not be scored on
the same scale, and here is why — three axes, each with evidence:**

1. **Direction.** A promoter's interest is opposed to the minority's on specific transactions and
   *aligned* on the share price. SEBI's interest is opposed to the minority's on **the price of the
   product** and is **indifferent to the share price entirely**. The regulator has re-priced BSE
   retrospectively (RF-019) and the RBI extinguished an entire BSE segment by direction — currency
   derivative ADT **₹9,734 crore → nil** after the 5-Jan-2024 ETCD directions (`W2_industry.md`). A
   promoter test hunts for *extraction*; the correct test here hunts for a *rule change*, and the two
   leave completely different evidence trails.
2. **Tail shape.** Promoter risk is diversifiable across a portfolio and typically costs a fraction of
   equity value. Regulator risk here is concentrated on **one counterparty** who sets the rate, approves
   every director, licenses the venue and audits the licence. Against that counterparty BSE's appellate
   record over five years is: one order stayed for four years and still pending, one set aside, one
   paid, one disposed without penalty. Nothing in the record shows the company reversing a regulatory
   outcome that had already cost it money.
3. **Observability.** Promoter behaviour leaves a trail in the RPT note, the pledge disclosure and the
   shareholding pattern — all quarterly, all mandatory. Regulator risk leaves **no trail at all** until
   the circular is issued, and BSE's own FY2026 BRSR names *"regulatory scrutiny, penalties, or legal
   consequences"* as a material risk while quantifying none of it `[AR FY26, BRSR, PDF p113/pr102]`.

**What the absent promoter genuinely buys the minority holder, and it is real:** no pledge to unwind, no
promoter-entity RPT chain, no dilution in nine years of any kind, and — under SECC — no equity-linked
pay for the MD at all. **What it costs:** the alignment goes with it. The board and the CEO are
structurally indifferent to the share price, and the only external check that exists is the regulator
itself.

### 4.1 The verdict: PATTERN — established on direction, not on count

**Five instances is not many, and the case does not rest on the count.** It rests on three tests, run in
order.

**Test 1 — Direction. A capacity problem is unbiased; this is not.** Of the six statutory disclosures
that stopped, only **two** correspond to an item that genuinely ceased (Specified Bank Notes, buyback)
and one is genuinely both (the Liquidity Enhancement Scheme — BSE's own ended in FY2023 while India
INX's continues at ₹556 lakh inside Note 28). **Three of six are live, and in two cases growing**
(`W5_earnings_quality.md` §7). In **every** instance above where the underlying item's trajectory can be
established, the item was **growing or adverse at the moment of the deletion**. That is not what random
error looks like.

**Test 2 — The controlled comparison inside the same company, and this is the decisive one.** BSE's
*accounting* defects and BSE's *disclosure* deletions are produced by the same organisation in the same
year, and they point in **opposite directions**:

- On accounting, the direction runs **against** the company. `W5_earnings_quality.md` establishes five
  defects running against BSE's interest; capitalisation and depreciation conservative in every case;
  and the FY2025 cash-flow basis change moving reported CFO *toward* own-account while **cutting** the
  FY2024 comparative by ₹19,857 lakh — the opposite of what earnings management produces.
- On disclosure, the direction runs **with** the company, in every establishable case above.

**One organisation cannot be simultaneously unbiased in its arithmetic and unbiased in its disclosure
perimeter while producing that asymmetry by chance.** The arithmetic is prepared without direction; the
perimeter moves with the news. Those are two different functions and only one of them shows direction.

**Test 3 — Cross-channel propagation.** RF-002, RF-008, RF-009, RF-050 and RF-032 live in the **statutory
annual report**, produced by the secretarial and financial-reporting function, on an annual calendar,
under statutory assurance. RF-018 lives in the **investor-relations channel** — prepared remarks and
decks — produced by a different team, on a quarterly calendar, with no assurance at all. A capacity
constraint in the secretarial function **does not propagate into the MD's prepared statement**. The same
shape appearing in both channels is the strongest available evidence that it is not a staffing problem.
The seven offline routings of §1 point 10 are the same valve, observable live.

**The asymmetry test, run inside one disclosure (§1a-i).** Note 22 splits *Other operating income* of
₹34,851 lakh — **7.21%** of revenue — into six named lines, and gives *Auction charges* of ₹60 lakh
(`60 ÷ 4,83,395 = 0.012%` of revenue) its own audited caption, while leaving ₹3,79,499 lakh — **78.51%**
of revenue — as a single number `[AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200]`. Granularity was added exactly
where it was immaterial and withheld exactly where it decides the case. The unsplit line is
`3,79,499 ÷ (34,851 ÷ 6) = 65×` the average sub-line the company was willing to name. Four of four peers
publish the Ind AS 115 timing split BSE does not `[NSE AR FY26, Note 27, PDF p159-160/pr314-317]`.

### 4.2 The base rate, which doctrine §1a-ii requires before significance is asserted

> **Has disclosure narrowing at BSE ever cost money? Once, cleanly. Twice, arguably. And the gap itself
> has never cost a rupee.**

**The one clean instance — the SEBI differential regulatory fee, and the sequence matters.** FY2024: a
discrete note, with a ₹16,977 lakh provision made *"based on the prudence"* while the company stated it
intended to contest. FY2025: the contest abandoned after a single SEBI letter of 14-Aug-2024. FY2026:
**the note is gone** and the annual cost is ₹56,616 lakh, `56,616 ÷ 33,141 = +70.8%` in one year and
`56,616 ÷ 17,212 = 3.29×` the restated FY2024 base in two `[AR FY26, Note 27, PDF p212/pr201]` against
`[AR FY25, Note 46, PDF p217/pr206]`. Disclosure stopped at the point the item became permanent and
largest. **One for one.**

**Two weaker candidates, and the ordering disqualifies them from carrying the conclusion.** Bankex fell
95% — but the collapse was **disclosed first** and the silence followed, so the disclosure did not
conceal a loss, it stopped allowing verification of a stated recovery. The ₹170 crore SEBI provision
dropped from the narrative in Aug-2024 was **already charged** against FY2024 profit and had already cut
that year's payout ratio to 71.3% from 99% `[Q3FY25 Concall 12Feb2025, MD]`;
`[Q4FY24 Concall 13May2024, MD]`; `[IP H1FY26 11Nov2025, p31]`. In both, the loss came first and the
silence second.

**Everywhere else the base rate is nil, and it is nil in a way that must be stated plainly:**

- **RF-002:** the ₹5.05 crore was paid on 09-Apr-2025 and announced to the market at 22:43 on the day
  the order was passed. No restatement, no qualification, and **no regulatory action against BSE for
  non-disclosure** appears in any report or in the 477 announcements read.
- **RF-032:** *"No realised loss is attributable to this, here or in the system"*; a Schedule V omission
  would ordinarily draw a monetary penalty from the listing exchange, not a market consequence.
- **RF-008:** **no clearing-corporation default has been realised in India** since interoperability went
  live in June 2019 and ICCL's Core SGF has never been drawn on. Nothing on the balance sheet moved —
  Core SGF ₹1,24,683 lakh and clearing liabilities ₹3,55,423 lakh both **grew**.
- **RF-050:** no realised loss is attributable to any of the eight entities; no transaction with any of
  them was ever large — the IPF's own P&L cost is ₹279 lakh against ₹267 lakh
  `[AR FY26, Standalone Note 27, PDF p287/pr276]`.
- **RF-009:** BSE's history contains **no instance** of a contingent claim crystallising into a material
  charge in the nine years read.

**And the governing base rate above all of them:** in ten years, **zero rupees** of realised loss at BSE
is attributable to an accounting-quality channel. Every rupee actually lost came from a regulator
(₹1,861 lakh FY2020 ISF/IPF plough-back; ₹8.33 crore of SEBI penalties; the retrospective levy; RBI's
ETCD directions), a counterparty (₹1,453 lakh S&P Dow Jones arbitration; ₹1,535 lakh of receivable
write-offs; the ~₹40 crore ICCL single-name provision) or a capital-allocation decision (₹20,919 lakh
into India INX against ₹24,023 lakh of cumulative loss). Source: `W5_earnings_quality.md` §5.4, on
`[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p94-95/pr83-84]`, `[AR FY26, Form AOC-1, PDF p312/pr301]` and
`[AR FY26, Standalone Note 7, PDF p267/pr256]`.

### 4.3 The honest three-part answer

1. **It is a pattern, not a coincidence.** Directional in every establishable case, propagating across
   two independent reporting channels with different producers, calendars and assurance regimes, and
   asymmetric inside a single disclosure. Coincidence is rejected.
2. **It is not, on this evidence, a concealment pattern — and it is decisively not the channel through
   which this company loses money.** The base rate is one clean instance in ten years, two ambiguous
   ones where the loss preceded the silence, and **zero instances of the disclosure gap itself producing
   a rupee of loss**. Anyone who reads §4.1 and stops has overstated the finding.
3. **A capacity component is real and is worth roughly the smaller half — and on three of the five, the
   evidence is insufficient to distinguish process from intent.** Say so rather than resolve it:
   - **Mechanically explicable without intent (3 of 5):** RF-002 — the CG penalty table is entity-scoped
     and two secretarial audits were signed by two different firms thirteen days apart (ICCL's
     23-Apr-2025, BSE's 06-May-2025), so nobody had to consolidate and nobody did. RF-050 — six of the
     eight are legacy charitable trusts of the pre-demutualisation exchange with immaterial or nil
     transactions, and dropping them arguably improves signal-to-noise. RF-032 — a section letter was
     reused and everything after it shifted up, which is what a template edit looks like.
   - **Requires a positive act (2 of 5):** RF-008 — a comparative was **actively restated** by
     ₹10,60,000 lakh; somebody changed last year's number, which is not an omission. RF-050's two
     employee-benefit trusts — Ind AS 24 makes them related parties by definition, so removing them is a
     decision, not an oversight, and it happened in the year their cash flows multiplied 12.3× and 2.8×
     `[AR FY26, Consolidated Note 43, PDF p233/pr222]`; `[AR FY26, Consolidated Note 25, PDF p212/pr201]`.
   - **The strongest disconfirming fact against any intent reading, and it is strong:** the **same-day
     disclosure record is unbroken across 477 announcements**, including two director resignations, a
     Chief Regulatory Officer's departure and four adverse orders; FY2026 **added** three disclosures
     (BRSR Core reasonable assurance, a full discontinued-operation disposal table, a Clearing and
     Settlement footnote); colocation revenue was refused in Aug-2025 and then **disclosed quarterly and
     retrospectively** from Nov-2025; and one limb of the original five — treasury income on clearing
     and settlement funds — was **withdrawn as false** by the producing agent itself, because it is
     printed in all three 2026 decks with an explicit −21% column.

> **Verdict, in one line: the disclosure perimeter at BSE moves with the news while the arithmetic
> inside it does not, and in ten years that has cost the company approximately nothing and cost the
> reader a great deal.** Both halves ship. Stating only the first overstates it; stating only the second
> is a false clean.

## 5. THE STRUCTURAL CONFLICTS — SPECIFIC TO AN MII, WITH NO ANALOGUE IN AN ORDINARY COMPANY

### 5.1 BSE regulates the companies it earns listing fees from

**Governed on the money. Ungoverned on observability. And it widened in the year under review.**

The exposure is real and it is **falling fast**: listing fees plus corporate-services and book-building
fees as a share of standalone revenue went `217 ÷ 724.02 = 30.0%` (FY2022) → `240 ÷ 1,291.80 = 18.6%`
(FY2024) → `346 ÷ 4,836.34 = 7.15%` (FY2026) `[AR FY22, MD&A, PDF p81/pr72]`;
`[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p63/pr52]`; `[AR FY26, Board's Report, PDF p23/pr12]`. **The financial temptation to
go soft on an issuer is a fifth of what it was four years ago** — and that counter-proportion runs the
right way, which is why this conflict is graded lower than the other three.

Two further mitigants, both checkable. **Enforcement fines do not reach BSE's P&L** — penalties
collected and transferred as per SEBI directions, and fines collected from members by ICCL, go to the
settlement and protection funds `[AR FY26, Consolidated Note 40, PDF p231/pr220]`. And **BSE does delist
its own fee-payers**: 1,484 companies between 1-Apr-2016 and 31-Mar-2026 for non-compliance
`[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p66/pr55]`.

**The one uncomfortable number, recorded and not concluded:** enforcement delistings were 67 in FY2025
(`67 ÷ 5,452 = 1.23%` of listed companies) and 34 in FY2026 (`34 ÷ 5,663 = 0.60%`) — **halved as a share
of the base** in a year listing-fee income rose 8% `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p57/pr46, PDF p63/pr52, PDF
p66/pr55]`. The honest counter is that the stock of chronically suspended companies is finite and largely
worked through, and a declining run-rate is what exhaustion looks like as much as what forbearance looks
like. **Recorded, not concluded.**

**And the conflict widened in FY2026.** SEBI's circular of 12-Jul-2024 recognised **BSE Limited itself**
— not a subsidiary — as Research Analyst and Investment Adviser Administration and Supervisory Body for
five years from 25-Jul-2024, and BSE inspected **84 Investment Advisers and 71 Research Analysts** in
that capacity in FY2026 `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p65/pr54]`; `[AR FY26, MD&A, PDF p64/pr53]`. The
arm's-length subsidiary that had held the function — BSE Administration & Supervision Limited, principal
activity *"Administration and supervision of SEBI Registered Advisers"* — transferred ₹529 lakh of
membership and subscription fees to the parent in FY2025 and was then **merged into BSE Technologies
Private Limited, the group's commercial IT-services company**, with appointed date 1-Apr-2025
`[AR FY26, Standalone Note 35, PDF p297/pr286]`; `[AR FY26, Board's Report, PDF p28/pr17]`. **The fee
stream is not separately disclosed** — it sits inside *"Annual subscription and processing fees 4,448"*
or *"Other fees 2,620"*, and the segment note reports **one segment**
`[AR FY26, Standalone Note 22, PDF p285/pr274]`; `[AR FY26, Standalone Note 38, PDF p303/pr292]`.
`NOT-DISCLOSED -> the RAASB/IAASB fee schedule filed with SEBI under the 12-Jul-2024 circular.`
**So the supervisor and the fee recipient are now the same legal entity, its other business sells
technology to the population it supervises, and the revenue from the newly absorbed supervisory role
cannot be located in the financial statements at all.**

### 5.2 BSE competes with NSE while ICCL's Core SGF holds NSE's and MSE's money

**Governed by design — and the design's only quantified measure left the annual report in the year under
review.**

ICCL's Core Settlement Guarantee Fund at 31-Mar-2026 is **₹1,23,264 lakh**, contributed ICCL 60,896 ·
BSE 17,952 · **NSE 26,529** · MSE 934 · other 16,953 `[AR FY26, Consolidated Note 41, PDF p232/pr221]`.
NSE's money is `26,529 ÷ 1,23,264 = 21.52%` of the fund, against `24,760 ÷ 1,12,043 = 22.10%` a year
earlier — down as a share, up `26,529 ÷ 24,760 − 1 = +7.1%` in rupees
`[AR FY25, Consolidated Note 42, PDF p213/pr202]`. **The same mechanism runs the other way and is worth
more money:** under interoperability a trading member chooses its clearing corporation, so BSE pays its
rival's clearing arm whenever a BSE trade clears there — and in FY2026 BSE's **external** clearing cost
fell `31,856 → 24,584 = −22.83%` because members moved clearing onto ICCL, while the related-party
portion rose `12,607 ÷ 7,111 = +77.3%`.

**What governs it is the regulator's own design, and it is a mutual-hostage arrangement rather than a
disclosure.** Each clearing corporation holds the other's collateral, so neither can act against the
other without acting against itself. That is a real structural governor and it is the reason this
conflict has produced no incident. The contributor matrix is disclosed granularly, by contributor and by
segment, and ties exactly.

**What is ungoverned is the measurement.** The single quantified measure of exactly this exposure — the
**₹10,60,000 lakh inter-CCP bank guarantee**, `10,60,000 ÷ 4,42,373 = 2.396×` consolidated net worth —
left the annual report in FY2026 with the prior-year comparative restated to ₹24 lakh and no note, and
was replaced by a qualitative footnote asserting collateral adequacy with no figure
`[AR FY26, Consolidated Note 37, PDF p227/pr216]`. **The conflict most specific to an MII lost its only
number in the year under review.** Compounding it: `6,24,532 ÷ 13,44,614 = 46.45%` of consolidated total
assets — the half holding member margin, settlement obligations and the Core SGF — is audited by a firm
other than the group auditor while producing `38,458 ÷ 5,14,810 = 7.47%` of consolidated income, and the
consolidated audit carries **one** KAM, IT systems and controls, which cannot catch valuation or
classification inside ICCL's balance sheet — which is precisely where the ₹56,751 lakh reserve
reclassification happened.

**One further fact belongs on the record:** the group's clearing corporation was served a SEBI
show-cause notice for non-compliance with **the very interoperability framework that determines whether
BSE pays its own subsidiary or a competitor**, disposed of on 15-May-2025 without monetary penalty
`[AR FY26, ICCL Secretarial Audit, PDF p48/pr37]`.

### 5.3 SEBI approves every director

**The strongest governor in the framework — and the source of the independence it creates migrated
during the window, undisclosed as a change, and the report contradicts itself about it eight pages
apart.**

Independence at BSE is a **quota, not a judgement**: six of eight PIDs, a PID Chairperson, Chair/CEO
separation mandated, every appointment SEBI-approved, and SEBI holding an **unexercised** right to place
up to three nominee directors `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p78/pr67]`. No ordinary listed company's board
is this insulated from management.

**But the FY2026 annual report gives two different answers about who actually appoints a PID:**

- p78: *"While SEBI has the authority to appoint upto three nominee directors, there are currently no
  such appointments in place. All appointments of Directors have been duly approved by SEBI."* — SEBI
  **approves**; the company proposes `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p78/pr67]`.
- p85, eight pages later: *"As per SECC Regulations, Public Interest Directors **can be appointed by
  SEBI** on the Board of a Recognized Stock Exchange…"* — SEBI **appoints**
  `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p85/pr74]`.

**Both stand** (§7.2). And the nine-year language record supports a **migration from the second to the
first**: FY2019 *"SEBI has nominated… based on the recommendation of Nomination and Remuneration
Committee and Board"* `[AR FY19, Board's Report, PDF p28/pr19]`; FY2020–FY2022 *"nominated by SEBI"*
`[AR FY22, Board's Report, PDF p28/pr19]`; FY2024 *"Based on the recommendation of NRC, Board of
Directors and approval of SEBI"* `[AR FY24, Board's Report, PDF p21/pr15]`; FY2025–FY2026 *"All
appointments of Directors have been duly approved by SEBI"*. **The initiative moved inside the company,
to a Nomination and Remuneration Committee composed of incumbent PIDs choosing the next PIDs, with SEBI
holding only a negative.** That is self-perpetuation with an external brake — materially weaker than
external appointment, and **it is not disclosed as a change**; it is visible only by reading nine years
of the same paragraph. `NOT-DISCLOSED -> SEBI (SECC) Regulations 2018, Regulation 24 and Schedule II
Part B, and SEBI's approval letters, referenced but not reproduced at `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF
p78/pr67]`.`

**And the one structural change SEBI ordered specifically to separate regulation from business had not
been made.** The three vertical Executive Directors were unappointed at 7-May-2026 and the Chief
Regulatory Officer reports to the MD & CEO — the head of supervision reporting to the person whose
scorecard is business growth `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p79/pr68]`;
`[AR FY26, Board's Report, PDF p32/pr21]`. Every SEBI penalty the group has ever paid came out of that
function.

**A second-order cost of SEBI-administered board supply, and it is nobody's misconduct:** BSE recruits
PIDs from the same pool of retired senior officials and jurists that government and regulators recruit
from, so it systematically loses them to better offers. Two mid-term PID resignations in consecutive
years carried the **identical** stated reason — *"due to potential conflict with new assignment"* —
Pramod Agrawal after 345 days as a PID and Nandkumar Saravade after ~18.5 months
`[AR FY25, CG Report, PDF p76/pr65]`; `[AR FY26, CG Report, PDF p80/pr69]`. Both were disclosed to NSE
the same day. Mean PID tenure is 2.21 years and the Chairperson and the second-longest PID exhaust their
SECC terms **on the same day in May 2028**.

### 5.4 BSE's shares are listed on NSE, so NSE polices BSE's LODR compliance

**This is the conflict with a live, testable failure on the record, and the mitigant did not fire.**

BSE disclosed the SEBI LODR Schedule V Part C clause 9(d) market-price table and the clause 9(e)
performance-comparison charts in FY2024 and **neither in FY2025 or FY2026**. The section letter B was
reused for *Share Transfer System* and everything after it shifted up. Searched independently across all
three deep years: the phrase *"STOCK MARKET PRICE"* returns **zero** hits in FY2025 and FY2026, and
*"performance in comparison"* returns **zero** hits in all three CG reports
`[AR FY2024, CG Report, PDF p92/pr86]`; `[AR FY2025, CG Report, PDF p85/pr74]`;
`[AR FY2026, CG Report, PDF p92/pr81]`. Text-extraction failure was tested for and rejected — FY2024's
table extracts cleanly from the same pipeline.

**Why the counterparty is the point.** BSE is a stock exchange. Disseminating market price data is a
revenue line — Data services earned **₹6,763 lakh** in FY2026 `[AR FY26, Note 22, PDF p211/pr200]` — and
under the SECC Regulations **BSE supervises the LODR compliance of the companies listed on it**. The
entity that polices this exact disclosure requirement for others, and sells the underlying data
commercially, stopped making the disclosure about itself.

**How it is governed: by design, one step removed — and the design produced nothing.** SEBI's structural
answer to the conflict is that BSE's own shares are listed on NSE, so **NSE, not BSE, is BSE's listing
supervisor**. That is the correct architecture and it is why this is not a self-supervision case. But
across **two consecutive years**, two secretarial audits and two corporate-governance compliance
certificates, **neither the secretarial auditor nor the compliance certificate flagged the omission**,
and no query from the supervising exchange appears anywhere in the 477 announcements read. The
mutual-supervision design produced a two-year unremarked Schedule V omission at the one entity in India
whose business is enforcing Schedule V.

**Disconfirming, and it bounds the finding tightly:** the data is trivially obtainable from NSE, so no
investor is deprived of information. The defect is compliance and consistency, not information loss, and
the base rate is nil — a Schedule V omission would ordinarily draw a monetary penalty from the listing
exchange, not a market consequence.

## 6. FINDINGS

### FINDING: BSE's disclosure perimeter moves with the news while the arithmetic inside it does not — the pattern is real, it is directional across two independent reporting channels, and in ten years it has cost the company approximately nothing
S1 WHAT: Five separate instances of BSE's own disclosure narrowing are on the record — the ₹5.05 crore ICCL penalty disclosed in exactly one subsidiary annexure while the same annual report's BRSR penalty table reads "Nil" `[AR FY25, ICCL Secretarial Audit, PDF p45/pr34]`; `[AR FY25, BRSR, PDF p120-121/pr109-110]`; the Schedule V Part C clause 9(d) market-price table and clause 9(e) index charts present in FY2024 and absent from FY2025 and FY2026 `[AR FY2024, CG Report, PDF p92/pr86]`; `[AR FY2026, CG Report, PDF p92/pr81]`; Bankex and every contested market-share metric gone from six concalls and three decks `[Q3FY25 Concall 12Feb2025, MD]`; `[IP Q1FY26 07Aug2025, p18]`; the ₹10,60,000 lakh inter-CCP guarantee removed with the prior-year comparative actively restated to ₹24 lakh and no note `[AR FY26, Consolidated Note 37, PDF p227/pr216]`; and eight related parties deleted, including the two employee-benefit trusts Ind AS 24 para 9(b)(v) makes related parties by definition `[AR FY26, Standalone Note 35, PDF p295-302/pr284-291]`. Three adjacent instances belong with them: the contingent-liability "of which remote" grading, the SEBI regulatory-fee note, and the 20.57% trading-member reclassification after which no figure exists for how much of BSE is owned by the brokers BSE regulates.
S2 WHY: Not a single mechanism but a single incentive structure with two arms. The first arm is that nothing compels completeness: the Corporate Governance penalty table is framed as actions "on the Company" and is entity-scoped, the BRSR table applies a Regulation 30 materiality filter, Ind AS 37.86 makes a remote-contingency grading optional, no rule mandates a dedicated SEBI-fee note, and Form AOC-1 carries no comparative column at all. Where a format does not compel inclusion, the item does not survive year-end aggregation regardless of size. The second arm is that BSE has no promoter, no equity-linked pay and no dilution mechanism, so the ordinary levers of value transfer are closed by regulation — which leaves the disclosure perimeter as the one variable that is free to move, costs nothing to move, and triggers no restatement obligation when it moves.
S3 EVIDENCE: The direction test. Of six statutory disclosures that stopped, only two correspond to an item that genuinely ceased (Specified Bank Notes, buyback) and one is genuinely both (Liquidity Enhancement Scheme) — **three of six are live and in two cases growing**. In every instance where the underlying trajectory can be established, the item was growing or adverse at the moment of deletion: gratuity contribution `443 ÷ 36 = 12.3×` and provident and other funds `+182.4%` in the deletion year `[AR FY26, Consolidated Note 43, PDF p233/pr222]`; claims not acknowledged as debts `13,567 ÷ 10,504 = +29.2%`, their first material rise against `+1.0%` across the preceding eight years, in the year the grading vanished; SEBI regulatory fees `56,616 ÷ 33,141 = +70.8%` and `56,616 ÷ 17,212 = 3.29×` the restated FY2024 base in two years, in the year the note tracing them was deleted `[AR FY26, Note 27, PDF p212/pr201]`; and guarantees that had grown every year for six years, `31 → 50,192 → 1,00,219 → 2,00,220 → 3,00,217 → 7,70,006 → 10,60,024`, before the comparative was restated away. **The counter-proportion that makes this a finding rather than a list** is the controlled comparison inside the same company in the same year: BSE's *accounting* defects run **against** its own interest — five of them, with capitalisation and depreciation conservative in every case and the FY2025 basis change cutting the FY2024 CFO comparative by ₹19,857 lakh, the opposite of what earnings management produces — while its *disclosure* deletions run **with** it in every establishable case. A guarantee **released** during FY2026 would leave the FY2025 comparative at ₹10,60,024 lakh and drop only the current column; restating the comparative is possible only on a change of view that it was never a Group contingent liability — an Ind AS 1.41 reclassification or an Ind AS 8.49 error correction — and **neither is disclosed**.

Notes — the contingent-liability note across two years and the Core SGF contributor matrix. Balance sheet — an off-balance-sheet item 2.4× equity, and the ₹56,751 lakh Core SGF reclassification out of ICCL's reserves that Form AOC-1 records without a comparative column and no note explains `[AR FY26, Form AOC-1, PDF p312/pr301]`. Business — BSE competes with NSE for order flow while its clearing arm holds NSE's collateral and pays NSE's clearing arm whenever a BSE trade clears there. The compensating control is the mutual-hostage structure itself; the class of error it cannot catch is a **measurement** failure rather than an incentive failure — mutual dependence stops either CCP behaving badly and does nothing whatever to make the size of the exposure visible to a shareholder.
DOMAINS: Governance, Notes, BalanceSheet, Industry, Business
DISCONFIRM: Sought and partly found. A bank guarantee posted as inter-CCP collateral is a collateralised operating arrangement in the normal course rather than a credit exposure to a third party, so its removal from contingent liabilities may reflect a change in SEBI's collateral mechanism or a reclassification to a funded collateral line — a change of substance rather than of candour, and that reading cannot be excluded. Nothing on the recognised balance sheet moved: Core SGF of ₹1,24,683 lakh and clearing liabilities of ₹3,55,423 lakh both **grew**, and the guarantee never appeared in the cash flow in FY2024, FY2025 or FY2026, so `W5_earnings_quality.md` correctly rules that this bounds the completeness of the risk disclosure and not the reliability of profit. The contributor matrix that discloses NSE's and MSE's money is itself granular by contributor and by segment and ties exactly, which is the opposite of opacity. And the ICCL Core SGF reclassification, though undisclosed, **improves** the quality of the disclosure — restricted funds genuinely should sit outside equity. Two sizings of RF-008 remain on the record and both are retained: 2.32× equity and 102.5% of assets (`W3_notes.md`) against 1.56× and 78.8% (`W3_balancesheet.md`).
S5 MEANS: The single conflict that has no analogue outside a market infrastructure institution — one exchange's clearing arm holding a competing exchange's money while the two compete for the order flow that generates it — is governed by a regulator-designed mutual dependence that works, and is no longer measurable from the annual report, so a shareholder can be confident the incentive is contained and cannot size what would be at stake if it were not.
BASE-RATE: Inter-CCP collateral has never produced a realised loss at this group and **no clearing-corporation default has been realised in the Indian market since interoperability went live in June 2019**; ICCL's Core SGF has never been drawn on. The exposure is therefore theoretical rather than demonstrated. The demonstrated cost on this axis is different and much smaller: SEBI's show-cause notice to ICCL for interoperability non-compliance, on which SEBI indicated ₹8,62,50,000 as a probable settlement amount `[AR FY24, ICCL Secretarial Audit, PDF p43/pr37]` before disposing of the matter with no penalty at all, and ₹5.05 crore paid for control failures inside the same subsidiary.
S6 IMPLICATION: The group's largest off-balance-sheet number can no longer be tracked from the consolidated disclosure, so any assessment of clearing-corporation risk must now be built from ICCL's own section 136 financial statements rather than from the parent's note, and the trend line that ran to FY2025 is broken rather than continued — which argues for a wider margin of safety on the clearing subsidiary's contribution to value than on the exchange's, and for treating the balance sheet as complete only on the assumption that the ₹10,60,000 lakh really never was a Group obligation, an assumption a reader must make rather than verify. The thesis-breaker for the benign reading is confirmation from ICCL's own accounts that the guarantee remains outstanding and is simply no longer disclosed by the parent.

### FINDING: BSE is a company where regulation has removed every incentive to misstate and every incentive to align — which is why the numbers are clean and why the only thing that moves is the disclosure perimeter

S5 MEANS: The trustworthiness of BSE's reported numbers is a product of regulatory design rather than of demonstrated management virtue, which matters because it tells a reader **where** to look — not at the arithmetic, which has no motive behind it and no defect that survives testing at scale, but at the two places the design leaves free, being the disclosure perimeter and the retention decision, and those are exactly the two places where this file's findings cluster.

No qualification, no fraud reported, no auditor resignation, no ADT-4, and no earnings restatement beyond ₹2,256 lakh in FY2018.
S6 IMPLICATION: The trust question at this company should be posed as "what can the reader see?" rather than "is the number right?", because the second has an affirmative answer supported by five independent reconstructions and a ten-year loss record with nothing in it, while the first has a demonstrated deficiency — so the margin of safety belongs on disclosure completeness and on the ₹1,92,218 lakh of annual retention that no policy governs, not on the reported profit. Two monitorables: whether the FY2027 Board's Report either amends the Dividend Distribution Policy to state a payout band or explains the retention against a named use of funds, and whether the SECC pay bar is ever relaxed, since the incentive structure that keeps these numbers clean is regulatory and could change. The thesis-breaker is any equity-linked compensation appearing at this company, which would introduce for the first time the motive that every clean-numbers argument in this file currently rests on being absent.

## 1. TEN-YEAR SOURCES AND USES, FY2017–FY2026, OWN-ACCOUNT

### 1.1 The construction, and why it is built this way

`W1.7_mii_overlay.md` §5 makes the reported consolidated cash-flow statement unusable as a sources-and-uses
frame: its investing section's gross deposit lines commingle members' money with the company's, and
footnotes 2 and 3 remove an undisclosed subset of each `[AR FY26, Consolidated Cash Flow footnotes 2-4,
PDF p166/pr155]`. So the operating source is taken **float-stripped** — `W4_cash.md` §3.3's own-account
CFO series, which removes the bucket-(C) clearing float, the Core SGF add-backs and the RF-046 capital
advance — and every other line is read from **each year's own annual report**, not from a later
comparative. The deployment into securities and deposits is then the **residual**, because the gross
deposit lines cannot be reconciled (G-CASH-06).

**§4a triggers 1 and 2 fired** — ten-year cumulative aggregation over twelve series — so every column was
summed in code and each total re-derived independently.

## 2. THE RETENTION QUESTION, SIZED — the drag as arithmetic, not opinion

### 2.1 What the retained cash actually earns

`W4_cash.md` §6.2 establishes the destination: **72.3% of FY2026 own-account sources went into mutual
funds, bonds, state development loans, government securities and bank deposits.** The rate on that pool
is computable to the rupee.

`DERIVED`: own-account financial assets `3,71,074` opening and `6,14,282` closing, average
`(6,14,282 + 3,71,074) / 2 = 4,92,678`; investment income `29,030`; pre-tax yield
`29,030 / 4,92,678 = 5.89%`; effective tax rate `82,384 / 3,29,914 = 24.97%`; **after-tax yield
`5.89% × (1 − 0.2497) = 4.42%`** `[AR FY26, Note 23, PDF p211/pr200]` and
`[AR FY26, Notes 9, 11 & 12, PDF p190-202/pr179-191]` and `[AR FY26, Consolidated P&L, PDF p164/pr153]`.

**The analyst who put this to management was right and was told he was wrong.** On 13 May 2026 an analyst
asked why BSE holds the cash when *"you might be just generating 4% – 4.5% post-tax returns on the excess
cash"*. The MD & CEO: *"the money is not earning 4.5% and lying idle. It is earning much more than that
even in terms of treasury, which is very much available in the balance sheet and profit and loss for you
to see"* `[Q4FY26 Concall 13May2026, MD & CEO]`. The balance sheet and profit and loss he invited the
questioner to read give **4.42% after tax** — inside the range put to him, 0.08pp from its midpoint. The
denial is falsified by the document it cites.

### 2.2 The drag, computed two ways

**(a) One year's retention.** FY2026 net own-account cash retained after dividend is **1,76,486**
`[W4_cash.md §6.2]`.

`DERIVED`: income it produces at the marginal rate `1,76,486 × 4.42% = 7,802`.
Return on closing equity **as retained**: `2,48,725 / 6,79,550 = 36.60%`.
Return on closing equity **had it been distributed**: `(2,48,725 − 7,802) / (6,79,550 − 1,76,486) =
2,40,923 / 5,03,064 = 47.89%`.
**Drag = `47.89% − 36.60% = 11.29 percentage points = 1,129 basis points`**
`[AR FY26, Consolidated Balance Sheet, PDF p163/pr152]` and `[AR FY26, Consolidated P&L, PDF p164/pr153]`.

**(b) The accumulated pile.** Own-account financial assets net of the money that must be given back —
₹74,780 lakh of statutory remittances and ₹16,993 lakh of member deposits carried inside the "Owned"
bucket — are `DERIVED: 3,05,686 + (3,08,596 − 74,780 − 16,993) = 5,22,509`
`[AR FY26, Notes 9, 11, 12, 16 & 19, PDF p190-208/pr179-197]`.

`DERIVED`: income they produce `5,22,509 × 4.42% = 23,095`.
Return on the capital left if that pool were returned: `(2,48,725 − 23,095) / (6,79,550 − 5,22,509) =
2,25,630 / 1,57,041 = 143.67%`.
**Drag = `143.67% − 36.60% = 107.07 percentage points = 10,707 basis points`.**

## 3. INCREMENTAL ROCE ON INCREMENTAL CAPITAL EMPLOYED

### 3.1 Definition, and why the definition does the work

**Own-account capital employed = equity attributable to shareholders − the two permanently
non-distributable capital reserves.** The demutualisation capital reserve (66,179, *"shall not be used
for purposes other than the operations of the Exchange"*) and the capital reserve on business combination
(10,530) are **identical in every one of the eleven balance sheets read**, FY2016 through FY2026
`[AR FY26, Note 15, PDF p204/pr193]` and `[AR FY2017, Statement of Changes in Equity, PDF p120/pr118]`.
Because the ₹76,709 lakh deduction is constant, **it cancels entirely in the incremental measure**:
ΔCE = Δ(equity attributable), exactly.

The group is zero-debt — *"The Group is zero debt company and predominantly financed its operations
through equity"*, finance cost ₹16 lakh `[AR FY26, Note 35, PDF p222/pr211]` — so no interest add-back is
required and NOPAT is profit attributable. **The CDSL fair-value gap is excluded by construction**: it is
never carried, so book capital employed has never contained it (§7).

### 3.2 The measure

| Window | ΔCE (₹ lakh) | Δ profit attributable (₹ lakh) | **Incremental ROCE** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| FY2016 → FY2026 (ten years) | 4,15,008 | 2,36,405 | **56.96%** |
| FY2016 → FY2023 (the payout era) | 17,822 | 8,774 | **49.23%** |
| FY2023 → FY2026 (the retention era) | 3,97,186 | 2,27,631 | **57.31%** |
| FY2025 → FY2026 | 2,24,928 | 1,17,109 | **52.07%** |
| FY2016 → FY2026, **with CDSL restated to market** | 7,21,000 | 2,36,405 | **32.79%** |

`DERIVED`: equity attributable `2,52,293` at FY2016 (`2,51,219 + 1,074`) and `6,67,301` at FY2026, so
`6,67,301 − 2,52,293 = 4,15,008`; profit attributable `2,49,698 − 13,293 = 2,36,405`;
`2,36,405 / 4,15,008 = 56.96%`. Retention era: `6,67,301 − 2,70,115 = 3,97,186` and
`2,49,698 − 22,067 = 2,27,631`, `2,27,631 / 3,97,186 = 57.31%`. CDSL at market:
`4,15,008 + 3,05,992 = 7,21,000`, `2,36,405 / 7,21,000 = 32.79%`
`[AR FY2017, Statement of Changes in Equity, PDF p120/pr118]`,
`[AR FY26, Statement of Changes in Equity, PDF p167-168/pr156-157]`, `[AR FY26, Note 8, PDF p188/pr177]`.

### 3.3 The number is 57% and it is not a return on the capital

**Read the second row first.** Over the seven years to FY2023, shareholders' capital grew by ₹17,822
lakh — total, in seven years — because the company was distributing 88.6% of its profit and retiring
shares with the rest. Over the three years to FY2026 it grew by ₹3,97,186 lakh, **22.29 times as much**
(`DERIVED: 3,97,186 / 17,822 = 22.29`). The incremental return barely moved: 49.23% then, 57.31% now.

That stability is the tell. **The profit did not arrive because capital was deployed.** It arrived
because, under identical rules, identical securities transaction tax and identical RBI directions, BSE's
share of the two-exchange income pool went from 14.44% to 21.58% and roughly nine-tenths of its
incremental income came out of NSE rather than out of market growth `[W2_industry, recorded in
W1.7_mii_overlay.md §6]`. An incremental ROCE computed by dividing a market-share windfall by an
unrelated equity build measures nothing about allocation.

**What allocation actually did with the money is the fourth row, decomposed:**

| Of the ₹2,24,928 lakh by which shareholders' equity grew in FY2026 | ₹ lakh | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Increase in own-account financial assets (securities and deposits) | 2,43,208 | **108.13%** |
| — of which funded by government money held pending remittance | 44,937 | 19.98% |
| Increase in physical assets (PPE, CWIP, intangibles, IAUD) | ~36,412 | 16.19% |
| Increase in trade receivables | 24,349 | 10.83% |

`DERIVED`: `2,43,208 / 2,24,928 = 108.13%`; `6,14,282 − 3,71,074 = 2,43,208`;
`55,316 − 30,967 = 24,349`; physical `68,056 − 31,644 = 36,412` on `W3_balancesheet.md` §3's series
`[AR FY26, Consolidated Balance Sheet, PDF p163/pr152]` and `[AR FY25, Consolidated Balance Sheet, PDF
p156/pr145]`.

**More than the whole of the year's increase in shareholders' capital went into the securities and
deposit book, and the incremental return on that deployment is 4.42% after tax.** The
reported incremental ROCE of 52% belongs to the exchange; the 4.42% belongs to the allocator.

## 5. THE DIVESTMENT — BSE Institute Limited, and the ₹4,900 lakh that is not there

### 5.1 The decision

BSE Institute Limited (100%) and its section-8 subsidiary were sold on **2 May 2025**, on a share purchase
agreement signed that day after in-principle board approval on **8 May 2024** — a twelve-month gestation
`[AR FY26, Consolidated Note 49, PDF p238/pr227]`. The stated rationale is *"our strategic vision to
concentrate on our core operations"* `[Q4FY25 Concall 09May2025, MD & CEO]`. The unstated one is on the
record elsewhere in the same report: SEBI penalised BSE ₹3 lakh by order of 29-Jul-2022 for *"engaging in
unrelated / non-incidental activities by BSE or its subsidiaries"* under Regulation 38(2) of the SECC
Regulations; SAT set that order aside on **2 May 2025**, the day the sale closed
`[AR FY26, CG Report §8B item 2, PDF p94/pr83]`.

**As a capital-allocation decision it is correct and small.** BIL's revenue had been falling for six
years — 2,301 (FY2019) to 1,909 (FY2024) `[records/trend_10y.csv, row rev_training_institute]` and
`[AR FY24, Consolidated Note 22, PDF p200/pr194]` — its last full year produced total income 2,881 and
PAT 526, and it is `DERIVED: 526 / 2,49,698 = 0.21%` of group profit
`[AR FY26, Consolidated Note 49(a), PDF p239/pr228]`. Exiting a shrinking, regulator-questioned,
non-core business is the right call. The execution is the problem.

### 5.2 The discrepancy — three numbers for one price, and the tie breaks against the standalone

its investment" and "the company made a 32% return" is not disclosed anywhere in the annual report. That

**This is by an order of magnitude the best capital-allocation outcome in BSE's history — and not one

been a forced seller throughout and is now a forced holder.

### 7.2 The register's arithmetic is wrong by a factor of ten — corrected

**RF-047 states that the CDSL stake at market value is "25.2% of market cap" and that the unrecognised
₹3,05,992 lakh is "22.0% of the market value of the whole company". Both are wrong by 10×.**

`DERIVED`: market capitalisation `3,409.80 × 40,78,84,077 = ₹1,39,080 crore`
`[records/capital_structure.md]` and `[NSE Bhavcopy, 2026-09-04]`. The stake at market is ₹3,50,932 lakh
= **₹3,509 crore**. `3,509 / 1,39,080 = **2.52%**`, not 25.2%. The unrecognised portion,
₹3,05,992 lakh = ₹3,060 crore, is `3,060 / 1,39,080 = **2.20%**`, not 22.0%. The register divided by
₹13,908 crore, which is market capitalisation with one digit missing. The error propagated into this
agent's own task brief and is corrected here rather than carried forward.

**RF-047's other two figures are right and stand:** `3,05,992 / 6,79,550 = 45.03%` of total equity and
`3,05,992 / 5,86,065 = 52.21%` of distributable equity.

**The correction changes what the stake means.** BSE trades at `DERIVED: 1,39,080 / 6,795.5 = 20.47×`
book. A holding worth 45% of book value is worth 2.5% of market value *because the market is paying
twenty times book for everything else in the company.* The unrecognised gap is a large fact about the
balance sheet and a small one about the valuation — the opposite of what the register implied.

### 7.3 Has management ever discussed monetising, increasing or writing up the stake?

**No, in all three directions, and the record is specific.**

- **Writing it up:** asked once, on 19 November 2024 — *"CDSL we have a sizable holding. Why it is not

  disclosure, unanswered by management.**
- **Monetising:** searched across all fifteen transcripts in the corpus. Every occurrence of
  "monetisation" refers to colocation racks or derivatives pricing. **No question and no statement about
  monetising the CDSL holding appears anywhere.** Recorded as explicitly absent.
- **Increasing:** structurally unavailable — SEBI's 15% cap.

**As an allocation decision, the residual 15% is therefore not a decision at all: it is a regulatory
floor that happens to be worth ₹3,509 crore.** What *is* a decision is the cash it throws off, and there
the direction is worth noting: BSE took `DERIVED: 3,919 / 6,842 = 57.28%` of CDSL's attributable earnings
out as dividend in FY2026 against `3,449 / 7,900 = 43.66%` in FY2025 — **taking more cash out of a stake
whose earnings fell 13.4% and whose market value fell 8.2%.**

## 9. FINDINGS

### FINDING: BSE's payout regime inverted in the exact year the profit arrived — 88.6% of profit returned over seven years, 15.2% over the next three, with no policy governing either
S1 WHAT: Over FY2017–FY2023 BSE returned ₹1,65,964 lakh to shareholders — ₹1,01,547 lakh of dividends and ₹64,417 lakh of buybacks, plus ₹1,441 lakh to non-controlling interests in FY2017 — against profit attributable of ₹1,87,330 lakh and own-account free cash flow of ₹6,854 lakh `[AR FY26, Statement of Changes in Equity, PDF p167-168/pr156-157]`. Over FY2024–FY2026 it returned ₹70,094 lakh against profit attributable of ₹4,60,126 lakh and own-account free cash flow of ₹3,30,683 lakh. The payout ratio went 99% (FY2023) to 17.65% (FY2026), an 81.2 percentage-point fall `[W2_gov_early.md §9.1]`.
S2 WHY: Nothing that can be tested constrains the distribution. Own-account free cash flow covers the proposed dividend 4.29×; the parent's retained earnings, which Note 15.4 makes the legal cap, cover it 11.07× `[AR FY26, Standalone Note 15, PDF p280/pr269]`; rupee net-worth floors are ₹20,100 lakh against equity of ₹6,79,550 lakh, leaving 97.04% surplus (RF-054) `[AR FY26, Note 35, PDF p222/pr211]`; contracted capital commitments are ₹10,029 lakh `[AR FY26, Note 38, PDF p227/pr216]`; and the ICCL transfer-price channel accounts for 5.1% of the year's retention. What changed is not a constraint but the arrival of ₹4,60,126 lakh of profit into a company that had never before had to decide what to do with any.
S3 EVIDENCE: `DERIVED: 1,65,964 / 6,854 = 24.21×` returned against free cash flow in the first seven years, `70,094 / 3,30,683 = 21.20%` in the last three — a `24.21 / 0.2120 = 114-fold` change in the same measure. The counter-proportion that gives it shape: **88.98% of the decade's own-account operating cash (`4,10,654 / 4,61,513`) was generated in the last three years, and 29.69% of the decade's distributions (`70,094 / 2,36,058`) were made in them.** The company distributed most heavily when it earned least and least when it earned most. In rupees per year, distributions went from an average of `1,65,964 / 7 = 23,709` to `70,094 / 3 = 23,365` — flat in absolute terms, `23,365 / 23,709 = 0.985`, a 1.5% fall — while annual profit rose `DERIVED: (4,60,126 / 3) / (1,87,330 / 7) = 1,53,375 / 26,761 = 5.73×`.
S4 CONNECTS: Governance — there is no dividend policy, no target ratio and no capital-allocation disclosure in any of the ten annual reports, and RF-010 records the question being answered twice with a different metric, most recently market capitalisation `[Q4FY26 Concall 13May2026, MD & CEO]`. Business — the profit that arrived is a market-share transfer, not a capital-funded expansion: under identical rules BSE's share of the two-exchange income pool went 14.44% to 21.58% `[W2_industry, via W1.7_mii_overlay.md §6]`, so no reinvestment was needed to produce it and none was made. Cash flow — the retention landed in the securities and deposit book, 72.3% of FY2026 own-account sources `[W4_cash.md §6.2]`. Notes — Note 15.4 confines legal capacity to the parent's ₹4,55,828 lakh, which is 11.07× the proposed dividend, so the constraint that does exist is not binding. The compensating control is that no dilution has ever occurred — no ESOP, no preferential allotment, no QIP in any year read; the class of error it cannot catch is capital retained and deployed at a return below the cost of holding it.
DOMAINS: CashFlow, BalanceSheet, Notes, Governance, MgmtCommentary, Business
DISCONFIRM: Sought and partly found. (i) Management's stated reasoning is legitimate and was given: *"A 100% dividend payout or a significantly large payout may indicate a situation where the company is not having any growth idea at all"* `[Q4FY26 Concall 13May2026, MD & CEO]` — and the near-100% payouts of FY2021–FY2023 were indeed a signal of no reinvestment opportunity. (ii) Absolute distributions have risen every year since FY2022 and the FY2026 dividend is +30.4% bonus-adjusted, +66.7% excluding the prior year's special. (iii) The buyback route has been tested and is closed: 2.49% take-up on the FY2024 tender. (iv) Capital expenditure is real and capitalised — PPE more than doubled while CWIP fell. (v) The ICCL over-capitalisation demonstrably worked, with clearing share reaching 25–27%. Against the company: on corrected cash the FY2026 dividend of ₹41,198 lakh **exceeded** capital expenditure of ₹35,932 lakh, `DERIVED: 41,198 / 35,932 = 1.15×`, which reverses the disconfirming evidence W2_promise originally recorded; and after ten annual reports there is still no policy of any kind.
S5 MEANS: BSE did not change its capital-allocation policy in FY2024 — it acquired one, silently, by ceasing to distribute; and the regime it drifted into is the one in which a company with no controlling owner, no stated policy and no board-published framework accumulates a securities portfolio larger than the business that funds it.
BASE-RATE: Retention at this company has cost money once and made money twice. The realised loss is India International Exchange — ₹20,919 lakh injected across ten years, ten consecutive loss years, ₹24,023 lakh of cumulative loss, no dividend ever `[AR FY26, Form AOC-1, PDF p312/pr301]`, which is `DERIVED: 20,919 / 1,76,486 = 11.85%` of a single year's current retention. The gains are the CDSL stake reductions, ₹1,02,874 lakh of cash, and the three buybacks, which retired shares now worth ₹27,045 crore for ₹658 crore. In the system, the base rate for unexplained retention at a cash-generative company with no controlling owner is that value leaks slowly through non-core investment rather than through a discrete event — which is what the India INX record already shows in miniature.
S6 IMPLICATION: Any compounding case for this company rests on the return earned by retained capital rather than on the reported return on equity, because the two have separated: the business earns 195.02% on the capital it employs and the retained capital earns 4.42% after tax, and the second weight rose 8.18 percentage points in FY2026 alone. The margin of safety required rises with the retention rate, not with the growth rate. Monitorable, with a trigger: the FY2027 payout ratio against own-account free cash flow — if it falls below 20% again with capital expenditure under ₹600 crore, the retention is unexplained for a fourth consecutive year. Thesis-breaker: publication of a board-approved dividend distribution policy with a target range, which would convert this from an unexplained pattern into a disclosed choice. Next check: FY2027 annual report, Note 15 and the Board's Report dividend section.
SEVERITY: High

### FINDING: The retention drag is 1,129 basis points on one year's retained cash and 10,707 basis points on the accumulated pile — and management denied the premise using the document that proves it
S1 WHAT: FY2026 own-account financial assets rose from ₹3,71,074 lakh to ₹6,14,282 lakh and earned investment income of ₹29,030 lakh `[AR FY26, Note 23, PDF p211/pr200]` and `[AR FY26, Notes 9, 11 & 12, PDF p190-202/pr179-191]`. On 13 May 2026 an analyst put it to the MD & CEO that the excess cash *"might be just generating 4% – 4.5% post-tax returns"*; the answer was *"the money is not earning 4.5% and lying idle. It is earning much more than that even in terms of treasury, which is very much available in the balance sheet and profit and loss for you to see"* `[Q4FY26 Concall 13May2026, MD & CEO]`.
S2 WHY: The book is a deposit-and-debt-fund portfolio, not a business. `DERIVED: 29,030 / ((6,14,282 + 3,71,074) / 2) = 29,030 / 4,92,678 = 5.89%` pre-tax; at the reported effective rate of `82,384 / 3,29,914 = 24.97%`, the after-tax yield is `5.89% × 0.7503 = 4.42%`. The mechanism that produces the drag is arithmetic, not judgement: a marginal rupee earning 4.42% inside an equity base earning 36.60% pulls the average down by the difference, weighted by the marginal rupee's share.
S3 EVIDENCE: **One year's retention.** Net own-account cash retained after dividend was ₹1,76,486 lakh; it produces `DERIVED: 1,76,486 × 4.42% = 7,802`. Return on closing equity as retained `2,48,725 / 6,79,550 = 36.60%`; had it been distributed, `(2,48,725 − 7,802) / (6,79,550 − 1,76,486) = 47.89%` — **a drag of 11.29pp, 1,129 basis points.** **The accumulated pile.** Own-account financial assets net of ₹74,780 lakh of government money and ₹16,993 lakh of member deposits are `DERIVED: 3,05,686 + (3,08,596 − 74,780 − 16,993) = 5,22,509`; returning them gives `(2,48,725 − 23,095) / (6,79,550 − 5,22,509) = 143.67%` — **a drag of 107.07pp, 10,707 basis points.** The counter-proportion that makes it a finding rather than a rate: the same balance sheet holds ₹1,10,285 lakh of operating capital earning `2,15,055 / 1,10,285 = 195.02%` after tax, so **78.30% of shareholders' capital sits at 4.42% while 16.53% sits at 195.02%** — a 44-to-1 spread in return across a 5-to-1 spread in weight, inside one company.
S4 CONNECTS: Management commentary — the denial is falsified by the two statements it cites, and it is the second time the retention question has been answered with something other than the number asked for (RF-010 records market capitalisation being offered the first time). Balance sheet — W3_balancesheet establishes that the operating asset base is 10.0% of equity while own-account financial assets are 92.06%, so the group's shareholders own a securities portfolio with an exchange attached. Income statement — 30.6% of the ₹29,030 lakh is fair-value movement rather than contractual yield `[AR FY26, Note 23, PDF p211/pr200]`, so the 5.89% is itself partly a mark, not a coupon. Industry — W4_unitecon's RF-054 shows regulatory capital is 3.0% of the equity carried, so the MII justification for holding a large balance sheet does not reach 97% of it. The compensating control is that the portfolio is disclosed instrument by instrument and is genuinely low-risk; the class of error it cannot catch is the opportunity cost, which no disclosure records because no accounting standard requires it.
DOMAINS: BalanceSheet, IncomeStatement, CashFlow, MgmtCommentary, Industry
DISCONFIRM: Sought, and three things cut the company's way. (i) 5.89% pre-tax on a portfolio that is mostly bank deposits and government-backed paper in a falling-rate year is a competent treasury result, not a lazy one — the MD is right that it is not idle. (ii) The 4.42% is a single year and includes ₹8,875 lakh of fair-value gain that could as easily be a loss; on contractual yield alone the number is lower still, which strengthens rather than weakens the finding. (iii) The counterfactual assumes distributed cash would earn the shareholder more, which is a claim about shareholders and not about BSE. (iv) A clearing group carrying visible balance-sheet strength is a competitive asset, and management ties it directly to ICCL's clearing-share gain: *"a stronger balance sheet of an exchange is very essential to showcase to the world that the clearing corporation is strong enough"* `[Q4FY26 Concall 13May2026, MD & CEO]`. What survives: the balance-sheet-strength argument is unbounded — it justifies any amount, and 97.04% of the equity is surplus to every disclosed regulatory floor.
S5 MEANS: The company's headline return on equity is not a property of its business — it is a blend of a 195% exchange and a 4.42% deposit account, and every year of retention shifts the blend toward the deposit account; the reported 43.75% is therefore a decaying number by construction, and it will decay whether or not anything about the exchange changes.
BASE-RATE: No realised loss on the treasury book: no impairment, no default and no write-down of an investment appears in any of the ten annual reports read, and the ₹4,057 lakh FY2026 impairment charge is against trade receivables, not investments `[AR FY26, Consolidated Cash Flow, PDF p165/pr154]`.

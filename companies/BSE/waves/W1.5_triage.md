# W1.5 — Triage screen: BSE Limited

**Status: this is not a gate.** Doctrine §7 puts the 8-point quick screen at wave 1.5, *before* the
expensive work, so the pipeline does not pay full cost on a company the screen would have killed. In
this run it did not happen that way. Document collection and mapping had to precede it, wave 2 and the
evidence ledger completed first, and 31 entries were already in `records/red_flags.md` before this
agent started. **Nothing here gated anything.** Saying otherwise would be the same class of error as
marking an inapplicable test "clean".

What this artifact therefore is:

1. the **screen record itself**, honestly marked, for W6 (governance synthesis) and W7 (red-flag
   consolidation) to consume;
2. the **CFO/PAT screen on the correct basis**, which is the one number the doctrine's triage owns
   outright and which the register had wrong until W1_ledger fixed it; and
3. a **back-test of the method** — of the 31 entries already found, which ones a properly-run early
   screen would have surfaced, and which genuinely needed the deep work.

Prior work is cited, not re-derived. Where W3_group_rpt, W3_notes, W3_revenue or W4_cash own an item,
it is marked pending and left to them.

## 0. The subject problem, stated before the table

BSE has **no promoter**. Both sets of FY2026 financial statements say so in terms:
*"There are no promoters identified for the Company"* `[AR FY26, Standalone Note 49, p310]` and
identically `[AR FY26, Consolidated Note 52, p240]`. All **22 records** in the NSE quarterly
shareholding series — spanning 30-Sep-2021 to 30-Jun-2026, i.e. **20 calendar quarters plus two
intra-quarter revised filings (24-Mar-2022 and 26-May-2025)** — carry
`promoter and promoter group = 0` and `employee trusts = 0`, without exception `[NSE SHP,
shareholding.json]`. The FY2019 statutory annual return's promoter tables read *"NOT APPLICABLE"*
`[AR FY19, MGT-9, p60]`. No director holds any equity share of the company `[AR FY26, CG Report, p80]`.

`waves/W1.7_mii_overlay.md` §4.1 and `waves/W2_gov_early.md` §2.1 both say **six of the eight standard
promoter tests have no subject**, and name them: pledge · promoter-entity RPTs · promoter salary vs
profit · promoter shareholding trend · promoter-group cross-holdings · inter-corporate lending to
promoter entities.

**That six-item list is not the same object as the eight numbered screen points, and conflating them
would be the mirror-image of the error the overlay warns about.** Mapped onto the numbered screen:

| The six dead promoter tests | Where it lands on the numbered screen |
|---|---|
| Pledge | Point 1 — **kills it outright** |
| Promoter shareholding trend | Point 1 — kills it outright |
| Promoter-group cross-holdings | Point 1 — kills it outright |
| Promoter salary vs profit | Point 4 — **kills it outright** |
| Promoter-entity RPTs | Point 2 — kills the *promoter* subject only; the RPT point survives on an intra-group subject |
| Inter-corporate lending to promoter entities | Point 6 — kills the *promoter* subject only; the subsidiary point survives |

**Result: 2 of the 8 numbered points are wholly INAPPLICABLE (1 and 4). 2 more (2 and 6) return a
verdict only because their subject is substituted. 4 (3, 5, 7, 8) apply as written, two of them with
MII modifications.** Recording six numbered points as inapplicable would understate the screen exactly
as badly as recording them as passes would overstate it.

## 2. The one thing the screen found that the deep work had not

Point 4's substitute test passes on the s.197 ceiling, but the two Rule 5(1) disclosures sitting
adjacent in the same annexure move in opposite directions, and neither is labelled as being on a
different population.

**Implied median employee remuneration, DERIVED from the annexure's own two numbers** (MD pay ÷ the
disclosed ratio-to-median):

| FY | MD & CEO pay (₹ lakh) | Ratio to median | **Implied median (₹ lakh)** | Permanent employees |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2024 | 540.79 `[AR FY24, Annexure A, p33]` | 48.39× | `540.79 ÷ 48.39 = 11.18` | 518 |
| FY2025 | `540.79 × 1.3593 = 735.10` (+35.93% YoY) `[AR FY25, Annexure A, p36]` | 84.46× | `735.10 ÷ 84.46 = 8.70` | 771 |
| FY2026 | 864.94 `[AR FY26, Annexure A, p37]` | 79.16× | `864.94 ÷ 79.16 = 10.93` | 850 |

The implied median therefore moved `(8.70 − 11.18) ÷ 11.18 = −22.1%` then `(10.93 − 8.70) ÷ 8.70 =
+25.5%`, netting to `(10.93 − 11.18) ÷ 11.18 = −2.2%` over two years — against the annexure's own
*"percentage increase in median remuneration"* readings of **+47%, +22%, +26%**, which compound to
`1.47 × 1.22 × 1.26 = 2.26×`, i.e. **+126%** `[AR FY24, p33]`; `[AR FY25, p36]`; `[AR FY26, p37]`.

**Two candidate explanations, and the screen cannot choose between them.** (a) Rule 5(1)(ii)'s median
increase is computed on employees present in both years, while Rule 5(1)(i)'s ratio is computed on the
whole population including `850 − 518 = 332` net new heads (**+64.1%** in two years) — permitted,
common, and unlabelled here. (b) The FY2025 direction was misread: `|−22.1%|` against a stated 22% is a
**0.1pp** magnitude match, which makes a direction error on that one cell at least as likely as a
population difference — and W2_gov_early §1.4 already records that the two-column PDF extraction of
these annexures is unreliable.

`NOT-DISCLOSED -> AR FY2025 Annexure A printed folio 36 and AR FY2026 Annexure A printed folio 37,
read as rendered pages rather than as extracted text; the annexures do not state the population each
Rule 5(1) figure is computed on.`

Severity is low either way — MD pay at 0.371% of standalone PAT is not a governance exposure. It is
carried because a disclosure whose two halves point opposite ways is a fact about the filing.

## 3. The CFO/PAT screen, on the correct basis

### 3.1 Why the headline number is the wrong number

BSE consolidates Indian Clearing Corporation Limited. Member deposits, margin money and settlement
obligations move through group cash and land in CFO. That money is **not the company's**, so a headline
CFO/PAT ratio computed over the commingled total measures the growth of members' balances as if it
were the company's cash generation. `waves/W1.7_mii_overlay.md` §2 makes this a mandatory L6/L7 rule
for the sector, not an option.

Three reasons the headline fails, each with a number:

1. **It is mostly not the company's money.** FY2024's reported CFO of 2,84,203 lakh was **73.3%
   clearing-member float** — own-account was `75,840 ÷ 2,84,203 = 26.7%` `[AR FY26, Consolidated Cash
   Flow, PDF p165/pr154]`; `[AR FY26, Note 16, PDF p206/pr195]`.
2. **The error is not a constant bias you can adjust away.** In FY2025 the float ran the *other* way:
   own-account CFO of 68,008 **exceeds** the headline 41,015 by `(68,008 − 41,015) ÷ 41,015 = 65.8%`.
   The float flatters in two of three years and depresses it in one.
3. **It is directionally decisive.** The headline says conversion is comfortably above 1. The correct
   basis says it has never reached 1 cumulatively. A gate keyed to "CFO/PAT > 1" lands on the wrong
   side of the line.

### 3.2 The measurement

Note 16 splits current other financial liabilities three ways by name — **Owned 24,992 / Earmarked
26,004 / Clearing and settlement 3,55,423** `[AR FY26, Note 16, PDF p206/pr195]` — and Notes 11 and 12
label every bank and liquid-fund line Owned / Earmarked-others / Earmarked-SGF / Earmarked-IPF, tying
exactly to the balance sheet in all three years `[AR FY26, Notes 11 and 12, PDF p200-202/pr189-191]`.
Own-account CFO = reported CFO less the movement in the clearing-and-settlement bucket.

| ₹ lakh | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reported CFO `[AR FY26, Consolidated Cash Flow, PDF p165/pr154]` | 2,84,203 | 41,015 | 3,10,368 |
| **Own-account CFO** | **75,840** | **68,008** | **2,86,117** |
| PAT | 77,166 | 1,31,706 | 2,47,530 |
| Own-account CFO / PAT | `= 0.983` | `= 0.516` | `= 1.156` |
| Headline CFO / PAT | `= 3.683` | `= 0.311` | `= 1.254` |

**Three-year cumulative (DERIVED, arithmetic shown, recomputed independently per doctrine §4b):**

- Own-account: `75,840 + 68,008 + 2,86,117 = 4,29,965`
- PAT: `77,166 + 1,31,706 + 2,47,530 = 4,56,402`
- **`4,29,965 ÷ 4,56,402 = 0.942`**
- Headline: `2,84,203 + 41,015 + 3,10,368 = 6,35,586`; `6,35,586 ÷ 4,56,402 = 1.393`
- Overstatement: `6,35,586 − 4,29,965 = 2,05,621` lakh of members' money read as the company's —
  **0.450 turns of PAT**.

### 3.3 Sensitivity — the conclusion survives every basis choice

The ledger's PAT series mixes FY2024 total-operations PAT with FY2025/FY2026 continuing-operations
PAT, and RF-027's unexplained restatement puts FY2024 CFO in doubt. Both alternatives were run:

| Own-account CFO/PAT | FY2024 CFO **as first reported** (2,84,203) | FY2024 CFO **as restated** (2,64,524) |
|---|---|---|
| PAT = ledger basis (4,56,402) | `4,29,965 ÷ 4,56,402 = **0.942**` | `4,10,286 ÷ 4,56,402 = **0.899**` |
| PAT = attributable (4,60,126) | `4,29,965 ÷ 4,60,126 = **0.934**` | `4,10,286 ÷ 4,60,126 = **0.892**` |

`4,10,286` = `(2,64,524 − 2,08,363) + 68,008 + 2,86,117`. Attributable PAT = `77,839 + 1,32,589 +
2,49,698 = 4,60,126` `[AR FY26, Consolidated P&L, PDF p164/pr153]`. **The range is 0.892 to 0.942.
Every cell is below 1.**

### 3.4 The ten-year test cannot be run at all

The ten-year headline number is `8,21,693 ÷ 6,47,456 = 1.269` (FY2017–FY2026, corrected; the 1.264 in
`records/cash_ledger_note.md` is superseded). It is wrong twice over: commingled, **and unavailable on
the correct basis**. Note 16's three-way owned/earmarked/clearing split is first disclosed at FY2023
and the owned/earmarked liquid-funds split at FY2024, so an own-account series exists for **3 of 10
years = 30%** `[AR FY26, Notes 11, 12 and 16, PDF p200-206/pr189-195]`.

Doctrine §4a trigger 2 — multi-year cumulative CFO against PAT over 5–10 years — is the test the
method leans on hardest, and on the only honest basis **this company does not have enough disclosed
history to run it.** Three years is below the five-year floor. The 0.942 ships as a three-year number,
labelled as one.

`NOT-DISCLOSED -> AR FY2020 through AR FY2022 notes to "Other financial liabilities" and to cash and
bank balances, which do not carry an owned/earmarked/clearing split; nothing in the corpus would
extend the own-account series before FY2024.`

### 4.3 The one place the screen would have been actively wrong

Run at wave 1.5 as intended, the CFO/PAT limb would have reported the ten-year headline of **1.269** —
the number `records/cash_ledger_note.md` published and then withdrew. That is `1.269 ÷ 0.942 = 1.35×`
the correct three-year figure and it sits on the **opposite side of 1**. The screen would have recorded
cash conversion as comfortably positive on a company whose own-account conversion has never reached
parity. It would have passed the gate, correctly, for entirely the wrong reason.

The correction did not come from the screen. It came from W1_ledger reading Notes 11, 12 and 16 — deep
work, at wave 1 depth. **A screen that computes a ratio it cannot source is worse than one that
declines to compute it**, and the doctrine already says so: `waves/W1.7_mii_overlay.md` §2 requires the
float separation *before* any cash-conversion statement, or `NOT-DISCLOSED`. Running the screen early
would not have produced that discipline; running the ledger first did.

## 5. Findings

### FINDING: Two of the eight screen points have no subject and two more survive only on a substituted one, so the screen's real coverage on this company is six points, not eight — and the "six of eight" already on the record refers to a different object
S1 WHAT: BSE states in both sets of FY2026 financial statements that no promoters are identified for the company, and the ownership record corroborates it without exception, so the pledge point and the promoter-remuneration point return nothing at all, while the related-party point and the subsidiary point return a verdict only after their subject is redefined from promoter to intra-group.
S2 WHY: This is a legal consequence of the 2005 corporatisation-and-demutualisation scheme, not a drift in the register — the broker-members who owned the mutual exchange were converted into ordinary shareholders and the SECC framework caps what any single holder in a Market Infrastructure Institution may own, so no successor control block was permitted to form.
S3 EVIDENCE: Promoter and promoter-group holding reads 0 and employee trusts read 0 in all 22 records of the NSE quarterly shareholding series covering 20 calendar quarters from 30-Sep-2021 to 30-Jun-2026 plus two intra-quarter revised filings `[NSE SHP, shareholding.json]`, against the company's own statement *"There are no promoters identified for the Company"* `[AR FY26, Standalone Note 49, p310]`; `[AR FY26, Consolidated Note 52, p240]` and the FY2019 annual return's *"NOT APPLICABLE"* `[AR FY19, MGT-9, p60]`. The counter-proportion is what makes the register interesting rather than merely empty: the *public* category — the mirror of the promoter category — moved from **79.43%** at 30-Jun-2025 to **100.00%** at 30-Sep-2025, a **20.57pp** reclassification in one quarter with no share changing hands, on the remark that trading-member holdings are now folded into Public `[NSE SHP, shareholding.json]`. So promoter holding is 0.00% and stable while the category that must absorb it moved a fifth of the register in ninety days. Concentration is genuinely low on either side of that change: largest holder LIC **5.57%**, tenth-largest **1.17%** `[AR FY26, CG Report, p94]`, and no director holds any equity share `[AR FY26, CG Report, p80]` — so there is no insider block to pledge either.
S4 CONNECTS: Governance — six of eight directors are Public Interest Directors and every appointment needs SEBI's clearance `[AR FY26, CG Report, p78]`; Business — a Market Infrastructure Institution is licensed, not merely incorporated, so its permission to operate is the asset; Industry — the same SECC framework governs NSE, MCX, CDSL and NSDL, making this a sector structure rather than a company choice; Notes — the statement itself sits in Note 49 standalone and Note 52 consolidated. The compensating controls for an absent promoter are the SECC ownership caps and the PID quota. The class of error they cannot catch: caps and quotas constrain *who* sits on the register and the board, but say nothing about whether a diffuse register can discipline management — with no holder above 5.6% and directors paid entirely in cash with no equity, there is no shareholder with either the stake or the standing to force a change.
DOMAINS: Governance, Business, Industry, Notes
DISCONFIRM: Two searches were run against this finding. First, for a de facto controller: none exists — no director holds equity, none are inter-se related, there is no ESOP, no sweat equity, no convertible instrument, no preferential allotment and no QIP in any year read `[AR FY26, CG Report, p80]`; `[AR FY26, Annexure A, p40]`. Second, and more important, against the finding's own arithmetic: `waves/W1.7_mii_overlay.md` §4.1 says "six of the standard eight-point screen's promoter tests" have no subject, which reads as six of the eight *numbered* points. It is not — `waves/W2_gov_early.md` §2.1 names the six and they are promoter sub-tests, three of which collapse into numbered point 1 and one into point 4, with the remaining two killing only the promoter limb of points 2 and 6. Marking six numbered points inapplicable would understate the screen exactly as badly as marking them passes would overstate it, and the register would have carried a false gap instead of a false clean.
S5 MEANS: The screen's central instrument is promoter conduct, and on this company that instrument is absent rather than clean, so the trust question relocates entirely onto disclosure integrity and regulatory standing — which is precisely where fourteen of the register's fifteen High-and-above entries in fact sit.
BASE-RATE: No realised loss from ownership concentration has ever occurred at BSE and none could: no holder has exceeded 5.60% in any of the nine years read, and no pledge, encumbrance or promoter-entity transaction exists to have failed. The systemic base rate runs the other way and is not favourable — the material governance losses at Indian exchanges have come through the management-and-regulator axis, and all three of BSE's own realised penalties in the window are of that type. The absent promoter removed one failure mode and left the other untouched.
S6 IMPLICATION: The durability of the earnings stream cannot be underwritten by reading controlling-shareholder behaviour because the variable does not exist, so the base and bear driver assumptions must be built on regulatory events and on disclosure quality instead, and the monitorable is the quarterly shareholding pattern for any holder approaching a SECC cap, next checked at the 30-Sep-2026 filing.
SEVERITY: None

### FINDING: On the only basis the sector overlay permits, BSE has never converted a cumulative rupee of profit into a rupee of own-account cash — and the headline number says the opposite
S1 WHAT: Reported consolidated CFO includes clearing-member margin, deposits and settlement obligations that move through group cash but are not the company's money; stripping them out turns a three-year cumulative CFO/PAT of 1.393 into 0.942, and the ten-year headline of 1.269 cannot be corrected at all because the disclosure that permits the split begins only at FY2023.
S2 WHY: BSE consolidates Indian Clearing Corporation Limited, so every rise in member balances is an operating cash inflow to the group and every fall is an outflow, and those movements are driven by open interest and margin rules rather than by anything the company earned. The mechanism is not accrual manipulation; it is a consolidation boundary that puts other people's money inside the group's working capital.
S3 EVIDENCE: Own-account CFO, derived by removing the movement in Note 16's clearing-and-settlement bucket, runs 75,840 / 68,008 / 2,86,117 lakh against reported 2,84,203 / 41,015 / 3,10,368 `[AR FY26, Consolidated Cash Flow, PDF p165/pr154]`; `[AR FY26, Note 16, PDF p206/pr195]`. Cumulative: `75,840 + 68,008 + 2,86,117 = 4,29,965` against PAT `77,166 + 1,31,706 + 2,47,530 = 4,56,402` = **0.942**, versus headline `6,35,586 ÷ 4,56,402 = 1.393`. The shape is the finding, not the level: in FY2024 own-account cash was only `75,840 ÷ 2,84,203 = 26.7%` of reported CFO, so **73.3% was member float** — while in FY2025 the float ran the other way and own-account CFO **exceeded** the headline by `(68,008 − 41,015) ÷ 41,015 = 65.8%`. Neither share alone says it: the float is not a constant overstatement that can be netted off, it is a swing factor that flatters in two years of three and depresses in the third. Robustness: substituting attributable PAT of `77,839 + 1,32,589 + 2,49,698 = 4,60,126` gives 0.934, and accepting RF-027's restated FY2024 CFO of 2,64,524 gives 0.899 and 0.892 respectively — a range of **0.892 to 0.942**, every cell below parity.
S4 CONNECTS: CashFlow — the reported series and its working-capital lines; BalanceSheet — current other financial liabilities of 4,06,419 lakh against total equity of 6,79,550, a pool 60% the size of equity sitting in current liabilities `[AR FY26, Consolidated Balance Sheet, PDF p163/pr152]`; Notes — Note 16's three-way split and Notes 11 and 12's owned/earmarked labelling, which tie to the balance sheet exactly in all three years; Business — the exchange-and-clearing model is what puts members' money inside the consolidation in the first place; MgmtCommentary — no deck or concall in the window presents an own-account conversion figure, so the only number in circulation is the commingled one. The compensating control is that the split is fully and correctly disclosed once Note 16 is read. The class of error it cannot catch: the note discloses balances, not flows, so the attribution of the CFO movement to those balances is an ESTIMATE — reconciled to a residual of 29 on 72,767 (0.04%) in FY2026 but only 822 on 25,155 (3.3%) in FY2025.
DOMAINS: CashFlow, BalanceSheet, Notes, Business, MgmtCommentary
DISCONFIRM: Searched for evidence that the headline is defensible and found one real point in its favour: FY2026's own-account ratio is **1.156**, genuinely above parity, and FY2026 own-account CFO of 2,86,117 lakh is 92.2% of the headline, so the most recent year is very nearly clean. The cumulative shortfall is carried almost entirely by FY2025, where own-account conversion was 0.516. Searched equally for evidence that the ratio is worse than stated: the FY2024 CFO restatement of 19,679 lakh (RF-027) would push it to 0.899, but that restatement is unexplained on both source pages and was not adopted as the base case. Also recorded against this finding: cash conversion below 1 over three years is not on its own evidence of an earnings-quality problem at a company whose receivable *days* fell from 55.4 (FY2024) to 41.8 (FY2026) — see the next finding.
S5 MEANS: The one measure the triage screen owns outright was, on this company, both wrong and unfixable at triage depth — wrong because the commingled ratio sits on the opposite side of parity from the correct one, and unfixable because the disclosure permitting the correction exists for only 3 of the 10 years, which is below the five-year floor doctrine §4a trigger 2 sets for the cumulative test.
BASE-RATE: The pattern has already cost real money in this specific series: FY2025's reported CFO of 41,015 lakh was 0.311× PAT because member balances fell, and the fall was not an operating event — open interest dropped. So float reversal has demonstrably driven reported operating cash to less than a third of profit once inside three years at this company. No realised loss to shareholders followed, because the float was never theirs to lose; the realised cost is to the measure, not to the balance sheet.
S6 IMPLICATION: Any earnings multiple applied here is being paid on a profit stream that has not, cumulatively, converted into cash the company owns, so the margin of safety must absorb a conversion ratio between 0.892 and 0.942 rather than the 1.393 the face of the statement implies; the thesis-breaker is a year in which own-account CFO falls below 0.5× PAT with revenue still growing, and the monitorable is Note 16's clearing-and-settlement bucket at each annual report, next checked at AR FY2027.
SEVERITY: High

### FINDING: Board independence at BSE is structurally the strongest test on the screen and functionally its weakest, because the regulatory function the structure exists to protect has had no board-level owner
S1 WHAT: Six of eight directors are Public Interest Directors, chair and chief executive have been separate in every year read, and SEBI clears every seat — yet the SEBI-mandated Executive Directors for all three MII verticals were unappointed at the FY2026 corporate-governance report date, mean PID tenure is 2.21 years against a six-year permitted maximum, and the company has had seven chairpersons in nine years.
S2 WHY: The structural half is mandated by the SECC framework and cannot be varied by the board, so it tells an investor nothing about the board's own choices. The functional half has a mechanism: BSE recruits PIDs from the same pool of retired senior officials and jurists from which government and regulatory appointments are made, so it systematically loses independent oversight to better offers — two PIDs resigned mid-term in consecutive years, both citing *"potential conflict with new assignment"* `[AR FY25, CG Report, p76]`; `[AR FY26, CG Report, p80]`. That is a supply constraint, not misconduct, and it is why board memory is short.
S3 EVIDENCE: 6 of 8 = **75.0%** independent at 31-Mar-2026 `[AR FY26, CG Report, p78]`, with the Audit Committee 75% independent and chaired by the one PID whose skills matrix carries Finance & Accountancy `[AR FY26, CG Report, p81, p83]`. Against that: the CG report dated 7-May-2026 states the company *"has initiated the process"* for Executive Directors for Vertical 1 (Critical Operations), Vertical 2 (Regulatory, Compliance, Risk Management and Investor Grievances) and Vertical 3 (Business) `[AR FY26, CG Report, p79]`, and the Chief Regulatory Officer *"reports to the Managing Director & CEO"* `[AR FY26, Board's Report, p32]`. The counter-proportion is what makes tenure the finding rather than a statistic: mean PID tenure is `(3.87+3.87+3.12+1.19+1.00+0.21) ÷ 6 = 2.21` years, **36.8%** of the six-year SECC maximum, while the two longest-serving PIDs — the Chairperson and Justice Vazifdar — were appointed on the same day and therefore exhaust their terms on the same day in May 2028 `[AR FY26, CG Report, p99]`. So the board is simultaneously very short of average tenure and concentrated in its only two long-tenured seats, which is the opposite of a laddered board. Two chair-less interregnums (11 and 13 days) and a 163-day MD & CEO vacancy fall out of the same dates.
S4 CONNECTS: Governance — composition, committee structure, the SECC quota; Industry — the same PID framework binds every Indian MII, so the structural score is not a differentiator against NSE, MCX or CDSL; Business — an exchange's regulatory function is not an overhead but the condition of its licence, which is the asset; IncomeStatement — all three SEBI penalties in the period, ₹8.33 cr in total, came out of that unowned function and are `8.33 ÷ 2,496.98 = 0.33%` of FY2026 consolidated PAT. The compensating control is the Regulatory Oversight Committee, chaired by a PID with external professional participation `[AR FY26, CG Report, p83–87]`. The class of error it cannot catch: an oversight committee reviews what is escalated to it, and a Chief Regulatory Officer reporting to the executive controls what is escalated — so a decision not to escalate produces no artefact anywhere the committee can see.
DOMAINS: Governance, Industry, Business, IncomeStatement
DISCONFIRM: Searched hard for evidence the functional concern is overstated and found real weight on the other side. Attendance is near-perfect — five PIDs at 8/8 in FY2026 and part-year directors at full attendance `[AR FY26, CG Report, p79–80]`; the PIDs met separately four times with full attendance; every adverse event in the 2024–2026 announcement set was disclosed to the exchange **on the day it occurred**, with no delayed bad-news disclosure found; and the FY2022 retrospective ₹99.19 lakh top-up to a departing CEO — exactly the transaction that goes through on a nod at a controlled company — was quantified line by line, tied to a firm-wide policy, made conditional on SEBI's approval and put to a shareholder vote `[AR FY22, AGM Notice, p10, p23]`. Also against the finding: the cover page of AR FY2026, composed at print time on 23-Jul-2026, lists an Executive Director for Vertical 2 `[AR FY26, Board of Directors, p4]`, so at least one appointment may have been made between the report date and printing. That is unresolved. `NOT-DISCLOSED -> the appointment date and terms of the Vertical-2 ED and the status of Verticals 1 and 3, which would be settled by BSE's Reg. 30 intimation of May–July 2026 and by AR FY2027's Board's Report.`
S5 MEANS: A screen that scores board independence on composition would rate BSE at or near the top of any Indian listed company, and that score would carry no information, because the composition is a regulatory floor rather than a board decision — the discriminating variables here are continuity and functional coverage, and on both BSE scores badly.
BASE-RATE: The pattern has already produced realised losses, which is what separates it from a theoretical concern: three SEBI penalties totalling ₹8.33 cr in the window, every one of them from the regulatory function that has no board-level owner, plus a ₹7.25 cr CGST demand `[BSE Ann 17-Dec-2025]`. The money is trivial against a ₹2,497 cr PAT. The pattern is not, because the exposure that matters at an MII is an operational direction that suspends a revenue line, and the base rate for that in this sector is not zero — two of BSE's five segments were extinguished by regulatory direction inside four years.
S6 IMPLICATION: The earnings stream depends on a licence whose custodial function has sat under the executive rather than the board, so the bear driver assumption should carry a regulatory-action scenario that is operational rather than financial, and the monitorable is whether all three vertical Executive Directors appear in AR FY2027's board table reporting to the Governing Board; the thesis-breaker is a SEBI direction that restricts a product or segment rather than levying cash.
SEVERITY: High

### FINDING: The substitute test for the dead promoter-remuneration point passes on the ceiling by a wide margin, but the two Rule 5(1) figures that sit adjacent in the same annexure move in opposite directions and neither states its population
S1 WHAT: Point 4 has no subject, so s.197 managerial remuneration was run in its place; MD & CEO pay is 0.371% of standalone PAT against a 5% statutory ceiling, but the annexure's ratio-to-median implies a median employee pay that *fell* 2.2% over two years while the same annexure's own "percentage increase in median remuneration" reads as three consecutive increases compounding to +126%.
S2 WHY: Rule 5(1)(i) and Rule 5(1)(ii) can legitimately be computed on different populations — the ratio on all employees, the increase on employees present in both years — and BSE added `850 − 518 = 332` net heads over two years, so a wave of hiring below the median would depress the all-employee median while comparable-employee pay rose. That mechanism is permitted and common. What is not disclosed is which figure is on which population, so a reader cannot tell whether the divergence is that mechanism or an error.
S3 EVIDENCE: MD & CEO FY2026 pay ₹8,64,94,321 against standalone PAT ₹2,33,416 lakh = `864.94 ÷ 2,33,416 = 0.371%`, i.e. `0.371 ÷ 5 = 7.4%` of the single-managing-director ceiling `[AR FY26, Annexure A, p37]`; `[AR FY26, Board's Report, p24]`. The pay itself is decelerating against profit, which is the disconfirming half of the sizing: MD pay rose `864.94 ÷ 540.79 = 1.599`, +60.0% over two years, while standalone PAT rose `2,33,416 ÷ 75,339 = 3.098`, +209.8%, so pay as a share of profit *halved* from 0.718% to 0.371%. The counter-proportion that makes the disclosure problem visible: implied median pay is `540.79 ÷ 48.39 = ₹11.18 lakh` (FY2024), `735.10 ÷ 84.46 = ₹8.70 lakh` (FY2025) and `864.94 ÷ 79.16 = ₹10.93 lakh` (FY2026), a net **−2.2%**, while headcount over the same span rose `850 ÷ 518 = 1.641`, **+64.1%**, and the annexure's stated median increases of +47%, +22% and +26% compound to `1.47 × 1.22 × 1.26 = 2.26×` `[AR FY24, Annexure A, p33]`; `[AR FY25, Annexure A, p36]`; `[AR FY26, Annexure A, p37]`. So the workforce grew by two-thirds while the typical pay packet, on the annexure's own ratio, did not move at all — and one person takes at least `864.94 ÷ 29,781 = 2.90%` of the group's entire employee-benefits expense of ₹29,781 lakh (a floor, since the numerator is standalone and the denominator consolidated) `[AR FY26, Consolidated P&L, PDF p164/pr153]`.
S4 CONNECTS: Governance — this annexure is the *only* place s.197 remuneration is disclosed, because the financial-statement notes carry director and KMP compensation on a different basis inside the RPT note, and `waves/W1_docmap.md` §11 records the two must not be treated as the same disclosure; MgmtCommentary — the Board's Report presents the pay as policy-driven with half the variable component deferred three years under SECC `[AR FY26, CG Report, p90]`; IncomeStatement — employee benefits of ₹29,781 lakh, against which the MD's share is computed; Business — an MII bars its managing director from stock options entirely, so cash pay is the whole of the compensation and there is no equity tail to look at. The compensating control is the Nomination & Remuneration Committee, four PIDs and one NID, which met eight times in FY2026 with near-full attendance `[AR FY26, CG Report, p83–87]`. The class of error it cannot catch: an NRC approves the *quantum* of pay, not the arithmetic of the statutory annexure that reports it, so a population mismatch between two adjacent Rule 5(1) figures passes through the committee untouched.
DOMAINS: Governance, MgmtCommentary, IncomeStatement, Business
DISCONFIRM: Searched deliberately for evidence that this is nothing, and most of it points that way. The quantum is not a governance exposure on any reading — 0.371% of PAT and 7.4% of the ceiling, falling as a share of profit, with no equity, no options, no sweat equity, no commission and no remuneration from subsidiaries `[AR FY26, Annexure A, p37]`. PIDs take sitting fees only, at 0.66× to 3.41× median. The FY2022 retrospective CEO top-up — the one pay decision that could have been abusive — was published with its full arithmetic and put to a shareholder vote. Searched also for evidence the inconsistency is my own artefact rather than the filing's: it may well be. `|−22.1%|` against a stated +22% is a **0.1pp** magnitude match, which makes a direction misread on that single cell at least as likely as a population difference, and `waves/W2_gov_early.md` §1.4 already records that two-column PDF extraction of these annexures is unreliable enough that an attendance figure could not be pinned to a name. `NOT-DISCLOSED -> AR FY2025 Annexure A printed folio 36 and AR FY2026 Annexure A printed folio 37, read as rendered pages rather than as extracted text; the annexures do not state the population each Rule 5(1) figure is computed on.`
S5 MEANS: The point-4 substitute passes cleanly on every question an investor would actually ask about executive pay, and what is left is a disclosure-care observation rather than a pay observation — but it lands in the one annexure that is the sole statutory source for s.197 at this company, which is where care matters most and where nothing else cross-checks it.
BASE-RATE: **No realised loss, and explicitly none found.** No pay-related penalty, qualification, shareholder objection or regulatory observation appears anywhere in the nine years read; the s.197 ceiling has never been approached; there has never been an ESOP, a sweat-equity issue or a preferential allotment to test. The only remuneration item ever put to shareholders as a special resolution — the ₹99.19 lakh FY2022 top-up, 0.51% of that year's standalone PAT — was approved with its arithmetic disclosed. This is a theoretical concern on the evidence available, and is graded accordingly.
S6 IMPLICATION: Nothing here moves the driver assumptions or the margin of safety, since executive pay is 0.371% of profit and shrinking as a share of it; the item is carried only as a disclosure-quality data point for the governance synthesis, and the monitorable is whether AR FY2027's Annexure A states the population behind each Rule 5(1) figure.
SEVERITY: Watch

### FINDING: The screen would have surfaced roughly a third of what the deep work found, none of it Critical, and on its own signature measure it would have reported a number on the wrong side of parity
S1 WHAT: Applied retrospectively to the 31 entries already in the register, a properly-run 8-point screen at wave 1.5 depth would have fully surfaced 9 of the 30 BSE-relevant entries, partly surfaced 6, and missed 15 outright — and its one proprietary metric, cumulative CFO/PAT, would have been reported at 1.269 when the correct basis is 0.892 to 0.942.
S2 WHY: The screen reads *disclosed positions* — who owns what, who audits, who sits on the board, what the face of the statements says. Fifteen of the twenty-one High-and-above entries in this register are not positions but *conflicts between a disclosed number and a management claim located elsewhere in the corpus*, and detecting a conflict requires holding both halves at once, which is deep-read work by construction. The screen is a position test being asked to do a consistency test.
S3 EVIDENCE: Of 30 BSE-relevant entries (RF-029 is a defect in this pipeline's extraction, not a fact about BSE), `9 ÷ 30 = 30.0%` catchable, `6 ÷ 30 = 20.0%` partly, `15 ÷ 30 = 50.0%` requiring the deep work. The severity split is the shape that matters and it runs the wrong way: of the **14 High-severity** entries the screen catches **4 (28.6%)** fully, and the single **Critical candidate** — RF-019's retrospective SEBI re-pricing to FY2007 with 15% interest, ₹22,421 lakh charged across FY24–FY25 — is only **partly** catchable. The counter-proportion inside the screen itself: all nine catchable entries come from **3 of the 8 points** (3 auditor, 5 board, 7 accounting quality), and point 7 alone carries **6 of the 9**, while points 2, 6 and 8 yielded **nothing** the screen could reach alone and points 1 and 4 have no subject. So the screen's nominal breadth is eight tests and its effective breadth is one test doing two-thirds of the work.
S4 CONNECTS: CashFlow — the 1.269 the screen would have reported against the 0.942 the ledger established `[AR FY26, Consolidated Cash Flow, PDF p165/pr154]`; Notes — the correction came from Notes 11, 12 and 16, which are wave-1 depth, not screen depth `[AR FY26, Note 16, PDF p206/pr195]`; Governance — the three points that did yield are the three that read the auditor's report and the CG report, both front-matter documents; MgmtCommentary — every Partly and every Not verdict turns on a management claim that had to be matched against a number elsewhere, which is the class of work the screen does not do; Industry — RF-030 and RF-031 needed a peer's audited accounts, which no single-company screen can reach. The compensating control the doctrine already specifies is `waves/W1.7_mii_overlay.md` §2, which requires the float separation *before* any cash-conversion statement or else `NOT-DISCLOSED`. The class of error it cannot catch: the overlay tells the analyst not to quote a commingled ratio, but a screen running at wave 1.5 has no way to know whether the separating disclosure exists until it has done the note-level read the screen exists to avoid — so the rule protects against publishing the wrong number only by making the screen unable to publish any number.
DOMAINS: CashFlow, Notes, Governance, MgmtCommentary, Industry
DISCONFIRM: Searched for evidence that the screen would have been more useful than this back-test suggests, and found two real points. First, on cost: had it run first, the screen would still have surfaced RF-001, RF-005, RF-013 and RF-026 — the ED vacancy, the float problem, the absent segment P&L and the receivables break — which are four of the register's most load-bearing entries, and it would have done so for a fraction of the token cost of waves 1 and 2. Thirty per cent yield at five per cent of cost is a good trade and the doctrine's ordering argument survives this back-test intact. Second, against the finding's own framing: this back-test is contaminated by hindsight — knowing RF-025 exists makes "point 6 done properly opens standalone Note 35" look obvious, when in practice a screen has no reason to open a subsidiary's RPT note before something has pointed at it. The Partly column is therefore probably generous to the screen, not harsh on it. Evidence against the *severity* claim was sought and not found: no Critical entry was fully catchable on any reading.
S5 MEANS: The 8-point screen is a hygiene instrument, not a thesis instrument — it reliably finds the things a company has disclosed and not reconciled, and it structurally cannot find the things a company has disclosed correctly in two places that contradict each other, which on this company is where the investment case actually lives.
BASE-RATE: This is the first company in this system's record for which the screen has been back-tested against a completed register, so no prior base rate exists for its hit rate and none is asserted. What *has* already cost something is the specific failure in §4.3: `records/cash_ledger_note.md` published a cumulative CFO/PAT of 1.264 and had to withdraw it, and the note now carries a superseding header. That is a realised error in this pipeline's own output, caused by exactly the shortcut the early screen would have institutionalised, and it is the only realised cost on the record.
S6 IMPLICATION: The reader of the final report should not treat a clean early screen as evidence of anything, because on this company a clean early screen was available and would have been produced on a cash-conversion figure 1.35× the correct one; the monitorable for the method rather than the company is whether the next back-test on a different company reproduces the 30% catch rate, and the condition under which the early-screen ordering fails is a company whose findings are conflicts rather than positions — which is every company where disclosure is complete and management commentary is not.
SEVERITY: None

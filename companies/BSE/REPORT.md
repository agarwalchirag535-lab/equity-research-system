# 0.1 THE ANSWER, BEFORE THE EVIDENCE

**BSE's numbers are reliable in amount and its disclosure perimeter is not reliable in scope — and the price is being paid for a decade of growth in a variable a regulator allocates.**

1. **The accounts can be relied on; the most-used cash number cannot be used as printed.** Earnings-quality verdict **MODERATE**. Five independent reconstructions of the FY2026 accounts close to between **0.02% and 0.13%**; twenty-six restatement movements in ten years contain exactly **two** earnings restatements, one mechanical. But ten-year cumulative operating cash flow converts at **0.712 own-account against a printed 1.272** — `DERIVED: 1.272 ÷ 0.712 = 1.787`, **78.7% too high** — and no bound tested reaches parity (§9).

2. **The price is 56.26× reported earnings and 109.74× the same year's own-account operating owner cash.** `DERIVED: 3,409.80 ÷ 60.61 = 56.26×`; `DERIVED: 1,30,34,590 ÷ 1,18,779 = 109.74×`. **The constraints roughly double the effective multiple** (`DERIVED: 109.74 ÷ 56.26 = 1.95×`) by removing money that is in the reported number but is not a claim on the exchange (§18).

3. **83.80% of the market capitalisation is growth that has not been earned.** Treasury at book, CDSL at market and a no-growth capitalisation of own-account free cash flow together are `DERIVED: 22,52,841 ÷ 1,39,08,031 = 16.20%` of the price. The remainder requires own-account operating free cash flow to compound at **26.8%–31.7% a year for ten years** against a demonstrated pre-windfall six-year rate of **13.89%** (§18).

4. **8.20% of the assets carries 93.72% of the price.** ₹1,10,285 lakh of operating capital earns **195.02%** after tax; the ₹5,22,509 lakh treasury — **78.30% of shareholders' capital** — earns **4.42%** and is **3.76%** of the price. Stripping the balance sheet at fair value makes the multiple **worse** (§7, §18).

5. **The margin of safety required is that a reader pay on ₹1,18,779 lakh of own-account owner cash rather than ₹2,49,698 lakh of reported profit** — `DERIVED: 1 − 1,18,779 ÷ 2,49,698 = 52.43%` haircut — on growth no faster than 13.89%, with the clearing subsidiary at a wider discount than the exchange (§18).

6. **The register is long and it has never cost money.** Thirty-six issues, twenty-three at High. Against that: **in ten years, zero rupees of realised loss at BSE is attributable to an accounting-quality channel** (§14.4).

> **In one sentence.** BSE's arithmetic is trustworthy and its perimeter is not; the money risk is regulatory rather than accounting; and at ₹3,409.80 the price requires a regulator-allocated expiry calendar and a market-structure ratio management calls unforecastable to deliver a decade at roughly twice the rate the company demonstrated before a one-year share transfer it did not price.

# 1. What the company does

**BSE does not buy or sell securities. It sells access to a place where other people do, and it sells the by-products of running that place — the record of what happened there, the right to sit close to it, and the right to be listed in it.**

**The conclusion first: BSE is a toll booth attached to a rulebook.** The toll is charged on activity BSE does not own and cannot create, and its size is set inside a framework the regulator controls. The rulebook half is not decoration: BSE describes itself as a *"First Level Regulator"* with published notices and guidelines for the entities under its purview `[AR FY26, MD&A §11.B, PDF p71/pr60]`.

Eight lines, for a reader with no finance background:

1. **BSE runs marketplaces** — order matching for shares, share derivatives, bonds and exchange-traded funds, plus a separate order-routing system for mutual fund purchases `[AR FY26, MD&A §3, PDF p57-59/pr46-48]`.
2. **It charges brokers a small fee every time a trade happens on it**, recognised the instant the transaction occurs, at a specified rate `[AR FY26, Note 2.3.10, PDF p177/pr166]`.
3. **It charges companies for being listed** — an annual fee, and separate fees for the machinery of raising money `[AR FY26, MD&A §4.B, PDF p63/pr52]`.
4. **It sells the trading record.** Basic data is given away; the enhanced feed is sold — domestically by BSE, internationally by a partner that owns the customer relationship outside India `[AR FY26, MD&A §4.C, PDF p63/pr52]`.
5. **It rents physical proximity** — rack space next to the matching engine `[AR FY26, MD&A §8.A.III, PDF p66-67/pr55-56]`.
6. **It licenses its indices**, designed and maintained by a wholly owned subsidiary `[AR FY26, MD&A §8.B.III, PDF p67/pr56]`.
7. **The money it holds is mostly not its money** — members' margins and deposits, clearing bank deposits, settlement obligations and restricted regulatory funds all sit on the group balance sheet `[AR FY26, Note 16, PDF p206/pr195]`.
8. **It is priced, supervised and partly staffed by its regulator**, which approves every director, appoints Public Interest Directors, sets the minimum net worth and levies a fee calculated on turnover `[AR FY26, CG Report §2.A, PDF p78/pr67]`.

**The structural fact underneath all eight.** The people who generate BSE's revenue and the people who pay it are not the same people. Investors produce every rupee of transaction revenue and pay none of it; brokers write the cheque. Issuers pay listing fees and are the entities BSE delists for non-compliance. Members pay transaction charges and are the entities BSE inspects. **BSE's two largest paying constituencies are also its two supervised constituencies**, and its demand signal never reaches it directly from the person whose money is moving.

**The group runs one regulated function itself and must hold the other separately.** BSE Limited is the exchange — established 1875, corporatised and demutualised under a SEBI-notified scheme, with **no promoter by construction**, its own shares listed on the competing exchange, to which it pays an annual listing fee like any other issuer. The clearing corporation is wholly owned and is the group's only material subsidiary. **BSE is simultaneously a competitor of, a customer of, and a supervised peer alongside, the exchange on which its own equity trades.**

**What would have to be true for BSE to stop earning money.** Only one of the conditions the documents name is inside the company's control. Brokers must be willing and able to route orders to BSE; somebody must want to trade at all; volatility must be present, because the derivatives toll is charged on option premium; companies must keep choosing to list; the regulator must leave the charge structure alone; systems must not go down; members must remain solvent; and the regulator must keep approving the board. **A calm market is not merely a low-volume market for this business — it is a market in which the base of the charge itself shrinks relative to the base of the levy.**

# 2. Industry structure, and where pricing power sits

**Pricing power splits into two different powers sitting in two different places, and getting that split wrong is the commonest error about this company.**

| Power | Who holds it |
|---|---|
| **The rate** — what may be charged, on what base, retrospectively | **SEBI near-absolutely — and also RBI and the Finance Ministry** |
| **The share** — how much activity comes to which venue | **Contestable, and it moved decisively** |

**BSE does not set its own prices in any segment that matters.** In three years the regulator has re-based BSE's largest levy retrospectively to FY2007 with 15% interest, extinguished two of five derivative segments, and reallocated the expiry calendar three times.

**But the strong form of that hypothesis is falsified by FY2026 itself.** Under identical rules, identical STT and identical RBI directions, NSE's consolidated total income fell 2.4% and PAT 15.5% while BSE's total income rose 59.1% and PAT attributable 88.3% `[AR FY26, Consolidated P&L, PDF p164/pr153]`; `[SECONDARY: NSE Q4FY26 results, 2026-05-05]`. **The regulator prices a unit of activity; it does not decide whose venue the unit occurs on.**

### 2.1 The value chain — the node with the most counterparties captures the least

| Node | What BSE captures | Who can change the split |
|---|---|---|
| Broker / trading member (~610 active in Sensex options) | nothing | SEBI; the broker's routing |
| **Exchange** | transaction charges ₹3,66,948 lakh = **82%** of standalone revenue | **SEBI** sets structure, base, products |
| Clearing corporation (100%) | PAT ₹16,979 lakh on Form AOC-1 — **and ₹6,805 lakh on Schedule III; see §7.5** | SEBI (interoperability since 3-Jun-2019) |
| Depository (CDSL, 15% associate) | ₹6,842 lakh = 2.74% of PAT | **SEBI forced the divestment and caps the stake at 15%** |
| **Index (100% since 1-Jun-2024)** | ₹3,876 lakh revenue, **82.9% PBT margin** | **BSE — the one node it prices** |
| Listing (5,663 companies) | ₹38,861 lakh = **8.70%** of standalone revenue | SEBI slabs — and BSE **regulates these customers** |
| State | **negative** — SEBI fees ₹55,625 lakh standalone; ₹51,478 lakh of STT collected and remitted | SEBI and the Union Budget |

**The asymmetry, and neither share says it alone. 5,663 issuers produce 8.70% of standalone revenue; roughly 610 actively trading members produce 82%.** Together those say the business is **not a listing utility with a trading arm; it is a member-facing execution venue with a large, low-yield listing register attached** — inverting the first thing the MD&A says about itself.

### 2.2 The gradient is the finding

> index options premium **~32–34%** › clearing **~25–27%** › colocation **~14–15%** › equity cash **7–8%** › equity futures **~0.2%**

The first is `[SECONDARY: Business Today / Jefferies, 2026-07-03]` and cannot alone carry a conclusion; both ends are primary.

**The gradient says the derivatives franchise has not dragged the rest of the chain with it.** Order flow in index options is fungible day to day; in cash equities it is not, because best-price execution requires Smart Order Routing, which management states has been *"pending for more than six months at the other exchange"* — a claim about a competitor by an interested party, recorded as such, and the only mechanism management offers for why a 32% options share coexists with a 7% cash share.

**Two of five derivative segments were legislated to zero in four years.** Currency derivatives went **₹9,733.67 cr ADT → ₹1,525.34 cr → nil** on RBI's 5-Jan-2024 directions; interest-rate derivatives nil since FY2025; commodity derivatives at 3% of their FY2023 level. **A 40% four-year segment-extinction rate, and none of it competitive.**

### 2.3 The pie barely grew; the split moved

| ₹ crore | FY2025 | FY2026 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| BSE consolidated total income | 3,236.31 | 5,148.10 | **+59.1%** |
| NSE consolidated total income `[SECONDARY]` | 19,177 | 18,713 | **−2.4%** |
| **Combined pool** | **22,413.31** | **23,861.10** | **+6.46%** |
| **BSE share** | **14.44%** | **21.58%** | **+714 bps** |

**Share-neutral growth would have given BSE `DERIVED: 1,447.79 × 0.1444 = 209.05` cr of the ₹1,911.79 cr increment, so `DERIVED: (1,911.79 − 209.05) ÷ 1,911.79 = 89.07%` — nine-tenths — is share taken rather than market created.** It cuts both ways: a genuine competitive result under identical rules, **and** a redistribution, which is zero-sum and bounded above by a pool that grew 6.46%.

### 2.4 The mechanism was a circular, and it has moved three times in 21 months

SEBI's framework of **1-Oct-2024** limited each exchange to **one weekly expiry on one benchmark index**, converting an incumbency advantage in the *number* of weekly products into a symmetric one-each allocation. BSE moved its weekly expiry **Friday → Tuesday on 3-Jan-2025** and **Tuesday → Thursday on 1-Sep-2025**, each requiring regulatory approval.

**Approximately 95% of BSE's premium sits in the two nearest weekly expiries**, and premium share fell to 32.4% in June 2026 **solely because the competitor had an extra weekly expiry that month**.

**Management publicly misidentified the expiry slot as an exclusive moat for sixteen months** — *"it absolutely came because it had an expiry, which did not clash with any expiry"* `[Q4FY24 Concall 13May2024, MD]` — then surrendered that exclusivity from 1-Sep-2025 and posted the fastest growth in the product's history.

> **A share won on a calendar is held on a calendar.** That is the durability statement, and it is what §18's growth arithmetic must be read against.

### 2.5 Entry barriers — and the binding one is BSE's own history

Five barriers: liquidity is a network good and the network is the product; the index is a licensed asset; a new entrant must fund a clearing corporation, a settlement guarantee fund and an investor protection fund before earning a rupee; post-trade plumbing is controlled in practice by the incumbent; and SEBI restricts even the movement of people between competing institutions.

**The cleanest proof of the first is BSE's own record.** Its equity-derivatives segment ran at **498 contracts per day in FY2017, 182 in FY2018 and 126 in FY2019** after an incentive programme was discontinued. **Four separate incentive-funded relaunches produced turnovers that did not hold.** It took roughly ten years and at least four attempts before the May-2023 relaunch worked. **The base rate for "BSE sustains a derivatives franchise" is 1 in 5, and the one success is three years old.**

**The realistic substitute is abstention, not another venue.** The STT rise of 1-Feb-2026 and the RBI collateral directions of 13-Feb-2026 both raise the cost of the trade without changing where it happens. **BSE competes against one venue and against the participation rate, and the regulator moves both.**

### 2.6 Cyclicality — and what a down-cycle did to earnings

Equity-cash average daily turnover, ₹ crore, ten years: 2,996 · 4,025 · 4,402 · **3,127** · **2,676** · 4,197 · 5,396 · **4,132** · 6,622 · 7,766 · 7,950. **Three down-years in ten; peak to trough FY2018 → FY2020 is −39.2%.**

| ₹ lakh, consolidated | FY2019 | FY2020 | FY2021 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue from operations | 45,030 | 45,051 | 50,137 |
| Investment income | 20,218 | 15,866 | 12,908 |
| **PAT attributable** | **19,417** | **12,227** | **14,490** |

**`DERIVED: 12,227 ÷ 19,417 = 0.6297` — profit fell 37.0% on revenue that did not fall at all**, because the cost base is a ratchet **and** investment income fell 36.2% at the same time and through the same cause: rates come down when activity comes down. **The two shock absorbers fail together.**

**The new engine has a different cycle.** In FY2026 the Sensex fell 7.06% and equity-cash turnover rose only 2.4%, yet options premium turnover more than doubled — index-derivative revenue is a claim on volatility, participation and the rule-set, not on the index level. **The corollary is the risk: a bear market no longer switches the revenue off — a volatility collapse plus a regulatory tightening does.**

### 5.3 The levy — a permanent turnover tax on a base 959× the base BSE bills on

`DERIVED: 64,969 ÷ 4,83,395 = 13.44%` of consolidated revenue, **permanently**, on a base `DERIVED: 1,87,15,375 ÷ 19,523 = 958.6×` the base BSE bills on. It is `DERIVED: 64,969 ÷ 1,83,742 = 35.4%` of total expenses — **the largest single expense in the group.**

**The levy tracks its base almost exactly, which is the proof the base is notional.** SEBI regulatory fees `DERIVED: 56,616 ÷ 33,141 = +70.83%` against options notional turnover `DERIVED: 1,87,15,375 ÷ 1,10,66,674 = +69.12%` — a movement of **+1.02%**. Transaction charges tracked **premium** to within 1.83%. **Two different bases, each tracking its own driver to within 2%.**

**The FY2026 increase decomposes 101.7% volume, −1.7% rate, 0% base mismatch in the delta** — so the wedge is a **level** exposure, not a growth driver. **The mismatch moved in BSE's favour by ₹16,402 lakh**; the levy **fell** as a share of revenue, 13.88% → 13.44%. **Both halves ship: the same ratio that flattered the margin also flattered the levy, and it hands both back on reversion.**

**The retrospective element is settled and small.** SEBI advised by letter of **26-Apr-2024** that the fee be computed on the **notional** value of options **from FY2006-07 onwards, with 15% per annum interest**, reiterated **14-Aug-2024**, which ended the contest. The retrospective element is **₹7,347 lakh = `DERIVED: 7,347 ÷ 77,166 = 9.52%` of FY2024 consolidated PAT** `[AR FY24, Note 46, PDF p233/pr227]`. **No provision and no contingent liability survives at 31 March 2026.**

**Five independent tests settle it, all pointing the same way.** (i) The provisions note prints ₹5,444 lakh as a **closing balance** beside ₹16,977 lakh as its opening balance. (ii) The FY2025 cash-flow provision line is a **decrease**, `(10,793)`. (iii) The FY2026 report **reclassified** the ₹5,444 lakh into other current liabilities — `DERIVED: 47,947 − 42,503 = 5,444`, to the rupee — **and a charge cannot be reclassified.** (iv) The implied levy rate is flat across three years: `0.002819 / 0.002993 / 0.002972`. (v) The FY2024 note says so in terms: *"including ₹7,347 lakhs in respect of periods up to March 31, 2023."*

> **The forward number is the larger one.** The ongoing levy at ₹56,616 lakh is `DERIVED: 56,616 ÷ 7,347 = 7.7×` the entire retrospective charge, **every year**. **And the FY2026 annual report carries no SEBI-fee note at all**, in the year the item rose 70.8%.

**The governance point underneath the arithmetic:** a regulator re-read its own seventeen-year-old rule, applied 15% penal interest, and **the company's contest lasted one letter.**

**The disconfirming half is substantial and is carried.** The FY2024 restatement **cut** reported cash flow by **₹19,857 lakh** — the opposite direction from earnings management, so **the basis change moved the printed number toward the own-account number.** Every input is traced in all ten annual reports. **This is a caption defect, not fabrication.**

### 8.6 Four independent tie-out failures in one statement in three years

1. The eleven-word unquantified basis change.
2. **A ₹15,584 lakh capital advance crossed operating and investing in opposite directions in consecutive years** — making FY2026 capex look like a 3.36× step-up when on corrected cash it is **1.16×**.
3. The FY2024 restatement, cutting reported cash flow by ₹19,857 lakh.
4. An eight-year omission of the associate dividend.

**All four sit under an auditor in year nine of a ten-year rotation cycle.**

### 8.7 "Dividend received" has reported something other than dividends received for eight consecutive years

**Limb 1 — the omission is real, and it is eight years.** The consolidated cash flow reports **"Dividend received 1"** while Note 8 records **₹3,919 lakh** received from the associate. Across FY2019–FY2026 the omitted total is **₹18,100 lakh — 2.46× the two-year sizing first put on the record.**

**The proof is structural.** In **all ten years** the investing line equals the operating dividend-income adjustment **to the rupee** — `1,720 / 1,633 / 1,994 / 1,302 / 31 / 11 / – / 26 / 1 / 1`. **It is a mechanical mirror of consolidated P&L dividend income, and under the equity method an associate's dividend never enters that income — so the line cannot carry it in any year, by construction.** FY2023 is the clean case: **₹3,135 lakh received, a dash printed.**

**Limb 2 — the "consolidation mechanic" defence fails on the standard.** An equity-accounted associate sits **outside** the consolidated reporting entity, so nothing eliminates. **Ind AS 7.37 requires the investor to report cash flows between itself and the investee; 7.31 requires dividends received shown separately; 7.21 forbids netting.**

**Limb 3 — but consolidated operating cash flow is NOT overstated, and that half carries equal weight.** All five operating working-capital lines were rebuilt from the notes; residuals total **₹398 lakh, 0.13% of CFO.** **The money sits in *investing*, most consistently inside "Interest received 23,798", which exceeds computed cash interest by ₹3,918 lakh — one lakh from the dividend.**

**The standalone statements settle that it is cash:** `DERIVED: 6,118 + 3,919 + 1 = 10,038`, **tying exactly, every year**.

> **The defect is composition, not amount.** At least one line of this group's consolidated cash-flow statement is **built from the profit and loss account rather than from cash and is mislabelled** — and it has survived two audit partners, one rotation cycle and eight annual reports. **No realised loss arises: the ₹18,100 lakh is in the bank and closing cash ties to the balance sheet in all ten years.**

**The consequence:** consolidated investing cash flow **cannot be decomposed by counterparty at this group** — which matters because the associate's cash yield of **8.7% on carrying value is 1.12% on market value**, a spread only the standalone makes visible.

# 9. Earnings quality verdict, with reasons

> ## TRUST VERDICT: **MODERATE**
> ## **Valuation MAY PROCEED — on the own-account basis, under the constraints at §9.6.**
>
> **Reported profit is reliable in amount.** Reported operating cash flow is **not usable as printed** and must be replaced by the own-account series. Revenue **recognition** is clean; revenue **disclosure** is materially below the peer set on the line that decides the case.

### 9.1 Why not "cannot be relied upon" — five reasons, four of them affirmative evidence rather than an absence

**1. Five independent reconstructions close.** The own-account cash construction against the balance sheet: **₹49 lakh, 0.02%**. The five operating working-capital lines against their notes: **₹398 lakh, 0.13%**. Note 22's eleven revenue components against the P&L: **zero residual**. The tax charge: **zero residual**. Property gross and accumulated depreciation, the loss-allowance roll-forward, the settlement-fund contributor matrix, the FY2024 restatement's three subtotals and the auditor's disputed-dues table against the contingent-liability note — **every one ties.**

**2. The restatement base rate is low, and the restatements attach to identifiable events rather than a habit.** Twenty-six movements contain exactly **two** earnings restatements: FY2018 PAT `71,128 → 68,872, −3.17%`, **stated and quantified in the FY2019 note**, and FY2026 EPS `96.55 → 32.18`, **mechanical and correct under Ind AS 33**. **FY2020–FY2023 and FY2025 carry no earnings restatement at all** — four consecutive clean years between two clusters, and both clusters have a named cause.

**3. Five defects run against the company's interest — the signature earnings management does not leave.**

**4. The recognition model has almost no discretion.** Point in time, at a rate **filed with SEBI**, no variable consideration, no over-time measurement, policy unchanged — **on 78.51% of revenue.** The largest source of accrual manipulation in the general case is simply not available.

### 9.6 The verdict, and what it licenses

> **TRUST VERDICT: MODERATE. Valuation may proceed** — on the own-account cash series, on accounts explicitly flagged peak-cycle, and with a wider margin of safety on the clearing subsidiary than on the exchange, **because one off-balance-sheet exposure can no longer be sized from the parent's filings** (§7.4).

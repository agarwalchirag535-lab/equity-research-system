# New-listing overlay — a thirteen-month-old listing in jewellery retail

**Unit W1.7 · doctrine §7 sector/situation overlay · BlueStone Jewellery and Lifestyle Limited
(NSE `BLUESTONE`, BSE `544484`) · listed 19-Aug-2025 · written 2026-09-09.**

---

## 0. What this document is, and what it is not

**It is a methodology document.** It contains no judgement about BlueStone, no conclusion about the
company, no severity rating and no finding. Every number in it is a **parameter of a test** — an offer
size, a lock-in date, a scheduled deployment, a defined KPI — transcribed with its tag so that a later
agent can execute the test without re-deriving the scaffolding. Where a test would produce a verdict,
this document specifies the test and stops. The verdict belongs to the unit that owns it.

**It is binding on every later unit in this run.** Doctrine §7 inserts a sector/situation overlay where
the standard template does not fit. Two things do not fit here at once:

1. **The situation.** The standard doctrine assumes five to ten years of audited, comparable, statutory
   history. BlueStone has **two annual reports, five quarterly result sets, five earnings calls and one
   offer document**, and it crosses **two reporting bases**. `state/LOOP.md` §"THE NEW-LISTING
   ADJUSTMENT" is the owner's table of re-pointings; §1 below expands each row into an instruction with
   a failure condition.
2. **The sector.** A jewellery retailer's raw material is also its currency, also its principal
   liability and also its dominant asset. Revenue growth can be entirely a metal price. Gross margin is
   a making charge and a stone mix, not a spread. The balance sheet is inventory. §7 below is the
   sector half of the overlay.

**The one thing this document never licenses is estimation.** A thirteen-month record produces **more
`NOT DISCLOSED`, not more inference**. Every re-pointing below narrows a test onto evidence that exists;
none of them substitutes a guess for a filing. Where the replacement test cannot run either, the answer
is a gap register entry naming the document that would answer it — never a number the analyst made up.

**How this document is corrected.** A later unit that falsifies a rule here does **not** edit the rule.
It appends a dated `CORRECTION` block under the affected section (§10 gives the form), naming the unit,
the evidence and the corrected instruction. Nothing is deleted (owner principle 9).

## 1. Why the standard doctrine cannot run unmodified — test by test

Each row of `state/LOOP.md`'s table is expanded below into: **what the standard test is · why it cannot
run · the instruction that replaces it · the failure condition · the unit that owns it.** A re-pointed
test is still a test. An agent that reports "not applicable, new listing" has failed the row.

### 1.1 Multi-year trend and CAGR

- **Standard.** Ten-year series, `trend_10y.csv`, CAGR on revenue, margin, capital employed.
- **Cannot run.** Four financial years exist in total (FY2023–FY2026) and they are not one series:
  FY2023–FY2025 exist only as **restated consolidated** statements in the offer document; FY2025–FY2026
  exist as **reported** statutory statements. FY2022 and earlier are not obtainable from any statutory
  channel (`GAP-001`).
- **Instruction.** Build the series as **two stacked series, not one line** (§2.2). Maximum four points.
  Where a rate of change is presented, it is computed **within one basis only** and is labelled with
  `n`. `trend_10y.csv` is renamed in practice to a four-column frame and its header row carries the
  basis of each column.
- **Failure condition.** Any CAGR presented over fewer than four completed financial years on a single
  basis; any growth rate computed across the FY2025 basis boundary; any use of the word "trend" without
  `n` beside it.
- **Owner.** `W1_ledger` builds it; `W1.5_triage`, `W3_*`, `W5` consume it.

### 1.2 Cumulative CFO against cumulative PAT

- **Standard.** Doctrine §2c break 4 — cumulative CFO against cumulative PAT over five to ten years,
  never on a single year.
- **Cannot run.** Two reasons, and each alone is sufficient. **(a)** The window is four years, and it
  crosses a basis boundary. **(b)** PAT is negative in every year of the available record
  `[Prospectus p168/pr165, RESTATED: FY2025 (2,218.37) · FY2024 (1,422.36) · FY2023 (1,672.44), ₹ mn]`,
  so a CFO/PAT ratio has a negative denominator and its sign carries the opposite of its ordinary
  meaning. A conversion ratio on a loss is not a weak version of the test; it is an uninterpretable one.
- **Instruction.** §3 in full. Five replacement tests (A–E), each with the formula written out and each
  with a defined `BROKEN` condition.
- **Failure condition.** Any CFO/PAT ratio presented anywhere in this run. Any cumulative computed
  without `n` and without the basis of each year stated.
- **Owner.** `W4_cash` computes; `W5_earnings_quality` consumes.

### 1.3 Capital allocation over five to ten years

- **Standard.** Cumulative sources and uses; incremental return on incremental capital; dividend,
  buyback and acquisition record.
- **Cannot run.** There is no multi-year listed capital-allocation record. There is, however, something
  an old company does not have: **a dated, quantified, publicly filed capital-deployment promise,
  verified quarterly by an independent monitoring agency.**
- **Instruction.** §4 in full — the IPO-proceeds test, built from four Monitoring Agency Reports, four
  Statements of Deviation, the Prospectus deployment schedule, and the annual report's disclosure of
  unutilised proceeds. Run it **alongside**, not instead of, the pre-IPO capital history in the offer
  document (the equity and CCPS rounds, the NCDs, the rights issue of 13-Aug-2024).
- **Failure condition.** Treating the absence of a dividend/buyback record as a neutral fact; reporting
  the SoD's "no deviation" as though it answered the schedule question (§4.4).
- **Owner.** `W4_capalloc`.

### 1.4 Promise versus delivery over eight to twelve concalls

- **Standard.** A hit-rate over a long run of management statements, so that a miss is distinguishable
  from noise.
- **Cannot run.** `n = 5` earnings calls: Q1FY26 (11-Sep-25), Q2FY26 (12-Nov-25), Q3FY26 (29-Jan-26),
  Q4FY26 (29-Apr-26), Q1FY27 (28-Jul-26).
- **Instruction.** Widen the promise base rather than the call count. The testable promise set is:
  **(i)** the stated Objects of the Offer and their deployment schedule `[Prospectus p156/pr153]`;
  **(ii)** the twenty KPIs the company undertook in the Prospectus to keep disclosing — "**shall
  continue to disclose all the KPIs included hereinabove… at least once in a year… for a duration of one
  year after the date of listing… or until the utilization of Fresh Issue… whichever is later**"
  `[Prospectus p168/pr165]`; **(iii)** the strategy statements in "Our Strategies"; **(iv)** the five
  calls; **(v)** the four investor presentations, two roadshow decks and two management commentaries.
  Every hit-rate is reported as `x/n` with `n` shown, and the five-call base is named **in the finding**
  as a limit on the finding.
- **Failure condition.** A hit-rate quoted as a percentage without `n`. A KPI reported as "improved"
  where its definition changed between the offer document and the post-listing disclosure (§8.23). A
  promise counted as kept because management repeated it.
- **Owner.** `W2_promise`.

### 1.5 Governance track record

- **Standard.** Board tenure, auditor tenure, resignation history, related-party history, regulatory
  history — read over a decade.
- **Cannot run.** The listed governance record is thirteen months.
- **Instruction.** **Read the events that did occur at full weight, and read the absence of the rest as a
  gap.** The register for this run: auditor change (14-Jan-2026), director resignation (10-Mar-2026),
  director resignation and cessation (30-Jun-2026), company secretary appointment (02-Sep-2026), credit
  rating intimation (07-Aug-2026), six ESOP allotments (Jan/Feb/Mar/Jul/Aug/Sep-2026), the EtherealHouse
  investment (03-Feb-2026 and 06-Feb-2026), three postal-ballot and voting-result sets, and the promoter
  SAST Reg 31(4) filing of 24-Jun-2026 which is a scanned image (`GAP-002`). Pre-listing governance is
  read from the offer document, which is the deepest disclosure this company will ever make: litigation,
  promoter history, group entities, the pre-IPO shareholders' agreements and their termination.
- **Failure condition.** **Recording a thirteen-month record with no adverse event as a clean record.**
  The permitted sentence form is in §8.5. Also: reading auditor tenure as a quality signal when the
  incumbent's tenure is under one year.
- **Owner.** `W2_gov_early` collects; `W6_gov_synth` synthesises.

### 1.6 The base rate — "has this pattern ever cost money?"

- **Standard.** Doctrine §1a-ii: before significance is asserted, establish whether the pattern has ever
  produced a **realised loss**, at this company or in the system.
- **Cannot run at company level.** Thirteen months of listed record; four years of financial record;
  nothing before FY2023.
- **Instruction.** §6 in full — the substitution ladder, in priority order, and the **exact sentence
  form** an agent must use when no base rate is found, so that "no base rate" can never be read as "no
  risk".
- **Failure condition.** An `S5` that asserts significance with `BASE-RATE:` left thin, or with
  "none found" and nothing after it.
- **Owner.** Every unit that emits a finding.

### 1.7 Peer comparison

- **Standard.** Peers on a common period basis, common accounting, common perimeter.
- **Cannot run unmodified.** BlueStone's own series crosses a basis boundary, and the sector's headline
  metrics are defined differently by every issuer (§7.16).
- **Instruction.** Peer set fixed at **Titan (for CaratLane, the closest single comparable), Kalyan
  Jewellers, Senco Gold, PN Gadgil Jewellers** (itself listed Sep-2024, so it doubles as a
  recent-listing base rate), with **Thangamayil** as a south-India comparator. All comparisons on the
  **FY2026 reported consolidated** basis, all March year-ends. Each peer row carries D1–D4 plus the four
  sector qualifiers of §7.16 (GML treatment, lease treatment, studded-ratio definition, franchise
  accounting).
- **Failure condition.** A peer table without the comparability qualifiers; use of BlueStone's restated
  FY2023–FY2025 against a peer's reported FY2023–FY2025.
- **Owner.** `W2_peers`.

### 1.8 The materiality trigger for a fast-growing line (doctrine §3, trigger 2)

- **Standard.** "Moved ≥ 20% year-on-year in absolute terms — regardless of size" makes the chain
  mandatory.
- **Cannot run as written.** In a company whose revenue grew 67.06%, 64.24% and 39.83% across
  FY2023–FY2025 `[Prospectus p167/pr164, RESTATED]`, **almost every line moves more than 20%**. A
  trigger that selects everything selects nothing, and the chain budget is exhausted on lines that moved
  with the business.
- **Instruction.** Trigger 2 is **re-pointed to relative movement**. A line is triggered if **any** of:
  - `|growth(line) − growth(revenue)| ≥ 20 percentage points` in the same year and on the same basis; or
  - the line's **share of revenue** (or of total assets, for balance-sheet lines) moved by
    `≥ 1.0 percentage point` year on year; or
  - the line moved in the **opposite direction** to revenue, at any magnitude.

  The arithmetic is shown inline in each case: `growth(line) 84.1% − growth(revenue) 39.8% = +44.3pp`.
  Triggers 1 and 3–8 of doctrine §3 are unchanged and still apply as written.
- **Failure condition.** A chain-record set that contains every line in the statement (trigger 2 applied
  literally), or one that skipped a line because "everything grew" (trigger 2 abandoned).
- **Owner.** `W1_ledger` marks the triggers; `W3_*` executes the chains.

### 1.9 Accrual and earnings-quality tests that need three or more years

- **Standard.** Accruals ratio, receivable-day trend, inventory-day trend, over a window long enough to
  separate a policy from a swing.
- **Cannot run on one basis.** The reported window is two years (FY2025, FY2026); the restated window is
  three (FY2023–FY2025).
- **Instruction.** Run both windows and **report them side by side, never merged**: a three-year
  restated series and a two-year reported series, each with its own conclusion about direction, and an
  explicit statement of what the two windows agree and disagree about. Where they disagree, that
  disagreement is itself the output (doctrine §2c: the contradiction is the finding).
- **Failure condition.** A five-point series formed by concatenating the two windows. A direction
  asserted from the two-year reported window alone using the word "trend".
- **Owner.** `W5_earnings_quality`.

### 1.10 Valuation history and the multiple range

- **Standard.** A multi-year range of the company's own multiples, and the peer range.
- **Cannot run.** Thirteen months of trading. There is exactly one dated primary-market reference price:
  the Offer Price of **₹517.00 per equity share of face value ₹1**, at which
  **29,799,798 shares** were offered, of which a **fresh issue of 15,860,735 shares aggregating
  ₹8,200.00 million** and an **offer for sale of 13,939,063 shares**, the Offer constituting **19.69% of

  `DERIVED` tie-out: `15,860,735 + 13,939,063 = 29,799,798` ✓ and
  `13,939,063 × 517 = ₹7,206.50 mn`, `8,200.00 + 7,206.50 = ₹15,406.50 mn` ✓ against the stated Offer

- **Instruction.** Valuation is built from **peer multiples on the FY2026 basis** plus **the driver
  assumptions established in waves 3–4**, with the IPO price used as one dated reference point and

  cited as such: QIB **0.93x**, NII **0.34x** at NSE `[NSE IPO detail, bidDetails]`.
- **Failure condition.** Presenting a thirteen-month price range as a valuation range; describing any
  multiple as "historically high" or "historically low" for this company.
- **Owner.** `W8_valuation`.

### 1.11 The ₹100 map (doctrine §6)

- **Standard.** The map ships with "the cumulative 5–10 year CFO/PAT conversion stated as a single

- **Cannot run.** Same reason as 1.2.
- **Instruction.** The map ships instead with **Test A and Test C of §3** stated as single numbers —

### 1.12 The industry section of the offer document

- **Standard.** Industry evidence is a non-statement domain and can carry part of an `S4`.

## 2. The two-basis rule

### 2.1 The two bases, precisely

| | `RESTATED` | `REPORTED` |
|---|---|---|
| Document | Prospectus 14-Aug-2025 (and RHP 04-Aug-2025) | AR FY2025 (230 pp), AR FY2026 (314 pp) |
| Statements | Restated Consolidated Financial Statements, FY2023 / FY2024 / FY2025 | Statutory standalone **and** consolidated, Ind AS |
| Prepared for | The offer — comparability across the three periods presented, uniform accounting policies applied **retrospectively**, prior-period items and regroupings adjusted | Statutory reporting under the Companies Act 2013 |
| Assurance | Examination report by the statutory auditor under the SEBI ICDR Regulations and the ICAI Guidance Note on Reports in Company Prospectuses | Audit opinion under s.143, plus CARO, plus the directors' responsibility statement, adopted by shareholders |
| Perimeter | Consolidated | Both perimeters printed; the run must say which it used |

**The load-bearing property of the restated basis** is that it applies **one set of accounting policies
retrospectively across all three years**. That makes restated FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 internally
comparable **with each other** and *not* comparable with each of those years' own statutory accounts. A
restated series is therefore an excellent within-series object and a forbidden cross-series one.

### 2.2 How a series that spans both bases is written

**Two stacked rows, never one line.** FY2025 appears twice, once on each basis, and the reader can see
both. The shape is fixed:

```text
| FY     | Basis                          | Perimeter    | Source                       | Value |
| FY2023 | RESTATED (offer document)      | CONSOLIDATED | [Prospectus p<n>/pr<n>]      | ...   |
| FY2024 | RESTATED (offer document)      | CONSOLIDATED | [Prospectus p<n>/pr<n>]      | ...   |
| FY2025 | RESTATED (offer document)      | CONSOLIDATED | [Prospectus p<n>/pr<n>]      | ...   |
| FY2025 | REPORTED (statutory, audited)  | CONSOLIDATED | [AR FY25, p<n>/pr<n>]        | ...   |
| FY2026 | REPORTED (statutory, audited)  | CONSOLIDATED | [AR FY26, p<n>/pr<n>]        | ...   |
```

Immediately under it, the `BASIS:` line mandated by `state/TEMPLATES.md`:

```text
BASIS: FY23-FY25 = RHP/Prospectus restated consolidated | FY25-FY26 = AR reported consolidated |
       FY25 appears on both bases: restated <x> vs reported <y>, difference <z> -> <explanation|UNEXPLAINED>
```

**What the `BASIS:` line must say, in full.** Four elements, none optional:

1. **Which years sit on which basis**, by name of the document, not by year alone.
2. **The perimeter of each segment** (`STANDALONE` / `CONSOLIDATED`) and, where the consolidation
   perimeter changed inside the window, the entity that entered or left and the date. For this run the
   perimeter question is live: Ethereal House Private Limited became a subsidiary under a shareholders'
   agreement dated 06-Jan-2025 with shares allotted 13-Jan-2025 `[AR FY25, Form AOC-1]`, and further
   investment was made in Feb-2026 `[NSE ann 03-Feb-2026, 06-Feb-2026]`. An agent must **establish** the
   perimeter of each consolidated column from the subsidiary schedule of that document — never assume
   it is constant.
3. **The FY2025 pair, both values, and the difference in ₹ and as a percentage of the line.**
4. **The classification of that difference** per §2.3, or the word `UNEXPLAINED`.

**Rules of computation across the boundary.**

- A growth rate, ratio, margin, day-count or turnover may be computed **only within one basis**.
- The permitted FY2025→FY2026 growth rate uses **reported FY2025 from AR FY2025** as its base. There is
  **no** permitted computation of FY2025→FY2026 growth that uses the restated FY2025 figure from the
  offer document.
- A four-year cumulative (§3) is the one construct that necessarily spans the boundary. It is permitted,
  it is labelled `CROSS-BASIS DERIVED`, it states which basis was taken for FY2025 (the **reported**
  one, per §2.4), and it is re-run on the alternative FY2025 basis as a sensitivity with both results
  shown.

### 2.3 When restated FY2025 and reported FY2025 disagree

The disagreement is not a nuisance to be reconciled away. It is evidence, and there is a fixed procedure.

1. **Size it.** In ₹ million and as a percentage of the line on the reported basis. Show the
   subtraction inline.
2. **Locate it.** The offer document carries a statement of adjustments to the audited financial
   statements — restatement adjustments, material regroupings and reclassifications — inside the
   Restated Financial Information section. Read it and match the difference to a named adjustment.
3. **Classify it** into exactly one of five:
   - `RESTATEMENT ADJUSTMENT — DISCLOSED`: named and quantified in the offer document's adjustment
     statement, and the amount ties. The chain link stays `TRACED`; the difference is explained.
   - `POLICY HARMONISATION`: the restated series applies a later accounting policy retrospectively.
     `TRACED`; state the policy, the year it was adopted, and the effect on each restated year.
   - `REGROUPING / RECLASSIFICATION`: the total is unchanged and the split moved. `TRACED`; state both
     presentations. **A regrouping that changes a ratio must be flagged even though it changes no
     total** — see §8.12 and §3.4's `BROKEN-R4`.
   - `AUDIT ADJUSTMENT`: the statutory account itself was changed after the offer document, e.g. by a
     subsequent-events adjustment. `TRACED`; the reported figure governs.
   - `UNEXPLAINED`: the difference exists, exceeds the materiality floor, and no disclosed adjustment
     accounts for it.
4. **Route it.** An `UNEXPLAINED` difference on any line that meets a doctrine §3 materiality trigger is
   a chain **`BROKEN` at L1**, routes to the red-flag register at minimum severity **High**, and is
   written in the five-part form of doctrine §1d. An `UNEXPLAINED` difference below the trigger is a gap
   register entry naming the document that would answer it — the offer document's adjustment statement.
5. **Never smooth it.** Not by averaging the two. Not by adopting the more flattering one. Not by
   silently using one and footnoting the other. Doctrine §2c: the contradiction *is* the finding.

**Materiality floor for step 4.** The difference is material if it is ≥ 1% of the line, **or** ≥ 5% of
consolidated PBT, **or** it changes the sign of any metric derived from the line, **or** it moves any
ratio quoted in the report by ≥ 5% relative. The sign test is not decorative: in a loss-making window a
small absolute difference can flip a margin, a coverage ratio or a turnover comparison.

### 2.4 Why the audited annual report outranks the offer document — even though the offer document came later in time

Doctrine §5 fixes the source priority: **audited annual report > audited quarterly filing > unaudited
quarterly filing > concall statement > investor presentation > media.** The Prospectus is dated
14-Aug-2025 and the AR FY2025 covers the year ended 31-Mar-2025; the offer document is the later
publication, and it is also the more detailed one. It still ranks below. The reason is that **§5 orders
documents by the assurance and accountability attached to them, not by their recency or their depth.**

Four properties of the statutory annual report do the ranking, and none of them belongs to the restated
statements:

1. **It is the account.** The statutory financial statements are the company's account of itself under
   the Companies Act 2013, adopted by shareholders in general meeting. The restated statements are a
   *presentation derived from* those accounts for a specific regulatory purpose.
2. **The opinion is different in kind.** The annual report carries an audit opinion under s.143 on the
   truth and fairness of the statements themselves, plus CARO reporting, plus key audit matters, plus
   the directors' responsibility statement. The offer document carries an **examination report** on
   restated information prepared under the SEBI ICDR Regulations and the ICAI Guidance Note — an
   engagement on a compilation performed for the offer, not an audit opinion on statutory accounts.
3. **The purpose is different.** The restated statements exist to make three periods comparable to each
   other **for a buyer of the issue**. That purpose is legitimate and the resulting series is useful;
   it is not the purpose of establishing what the company reported in any one of those years.
4. **The accountability is different.** Directors adopt the statutory accounts and shareholders receive
   them. The offer document is a selling document filed by the issuer with the selling shareholders and
   the book-running lead managers, and its financial section is compiled to a regulatory template.

**The corollary the source register already states, and this overlay makes operational.** The offer
document is used to establish **the basis on which the issue was sold** — which is a governance and
promise-versus-delivery fact of the first order for a company thirteen months old — and it is the
first-class source for everything the annual reports do not carry: risk factors, pre-IPO capital
history, litigation, promoter history, the objects and their schedule, the KPI definitions, unit
economics, customer counts and the pre-listing shareholding. **Depth and rank are different questions.**
On a line where both documents speak, the annual report governs, and the difference between them is
sized and classified per §2.3 rather than averaged away.

**One further ordering, internal to the offer documents.** The **Prospectus dated 14-Aug-2025**
supersedes the **RHP dated 04-Aug-2025** — it is filed with the RoC and carries the final Offer Price
and the final basis of allotment. The RHP is used only where the Prospectus does not carry the item, and
any divergence between the two is noted where it is used.

## 3. What replaces the multi-year cumulative tests

Doctrine §2c break 4 tests cumulative CFO against cumulative PAT over five to ten years. §1.2 gave the
two reasons it cannot run. This section specifies exactly what is computed instead. **Doctrine §4a
trigger 2 fires on all of it** — multi-year cumulative aggregation — so it is computed in code, not by
hand, and QC must be able to reproduce every figure from a deterministic formula.

### 3.0 Window, basis and the standing header

Every test below is run over **FY2023, FY2024, FY2025, FY2026 — `n = 4` financial years**, and every
output carries this header:

```text
WINDOW: FY2023-FY2026 (n=4)
BASIS:  FY23, FY24 = Prospectus restated consolidated | FY25 = AR FY2025 reported consolidated
        (primary) | FY26 = AR FY2026 reported consolidated
SENSITIVITY: FY25 taken on the restated basis instead -> <result>, difference <x>
PERIMETER: <consolidated; entity changes inside window named>
```

FY2025 is taken on the **reported** basis in the primary computation, per §2.4. The restated-FY2025
sensitivity is mandatory, not optional, and both results appear.

### 3.1 Test A — cash absorption per incremental rupee of revenue

This is the test that replaces "did earnings convert to cash". In a loss-making, inventory-building,
store-opening business the answerable question is not *how much of profit became cash* but **how much
cash each rupee of built revenue consumed.**

```
              Σ CFO_t          (t = FY2023 … FY2026, each year's basis declared)
  A  =  ────────────────────────────────
         Revenue_FY2026  −  Revenue_FY2023
```

- `CFO_t` is **net cash generated from / (used in) operating activities**, taken from the face of each
  cash flow statement, after tax paid, before investing and financing.
- The denominator is the **increment in annual revenue built over the window**, not cumulative revenue.
- Report `A` to two decimals and state its sign convention explicitly in words: a negative `A` means
  operating activities consumed cash while revenue was being built, and the number is *rupees consumed
  per rupee of annual revenue added*.
- Report the numerator and denominator separately in ₹ million as well as the ratio. A ratio without its
  two inputs is not checkable.

### 3.2 Test B — cumulative CFO against cumulative EBITDA

`state/LOOP.md` names this test. It is specified here.

```
  B  =  Σ CFO_t  /  Σ EBITDA_t          (t = FY2023 … FY2026)
```

**Two EBITDA series are computed and both are shown.**

1. **Company-defined EBITDA**, exactly as the Prospectus defines it:
   `EBITDA = Profit/(Loss) before tax − Other income + Depreciation and amortisation + Finance cost`
   `[Prospectus p168/pr165, note (5)]`.
2. **Independently recomputed EBITDA** from the face of each income statement, using the same formula,
   with the arithmetic shown inline for each year (doctrine §4b: recomputed, not re-read).

If the two disagree in any year, the disagreement is reported and the **statutory-statement
recomputation governs**.

**The company's "Adjusted EBITDA" is not used as an input to `B`.** It may be reported alongside, and
when it is, its add-backs are listed and quantified: `Adjusted EBITDA = EBITDA + ESOP charge +
franchisee commission`, where franchisee commission "**includes minimum guarantee on the franchisee
deposits and the margin paid to the Franchisees over and above the minimum guarantee (forms part of
brokerage and commission…)**" `[Prospectus p168/pr165, note (7); p8/pr5, definition]`. See §7.9 for why
that particular add-back must be traced rather than accepted.

**Degenerate-denominator rule.** If `Σ EBITDA_t` is negative, or is smaller in absolute terms than 5% of
`Σ Revenue_t`, the ratio `B` is **not formed**. Instead the two cumulatives are reported side by side in
₹ million with the statement: *"The ratio is not computed: the cumulative denominator is
<negative | not materially different from zero>, and a ratio on it would not be interpretable."* This is
a stopping rule, not a failure — an uninterpretable ratio silently printed is the failure.

### 3.3 Test C — cumulative sources and uses, and the tie-out to cash

This is the capital-allocation half, and it is the test with a hard arithmetic identity behind it.

```
  SOURCES (FY2023-FY2026 cumulative)
    + equity issued for cash (pre-IPO rounds, CCPS subscriptions, rights issue, IPO fresh issue net of
      offer expenses borne by the company)
    + net increase in borrowings (non-current + current, excluding gold on loan)
    + net increase in gold on loan
    + net increase in franchisee deposits
    + net increase in customer advances / contract liabilities
    + proceeds from disposal of assets and investments
    + interest and other investment income received

  USES (FY2023-FY2026 cumulative)
    + operating cash absorbed            ( = −Σ CFO_t  where Σ CFO_t < 0 )
    + purchase of property, plant and equipment, intangibles and capital work in progress
    + investments made (including in the subsidiary and associate)
    + interest paid
    + principal element of lease payments
    + repayment of borrowings already netted above, if presented gross

  IDENTITY:   SOURCES − USES  =  Δ (cash and cash equivalents + bank balances + deposits)
                                  over FY2023 → FY2026
```

- Every line is taken from the cash flow statements and tied to the corresponding balance-sheet movement
  (doctrine §2a: the finding lives between L6 and L7).
- **Note the deposit trap.** Several BlueStone bank balances are **restricted**: the annual report
  discloses deposits given as security against gold on loan and margin money against gold on loan
  `[AR FY26, p187/pr208 and p191/pr212, footnotes to the bank-balance notes]`. The Δcash side of the
  identity must be split into **free** and **restricted/lien-marked**, and the identity must tie on the
  total while the free/restricted split is reported separately. Cash pledged against a gold liability is
  not liquidity.
- **Tolerance.** The identity must tie to within **±2.0% of cumulative SOURCES**. Any residual above
  that is named and sized. An unnamed residual is `BROKEN` (§3.4, `BROKEN-R2`).

`C = Σ CFO_t / Σ capital raised`, where capital raised is the SOURCES total excluding investment income
received, is reported as the single-number summary of this test and is one of the two numbers the ₹100
map ships with (§1.11).

### 3.4 Test D — working-capital absorption, and the inventory limb

```
  WC absorption  =  Σ ( ΔInventory_t + ΔTrade receivables_t
                        − ΔTrade payables_t − ΔCustomer advances_t )      (t = FY2023 … FY2026)

  WC intensity   =  WC absorption / ( Revenue_FY2026 − Revenue_FY2023 )

  Inventory limb =  Σ ΔInventory_t / ( Revenue_FY2026 − Revenue_FY2023 )
```

The inventory limb is separated because in this sector it is usually the whole answer (§7.7), and
because **part of it is a gold price movement rather than a stock build** — §7.7 gives the required
price/volume decomposition, and where grams are not disclosed the decomposition is `NOT DISCLOSED` and
the limb is reported as a single undecomposed number with that stated.

### 3.5 Test E — the lease-adjusted variant

Ind AS 116 moves the rent of a store network out of operating cash flow and into financing (principal)
and into interest. In a business whose expansion is a lease-financed store roll-out, CFO is therefore
flattered relative to a pre-Ind AS 116 presentation, and relative to any peer with a different
owned/leased mix.

```
  CFO_lease-adjusted_t  =  CFO_t  −  principal element of lease payments_t
                                   −  interest element of lease payments_t
```

- State which line each element was taken from and whether interest paid on leases sits in operating or
  financing in that year's presentation — the policy choice differs by company and sometimes by year.
- **Re-run Tests A, B and C on `CFO_lease-adjusted`** and report both sets. The two numbers are both
  true; the difference between them is the size of the Ind AS 116 effect, and it must be stated in ₹.
- Anchor magnitudes available for FY2026: right-of-use assets `₹8,430.31 mn` (FY2025 `₹7,430.33 mn`)
  `[AR FY26, p164/pr185, REPORTED, STANDALONE]`; depreciation on right-of-use assets `₹1,396.43 mn`
  (FY2025 `₹1,025.33 mn`) `[AR FY26, p183/pr204]`; lease-liability closing balance `₹9,318.29 mn` (FY2025
  `₹7,994.13 mn`) with accretion of interest `₹634.50 mn` and payments including rent concession
  `₹(1,618.25) mn` `[AR FY26, p182/pr203]`. These are the standalone figures; the consolidated
  equivalents must be taken from the consolidated notes and the perimeter declared (D2).

### 3.6 What counts as a BROKEN chain under the replacement tests

A `BROKEN` state routes to the red-flag register at minimum severity **High** and is written in the
five-part form of doctrine §1d. Under the replacement tests, exactly these five conditions constitute a
break. They are exhaustive for the cash-conversion question, and each is decidable without a second
judgement call.

| Code | Condition | Why it is a break |
|---|---|---|
| **BROKEN-R1** | `Σ CFO < 0` while `Σ EBITDA > 0` over the window, **and** the gap is not fully accounted for by working-capital movements whose components tie to the balance sheet within **±2% of cumulative revenue** | Operating profit exists on the accrual and does not exist in cash, and the residue is not locatable on the balance sheet. This is doctrine §2a's L6/L7 gap, cumulated |
| **BROKEN-R2** | The sources-and-uses identity of §3.3 fails to tie to Δcash by more than **±2.0% of cumulative SOURCES**, and the residual is unnamed | A funding source or use that no statement discloses |
| **BROKEN-R3** | `Σ ΔInventory > Σ Gross profit` over the window **and** inventory days rose in every year measured on a single basis | Growth is being funded by an asset that is not turning; the accrual gross profit has been reinvested into stock faster than it was earned |
| **BROKEN-R4** | A conversion, leverage or margin metric improves **only** when the basis, definition or classification changes — restated→reported, with-GML→without-GML, EBITDA→Adjusted EBITDA, standalone→consolidated — and not on any single consistent basis | The improvement is a presentation change. This breaks at **L1**, not L7, and it is written as a finding about disclosure, not about cash |
| **BROKEN-R5** | `Σ CFO > 0`, but `Σ CFO` excluding the movements in **customer advances / contract liabilities**, **franchisee deposits** and **gold on loan** is `< 0` | Operating cash is third-party-funded. The company is generating cash by holding customers', franchisees' and bullion lenders' money, not by converting its own trading. Break at **L7**; the finding is what happens when any of the three stops growing |

**`BROKEN-R5` is computed always, whether or not `Σ CFO` is positive**, and its two numbers are reported
side by side. It is the single most diagnostic computation in this section for this sector, and it is
cheap: three balance-sheet movements, subtracted.

**Not a break, and must not be reported as one:** a single year of negative CFO in a window where the
cumulative is explained; a working-capital build that ties to a disclosed inventory increase in a year
of store openings; a CFO/EBITDA ratio outside some remembered norm. The tests above are the tests; a
norm carried in from another sector is not evidence.

### 4.2 How OBJECT records are built

**One record per stated object, per reporting quarter, cumulative** — so eight records minimum for this
run (two objects × four quarters), plus a ninth and tenth for the offer-expenses line and the total.
The block is fixed by `state/TEMPLATES.md`:

```text
### OBJECT: <stated object of the issue, in the Prospectus's own words>
PROMISED    <Rs mn> | <deployment schedule per Prospectus> | <tag>
DEPLOYED    <Rs mn as at latest MAR> | <tag>
UNUTILISED  <Rs mn> | <where parked> | <tag>
DEVIATION   <none | description> | <tag>
STATE: ON-PLAN | BEHIND | DEVIATED | NOT-DISCLOSED
```

**Construction rules.**

1. **The object is quoted in the Prospectus's own words**, not paraphrased. "Funding our working capital
   requirements" and "General corporate purposes" are the two objects; any later document that describes
   the object differently is itself evidence and is quoted alongside.
2. **`PROMISED` carries both the amount and the schedule.** For this issue the schedule is the whole
   test: 100% in Fiscal 2026. A record whose `PROMISED` field states only an amount has dropped the
   dated half of the promise and fails.
3. **`DEPLOYED` is cumulative from allotment**, not the quarter's movement, and its source is named.
   Source priority for this field, applying doctrine §5:
   - the **annual report's disclosure of utilised and unutilised proceeds** (audited, statutory) —
     highest;
   - the **Monitoring Agency Report** — independent of management but explicitly **not an audit** (§4.5);
   - the **Statement of Deviation** — the company's own quarterly declaration in the SEBI format;
   - the investor presentation, management commentary or concall — lowest, and never an `L1`.
   Where two of these disagree for the same date, both are reported and the higher-ranked one governs,
   with the difference sized. This is the §2.3 procedure applied to a non-financial-statement disclosure.
4. **`UNUTILISED` names where the money is parked**, in the parking instrument's own words (deposits with
   scheduled commercial banks, mutual funds, monitoring-agency-approved instruments), with the amount and
   the instrument type. "Unutilised" without a destination is `NOT-DISCLOSED` and names the document that
   would answer it.
5. **`DEVIATION` reports the MAR's answer and the SoD's answer separately**, because they answer two
   different questions in the SEBI formats: the MAR reports "(a) Deviation from the objects" and "(b)
   Range of Deviation"; the SoD reports "Is there a deviation/variation in use of funds raised?" and, if
   yes, whether it followed a change in the terms of a contract or the objects approved by shareholders.
6. **The tie-out identity is computed at every reporting date** and is the record's arithmetic backbone:

```
   Gross proceeds 8,200.00
     =  actual offer expenses borne by the company
      + cumulative deployed to Object (a) working capital
      + cumulative deployed to Object (b) general corporate purposes
      + unutilised balance
```

   A residual that does not tie is named and sized. An unnamed residual is `BROKEN` and routes to the
   red-flag register. Note that **actual offer expenses may differ from the ₹492.51 mn estimate**; that
   difference is itself an object-level variance and is reported, not absorbed.

### 4.3 ON-PLAN, BEHIND, DEVIATED, NOT-DISCLOSED — the definitions

| State | Definition, decidable without judgement |
|---|---|
| **ON-PLAN** | Cumulative deployed **≥ cumulative scheduled** at that reporting date under the Prospectus schedule; **and** the object as described in the current filing matches the Prospectus wording; **and** the MAR reports deviation "Nil"; **and** the SoD reports no deviation/variation |
| **BEHIND** | Cumulative deployed **< cumulative scheduled** at that reporting date, with **no shareholder-approved variation**. Sized as the ₹ shortfall and as a percentage of the object. Because this issue scheduled **100% of net proceeds within Fiscal 2026**, any unutilised balance at **31-Mar-2026** is `BEHIND` by definition — irrespective of what the MAR or SoD says about deviation |
| **DEVIATED** | Proceeds applied to an object other than as stated, **or** the objects or schedule varied. A variation of objects requires a special resolution under s.27 of the Companies Act 2013 with an exit offer to dissenting shareholders; the evidence set is the postal-ballot notice and voting result, the SoD answering "yes", and the MAR's range of deviation. Postal ballots are held in this run for Jan-2026 and Jun-2026 and must be read for this purpose |
| **NOT-DISCLOSED** | The quarter's MAR or SoD is absent, **or** the object-wise split is not given, **or** the parking destination is not named. Names the document that would answer it after `->` |

> **The instruction that this whole section exists to deliver.** `Reg 32(1)` asks whether there is a
> **deviation from the objects**. It does not ask whether deployment is **on schedule**. A company can
> report "no deviation" every quarter, truthfully, while deploying nothing. **"No deviation" is not an
> answer to the schedule question, and an agent that reports it as one has not run this test.** The
> schedule test is run separately, against the Prospectus, by the analyst.

### 4.4 Why "unutilised, parked in deposits" is a finding and not a neutral fact

Five reasons, each of which is a separate, computable test. An agent reporting an unutilised balance
must run all five or say which it could not run and why.

1. **It is a promise with a date on it, and the date is checkable.** The Prospectus scheduled the full
   ₹7,707.49 mn in Fiscal 2026 `[Prospectus p156/pr153]`. A balance still unutilised after that date is
   a promise-versus-delivery miss, sized in ₹ and in months, and it belongs in `W2_promise` as well as
   `W4_capalloc`. Its domains are Governance and MgmtCommentary, not merely Balance Sheet.
2. **It changes the return on the capital the shareholder paid for.** The equity was priced at ₹517 on
   the economics of a jewellery retail business; money in a bank deposit earns a deposit rate. Compute
   the drag explicitly: `unutilised balance × (the business's incremental return on the object − the
   realised yield on the parking instrument) × time held`. Where the incremental return on the object
   cannot be established from disclosure, say so and report the yield alone — do not assume a return.
3. **It contaminates other income, and therefore every metric computed above it.** Interest earned on
   unutilised issue proceeds is not operating income. It must be separated before any statement about
   margin, EBITDA, ROCE or profitability, and before any comparison with a peer that has no such balance.
   This connects directly to the company's own EBITDA definition, which subtracts other income
   `[Prospectus p168/pr165, note (5)]` — so the definition already contains the mechanism, and an agent
   must confirm the subtraction was actually performed in the year in question rather than assuming it.
4. **It contaminates net debt and the liquidity picture.** Money earmarked for a stated object is not
   free cash available for debt reduction, opportunistic capex or the ordinary working-capital cycle.
   Whether it sits inside "cash and bank balances" or under the separate head the company undertook to
   use `[Prospectus p163/pr160]` changes the net-debt number, and this company reports net debt on two
   definitions already (with and without gold metal loan — §7.2). State which cash is in the numerator.
   **If the separate head the Prospectus undertook to present is not present in the balance sheet, that
   is a disclosure failure against an explicit undertaking**, and it is a finding about the filing.
5. **It is a statement about the business, not about the treasury.** The object was working capital for
   inventory in an expanding store network. An unutilised working-capital balance means one of exactly
   two things: the expansion consumed less working capital than planned, or the working capital was
   funded another way. The second is directly testable, because the alternatives are all disclosed —
   gold on loan (§7.2), franchisee capital (§7.9), customer advances (§7.11), and bank borrowings.
   **Compare the movement in inventory against the movement in each funding line over the same window.**
   Which of the two explanations is true is the finding; neither may be asserted without that comparison.

Additionally, and separately: **general corporate purposes is the least-specified object** and carries a
regulatory cap. Track that cumulative GCP does not exceed ₹2,050.00 mn (25% of gross proceeds), and that
the GCP spend is described somewhere. GCP described only as "general corporate purposes" in every
quarterly filing is `NOT-DISCLOSED` at `L2` of the object's chain, and the document that would answer it
is the annual report's utilisation note.

### 4.5 The monitoring agency's report is not assurance

Every citation of a Monitoring Agency Report must carry this qualification, because the report carries it
itself:

> "We declare that this report provides an objective view of the utilization of the issue proceeds in
> relation to the objects of the issue **based on the information provided by the Issuer**… **The
> Monitoring Agency (MA) does not perform an audit and undertakes no independent verification of any
> information/ certifications/ statements it receives.**" `[MAR Q1FY27, CARE Ratings, 20-Jul-2026]`

The MAR is therefore evidence of **what was reported to the monitoring agency and what the agency
concluded on that basis** — which is genuinely useful, and is a rung above the company's own SoD because
a regulated third party signed it. It is **not** an audit, and it does not verify that the money went
where the certificate says. Assurance on utilisation comes from the annual report's audited disclosure
and from the annual statutory-auditor certificate on funds used for purposes other than those stated
`[Prospectus p163/pr160]`. `D3` (§0a) exists to force this distinction onto every number sourced this way.

### 5.3 How selling is evidenced — the four channels, in rank order

| Rank | Channel | What it proves | Held in this run |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Shareholding pattern XBRL, quarter on quarter** | The authoritative period-end position by category and, for holders above 1%, by name. This is the reconciliation spine | **5 quarters**: 18-Aug-2025 (listing), 30-Sep-2025, 31-Dec-2025, 31-Mar-2026, 30-Jun-2026 — `data/filings/SHP_*.xml` |
| 2 | **SAST Reg 29 disclosures** (acquisition/disposal crossing 5%, and every 2% change thereafter by a holder above 5%) and **Reg 31 encumbrance** | Named, dated, share-counted transactions by large holders | Announcement feed; SAST 31(4) of 24-Jun-2026 is `GAP-002` |
| 3 | **PIT Reg 7(2) continual disclosures** by designated persons and their immediate relatives (trades above ₹10 lakh in value in a calendar quarter) | Insider transactions, named and dated | `data/filings/insider.json`, Aug-2025 → Sep-2026 |
| 4 | **Bulk deals** (≥0.5% of listed equity in a day on an exchange) and **block deals** (block window, minimum ₹10 crore) | Exchange-reported trades with counterparty, quantity and price | **`GAP-003` — not captured programmatically.** NSE `historical/bulk-deals` returned HTTP 503; the BSE API redirected. The four deals of 09-Jun-2026 seen in the BSE section inventory are `SECONDARY` until reconciled |

**The reconciliation, which is the whole test.** For each pair of consecutive shareholding patterns, and
for each holder or category, run:

```
  Shares_held(q)  =  percentage(q)  ×  TotalSharesOutstanding(q)          [both read from the same XBRL]

  Δ Shares(q)     =  Shares_held(q) − Shares_held(q−1)

  Δ Shares(q)     =   − Σ observed disposals in the quarter (bulk + block + Reg 29 + PIT)
                      + Σ observed acquisitions in the quarter
                      + allotments received in the quarter (ESOP exercise, preferential, conversion)
                      + UNEXPLAINED RESIDUAL
```

The `UNEXPLAINED RESIDUAL` is named and sized every quarter. It is expected to be non-zero — open-market
sales below the bulk-deal threshold are not individually reported — and **that expectation is itself the
finding to be sized**: how much of the movement is evidenced transaction by transaction, and how much is
only visible as a period-end difference.

> **The trap this run must not fall into.** BlueStone's promoter-and-group holding moves **16.36% →
> 16.25%** across the five shareholding patterns (`data/source_register.md`, Tier 3). **A percentage
> movement is not evidence of selling.** The denominator changes: six ESOP allotments occurred between
> Jan-2026 and Sep-2026, each of which increases shares outstanding and therefore reduces every
> unchanged holder's percentage. **Convert to share counts before comparing anything.** A conclusion
> about promoter selling drawn from a percentage movement, without the share-count computation above, is
> a QC failure and is rejected.

### 5.4 The residual overhang calculation

Two numbers, both required, both stated with their date:

```
  Free float F(t)      =  TotalSharesOutstanding(t)  −  Promoter & promoter group shares(t)
                          [both from the shareholding pattern XBRL for date t]

  Residual overhang R(t)
      =  Σ over pre-IPO financial holders [ shares held at listing (18-Aug-2025 SHP)
                                            −  shares disposed of to date (evidenced) ]
         +  shares still within an unexpired lock-in class, by class and by expiry date

  Expressed as:   R(t) / F(t)                    -> proportion of free float
                  R(t) / ADTV_90d(t)             -> days of average daily traded volume
```

- `ADTV_90d` is a market input, not a filing: it is named, dated and sourced, and any conclusion resting
  on it alone is `SECONDARY`.
- The overhang is reported **by expiry tranche**, not as one aggregate, because the tranches become
  sellable on different dates. At the date of this run (09-Sep-2026), the 30-day, 90-day and six-month
  classes have passed their computed expiry and the **18-month class of 30,818,152 shares has not**
  (computed expiry 14-Feb-2027). The ladder is therefore still live and `W2_exits` reports it as a
  forward calendar, not as history.
- `EXIT` records use the block in `state/TEMPLATES.md`. One record per holder per event, with
  `PRE-IPO STAKE`, `LOCK-IN CLASS` and expiry, `SOLD` (quantity, price, route), `POST STAKE`, and
  `RESIDUAL OVERHANG`.

### 5.5 Failure conditions for the exit ladder

1. **An exit narrative built only on media, deal tickers or an aggregator, without the shareholding
   pattern reconciliation of §5.3, is `SECONDARY` and cannot carry a conclusion** (doctrine §1a-iii).
   `GAP-003` makes this the live risk in this run: the deal-level data currently held for 09-Jun-2026 is
   from a section inventory, not from the exchange bulk/block feed, and it stays `SECONDARY` until the
   share counts tie to the 30-Jun-2026 shareholding pattern.
2. A lock-in expiry computed from the listing date rather than the allotment date.
3. A percentage-based statement about any holder's stake changing (§5.3).
4. Classifying the PC Shortfall Shares as six-month-locked, or classifying their holders as promoters.
5. Applying the six-month Reg 17 lock-in uniformly to VCF/AIF/FVCI holders without testing the
   date-of-purchase carve-out and the Reg 8A restriction, holder by holder.
6. Treating the anchor overhang as sized when the **anchor allotment share count has not been read**.
   The Prospectus states that 60% of the QIB Category was allocated to anchors `[Prospectus p467/pr464,
   Offer Structure note (1)]`; the actual anchor allotment must come from the anchor-allocation
   intimation filed with the exchanges on/around 08-Aug-2025 or from the Prospectus capital-structure
   tables. If it is not in `data/filings/announcements_2025.json`, this is a **new gap** to be registered
   with that filing named as the document that would answer it, and the anchor tranche is reported as
   `NOT-DISCLOSED` rather than estimated from the 60% rule.
7. Reading `GAP-002` (the scanned SAST Reg 31(4) filing) as "no encumbrance".

## 6. The base-rate substitution

Doctrine §1a-ii: *"S5 fails when it asserts that something matters without establishing whether it has
ever mattered."* A thirteen-month-old company has almost no company-level base rate. The rule is not
suspended; the **subject** of the rate is substituted, in a fixed priority order, and the substitution is
declared.

### 6.1 The permitted substitutes, in priority order

| Tier | Source of the base rate | What it can establish | Standing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1** | **A realised loss inside BlueStone's own listed record (19-Aug-2025 → date)** — an actual write-off, impairment, provision reversal, inventory write-down, penalty, covenant breach, receivable loss, or a restatement | The pattern has cost this company money | Highest, and thin by construction. When it exists, it is used and nothing lower is needed |
| **T2** | **BlueStone's own pre-listing record as disclosed in the offer document** — the restated FY2023–FY2025 statements, the litigation schedule, the risk factors, the pre-IPO capital history, contingent liabilities, and any prior impairment or write-off | The pattern has cost this company money **before it was listed**. Company-level, but on the restated basis and from a selling document — so the basis (D1) and the document's purpose are declared with it | Second |
| **T3** | **The closest single comparable: CaratLane, inside Titan** — online-first, studded-led, same customer, separately disclosed within Titan's filings | The pattern's realised outcome in the nearest business model, over a longer record than BlueStone has | Third |
| **T4** | **The listed jewellery peer set** — Titan, Kalyan Jewellers, Senco Gold, PN Gadgil Jewellers, Thangamayil — read for the specific failure mode in question, from their own filings | Sector-level realised losses: inventory write-downs, gold-price mismatches, franchisee failures, scheme liabilities, receivable losses | Fourth |
| **T5** | **The sector's historical realised-loss episodes**, named and dated, sourced to filings or regulatory orders rather than to commentary | That the mechanism has produced losses in Indian jewellery retail — the strongest available answer to "has this ever cost anyone money" when the peer set is silent | Fifth; each episode cited individually, never as "the sector has a history of" |
| **T6** | **The recent Indian IPO cohort**, for promise-versus-delivery and proceeds-deployment questions specifically. **PN Gadgil Jewellers (listed Sep-2024) sits in both T4 and T6** and is the highest-value single observation available to this run | Whether stated objects, schedules and KPI commitments are met by recently listed Indian issuers | Sixth, and only for the promise/deployment class of question |
| **T7** | **The regulatory enforcement record** — SEBI orders on misutilisation of issue proceeds, monitoring-agency deviations, and ICDR/LODR enforcement | That the failure mode is enforced against, and what it costs when it is | Seventh; use for the IPO-proceeds and disclosure-obligation classes of question only |

**Rules of use.**

1. **Descend the ladder; never skip a tier without saying you searched it.** The `BASE-RATE:` line names
   the tier used and states that the higher tiers were searched and were empty.
2. **A substitute base rate is labelled as a substitute in the finding itself**, not in a footnote. The
   reader must be able to see that the rate is not this company's.
3. **A tier is only satisfied by a realised loss, not by a risk factor.** A risk factor in an offer
   document describes what could happen; it is `T2` evidence only where it discloses something that
   *did* happen.
4. **`SECONDARY` sourcing rule still applies.** A base rate assembled from media or aggregators is
   `SECONDARY` and cannot alone carry the conclusion (doctrine §1a-iii).

### 6.2 The exact sentence form when a base rate exists only as a substitute

```text
BASE-RATE: No company-level realised loss is available for this pattern: BlueStone's audited financial
record is 4 years (FY2023-FY2026, of which FY2023-FY2025 restated) and its listed record is 13 months,
which is shorter than the interval over which this pattern has produced a realised loss elsewhere.
Tiers T1-T<k-1> were searched and are empty. Substitute base rate, tier T<k> (<named source>):
<the realised loss, sized in Rs and dated, with its tag>. The substitution is a limit on the evidence,
not a reduction in the risk.
```

### 6.3 The exact sentence form when no base rate exists at all

This is the sentence that stops "no base rate found" from being read as "no risk found". It is mandatory
and it is used verbatim in structure:

```text
BASE-RATE: NOT ESTABLISHED. No realised loss found at company level - the record is too short
(4 financial years, 13 months listed) to distinguish a low rate from a zero rate - and none found in the
substitute set searched: <list the tiers searched, by name>. This is an UNMEASURED risk, not a measured
absence. -> <the document, dataset or disclosure that would answer it>.
```

Three properties of that form are load-bearing and none may be dropped:

- **"too short to distinguish a low rate from a zero rate"** — states the epistemic position rather than
  implying a result.
- **"UNMEASURED risk, not a measured absence"** — the sentence that does the work. Without it, an empty
  base-rate line reads as reassurance.
- **the `->` pointer** — doctrine §1a-iii's stopping rule: an unnamed unknown is an unasked question.

### 6.4 The prohibited forms

Never: *"no adverse history"* · *"a clean record"* · *"no evidence of X"* used where the evidence base is
too short to have shown X · *"has not been an issue"* · *"the company has consistently…"* over a
thirteen-month record. §8 repeats these as prohibitions; they are listed here because §6 is where they
are most tempting.

## 7. What a jewellery retailer must be traced on — the sector half

Fifteen traces. For each: **what moves it**, **the quantity that must be found and where**, **the chain
link it attaches to**, and **what its absence means**. "What its absence means" is never "we could not
assess this" — it is a named `NOT DISCLOSED` with a document after `->`, and a statement of which later
conclusion is thereby unavailable.

### 7.1 Gold as raw material

- **What moves it.** Grams consumed × the landed cost per gram (international price × exchange rate +
  import duty + premium/discount + making/refining loss).
- **Quantity required.** Cost of materials consumed, purchases of stock-in-trade and changes in
  inventories, decomposed into **gold / diamond and coloured stone / other**, and **the physical quantity
  consumed in grams or kilograms**. Where the annual report gives a quantitative particulars note or a
  raw-material consumption note, it is read; where the offer document gives a sourcing description, it is
  read alongside.
- **Chain link.** `L3` (driver) of the COGS chain; `L1`/`L2` of cost of materials consumed.
- **If absent.** `NOT DISCLOSED -> AR FY26 raw material consumption / quantitative particulars note; the
  offer document's "Our Business - Raw Materials" section.` Consequence: **the price/volume decomposition
  of COGS cannot be run**, and therefore no statement may be made about whether input cost movement was
  price or quantity (§8.13).

### 7.2 Gold as a liability — the gold metal loan

This is the trace that most distinguishes a jeweller's balance sheet from an ordinary retailer's, and
BlueStone presents it as its own caption rather than inside borrowings.

- **What moves it.** The outstanding quantity of gold borrowed × the gold price at the reporting date,
  plus the lease rate accrued.
- **Quantities required.**
  - The **rupee balance**: `Gold on loan ₹2,848.14 mn` at 31-Mar-2026 (31-Mar-2025: `₹3,865.53 mn`)
    `[AR FY26, Note 22, p204/pr225, REPORTED, STANDALONE]`, and the consolidated equivalent
    `[AR FY26, Note 23, p243/pr264, REPORTED, CONSOLIDATED]`. The note describes it as "amounts payable
    against gold purchased from various banks under gold on loan scheme".
  - The **physical quantity in grams or kilograms**, without which the rupee movement cannot be split
    between price and quantity.
  - The **effective rate**: the offer document states "typically our effective interest rate gold metal
    loans is 2.5% - 9%" `[Prospectus p73/pr70]`. The actual charge and where it sits in the P&L
    (finance cost, or inside COGS) must be traced, not assumed.
  - The **tenor, the bank counterparties, and the security**: deposits given as security against gold on
    loan and margin money held against it are disclosed in the bank-balance note footnotes — several
    such amounts appear at both year-ends `[AR FY26, bank balance notes, p187/pr208 and p191/pr212]`.
    **These deposits are restricted cash** and must be excluded from free liquidity (§3.3).
  - The **revaluation treatment**: a liability denominated in gold is remeasured as the gold price moves.
    Where that remeasurement lands — cost of materials, finance cost, other expenses, or other income —
    changes gross margin, EBITDA and finance cost, and it must be read from the accounting policy note,
    not inferred.
- **Chain link.** `L1`/`L6` of the gold-on-loan chain; `L5` for the remeasurement policy; `L7` for the
  financing/operating classification of its movement (and it is one of the three lines in `BROKEN-R5`).
- **If absent.** Grams `NOT DISCLOSED -> AR FY26 financial risk management note (commodity price risk)`,
  which is where the exposure quantity is most often given — the risk-management note already discusses
  the strategy of "procuring gold on loan basis" `[AR FY26, p225/pr246]`. Consequence: the leverage
  question cannot be settled, and **both** net-debt definitions must be carried (below).
- **The two net-debt definitions are the company's own, and both must be reported.** The offer document
  defines `Net Debt (with GML)` and `Net Debt (without GML)` and reports both, with FY2025 values of
  `₹6,094.47 mn` and `₹6,013.14 mn` respectively `[Prospectus p168/pr165, RESTATED]`. That the issuer
  publishes two is itself the disclosure that the classification is contestable. **Report both, always,
  and say which one any leverage statement used.** Using one silently is `BROKEN-R4` territory.

### 7.3 Gold price pass-through — separating price growth from volume growth

- **What moves it.** Revenue = (grams of gold sold × realised gold price) + (stone value) + (making
  charges) − (discounts and old-gold credits). A jeweller's revenue can grow with zero additional
  customers.
- **Quantities required.** In descending order of usefulness: grams sold; orders/bills; the disclosed
  proxies **AOV ₹47,671.26 (FY2025), ₹41,204.71 (FY2024), ₹32,038.38 (FY2023)**, **number of customers
  life-till-date 771,845 / 562,729 / 390,959**, and **same-store sales growth 32.14% / 51.16% / 72.06%**
  `[Prospectus p168/pr165, RESTATED]`; and a named, dated external gold-price series.
- **The decomposition, written out.**

```
      (1 + revenue growth)  =  (1 + volume growth) × (1 + realisation growth)

      realisation growth    ≈  (1 + gold price growth × gold share of ticket)
                               × (1 + mix/making-charge growth) − 1
```

  Every term that is not disclosed is marked `NOT DISCLOSED` and the identity is reported with the
  missing term as the residual — **the residual is named, never distributed across the known terms**.
- **The external-input rule.** A gold-price series is not a filing. It is named (IBJA, MCX, or another
  identified series), dated, used identically everywhere in the report, and any conclusion resting on it
  alone is `SECONDARY`.
- **If absent.** Consequence: **no statement anywhere in this report that growth was volume-led or
  price-led** (§8.13). AOV growth alone does not establish either: AOV rises with the gold price with no
  behaviour change at all.
- **Chain link.** `L3` of the revenue chain.

### 7.4 Making charges — the real margin

- **What moves it.** Making charge as a percentage of gold value, the discount rate applied to it, and
  whether the customer was charged on gross or net weight.
- **Quantities required.** Making charge realisation (per gram, or as a percentage of gold value);
  discounting on making charges, including any "zero making charge" promotion and the periods it ran; the
  offer document notes that "the margins are structured around making charges and brand-led premiums"
  `[Prospectus p202/pr199, industry section]` and that making charges vary significantly across
  jewellers.
- **Chain link.** `L3` of the gross-margin chain; `L2` if a making-charge line is separately disclosed.
- **If absent.** `NOT DISCLOSED -> AR FY26 revenue disaggregation note (Ind AS 115) and the concall
  transcripts, where making-charge and discounting questions are routinely asked.` Consequence: **gross
  margin movement cannot be attributed between mix, gold price and discounting**, and any attribution to
  one of the three is unsupported.

### 7.5 Studded ratio

- **What moves it.** The share of revenue from studded (diamond and coloured-stone) jewellery, which
  carries a structurally different margin from plain gold.
- **Quantities required.** `Studded Revenue (%)`: **67.88 (FY2025), 67.44 (FY2024), 68.31 (FY2023)**
  `[Prospectus p168/pr165, RESTATED]`, defined by the company as "the percentage share of studded
  jewellery sales of the total jewellery sales" and explained as the KPI that "impacts the margin
  performance" `[Prospectus p169/pr166]`. Post-listing continuity of this KPI must be checked against the
  Prospectus undertaking (§1.4).
- **The asymmetry test (doctrine §1a-i) is mandatory here.** A studded share of revenue stated alone is
  half an observation. The finding is in the **disproportion between the studded share of revenue and the
  studded share of gross profit** — that pair is what says whether the mix is doing what management says
  it does. Where gross profit by category is not disclosed, that is the `NOT DISCLOSED` and the
  single-share statement is reported as incomplete rather than as a finding.
- **Comparability warning.** The offer document itself computes peers' studded ratios on four different
  bases — Titan from quarterly studded share of domestic jewellery, Kalyan from quarterly Indian jewellery
  sales, Senco as reported, Thangamayil from diamonds as a share of traded goods, and PC Jeweller not
  reported separately `[Prospectus p169/pr166, notes a–e]`. **Any peer studded comparison must state the
  basis used for each peer** (§1.7, §7.16).
- **If absent post-listing.** `NOT DISCLOSED -> AR FY26 MD&A and the quarterly investor presentations.`
  Consequence: the gross-margin bridge loses its largest mix term.

### 7.6 Old gold exchange

- **What moves it.** Customers trading in old jewellery, receiving credit, and applying it against a
  purchase.
- **Quantities required.** The value of old gold taken in; whether revenue is recognised gross (sale
  recognised at full value with a corresponding purchase) or net of the credit; the outstanding credit
  liability; the lapse/breakage policy and the amount of credits that expired. BlueStone runs "**The Big
  Gold Upgrade**", crediting the assessed value of old gold to the customer's account as **Blue Credits**
  `[Prospectus p57/pr54]`, and the liability "**forms part of 'advance received from customers'**" —
  which was **₹272.85 mn = 1.04% of total liabilities at 31-Mar-2025** (FY2024 `₹99.72 mn` = 0.48%;
  FY2023 `₹75.91 mn` = 0.57%) `[Prospectus p57/pr54, RESTATED]`. Note that this line **also** contains
  the instalment-scheme liability of §7.11, so the two must be separated before either is used.
- **Chain link.** `L5` (recognition — gross versus net, and the breakage policy) and `L6` (the credit
  liability) of the revenue chain.
- **If absent.** `NOT DISCLOSED -> AR FY26 revenue recognition policy and the contract-liabilities note
  (Note 24 standalone p207/pr228; Note 25 consolidated p288/pr309).` Consequence: **revenue is not
  comparable with a peer that nets old-gold exchange**, and SSSG and AOV cannot be interpreted, because an
  exchange transaction can inflate both without new money entering the business.

### 7.7 Inventory — the dominant asset

- **What moves it.** Store count × inventory per store, plus warehouse and in-transit stock, plus the gold
  price applied to the same physical stock.
- **Quantities required.**
  - Composition: raw materials, work in progress, finished goods, stock-in-trade — and within them, gold
    versus diamond versus other.
  - **Physical quantity**, so that a rupee increase can be split between price and stock build.
  - Ageing, and the provision for slow-moving or non-moving stock, with the movement in that provision.
  - Location split: own stores, warehouse, franchisee stores, goods with third parties (job work,
    consignment, memo), and goods pledged or under lien.
  - The turnover ratio and its definition. **The company's own `Inventory Turnover Ratio` is
    `Revenue from Operations / Average Inventory`** — a turnover on **revenue**, not on COGS — giving
    **1.34 (FY2025), 1.83 (FY2024), 2.75 (FY2023)** on average inventory of **₹13,218.84 / ₹6,932.69 /
    ₹2,807.20 mn** `[Prospectus p168/pr165, RESTATED, notes (11) and (12)]`. A COGS-based inventory-days
    figure computed by the analyst is a **different number** and must be labelled as such; the two are
    never mixed in one series.
  - **The price/volume decomposition of the inventory build:**

```
      Δ Inventory (₹)  =  (closing grams − opening grams) × opening price      [quantity effect]
                        +  closing grams × (closing price − opening price)     [price effect]
                        +  mix/valuation effects (NRV, provision movement)     [residual, named]
```

- **Chain link.** `L6` of the revenue and COGS chains; `L1`/`L2` of its own chain; and the inventory limb
  of `Test D` and `BROKEN-R3` in §3.
- **If absent.** Physical quantity `NOT DISCLOSED -> AR FY26 inventory note and the financial risk
  management note (commodity price risk)`. Consequence: **an inventory increase cannot be called a stock
  build**, because a gold price rise produces the same rupee movement with no additional stock.

### 7.8 Store economics

- **What moves it.** New stores × capex and inventory per store × time to maturity, less closures, less
  cannibalisation of nearby stores and of the online channel.
- **Quantities required.** Store count by format at each period end and the opened/closed movement; area
  in square feet; revenue per store and per square foot; a **cohort/maturity table** (revenue by year of
  opening); capex per store; **inventory per store**; payback period; closure count and reasons; city and
  region count. Anchors on the record: **275 stores in 117 cities at 31-Mar-2025** (FY2024: 192 stores,
  80 cities; FY2023: 155 stores, 71 cities) `[Prospectus p168/pr165, RESTATED]`, and the AR FY2026
  presents store count and total area in '000 sq ft `[AR FY26, p12/pr33 and p20/pr41]`.
- **Chain link.** `L3` of the revenue chain (the physical driver) and `L6` of the capex/ROU chain.
- **If absent.** Cohort table `NOT DISCLOSED -> quarterly investor presentations and the concall
  transcripts.` Consequence: **maturity, payback and cannibalisation may not be asserted** (§8.26).
  Same-store sales growth alone evidences none of the three: SSSG covers only stores open twelve months
  and says nothing about what a new store earns, what it cost, or what it took from its neighbour.

### 7.9 The franchise structure — COCO versus FOCO, and the commission add-back

This is the highest-yield structural trace in the sector overlay for this company, because the
arrangement moves capital, inventory and a fixed obligation between the company and a third party.

- **The structure, in the offer document's words.** "We operate certain stores under a **franchise owned,
  company operated** model, where the franchisee owns the store, but we are responsible for its
  day-to-day operation and inventory management. **Franchisee partners only provide capital for the
  investment made in the franchisee stores that includes capital expenditure and inventory costs.** In
  exchange… we typically pay them **the higher of a minimum guaranteed fixed return on the investment, or
  a fixed return on the revenue generated by the store**" `[Prospectus p72/pr69]`. Franchisee store count
  ran **54 → 106 → 96 → 75** across the FY2023–FY2025 openings/closings table `[Prospectus p72/pr69]`.
- **Quantities required, and each is a separate question.**
  1. **Is franchisee-funded inventory on BlueStone's balance sheet or the franchisee's?** Read the
     inventory note, the revenue recognition policy (principal versus agent under Ind AS 115), and the
     franchisee deposit disclosure. This single answer changes inventory days, capital employed, ROCE and
     the working-capital story.
  2. **What is the franchisee deposit balance**, and is it a financial liability? The offer document
     refers to "deposit made by franchisee" among other financial liabilities `[Prospectus p67/pr64]`.
  3. **What is the minimum guarantee obligation** — the amount, the rate, the tenor, and whether it is
     disclosed as a commitment or a contingent liability.
  4. **Where does the commission sit in the P&L?** It "forms part of the 'Brokerage & Commission' line
     item in other expenses" `[Prospectus p168/pr165, note (7)]` — i.e. **above** EBITDA in the statutory
     presentation.
  5. **What does management's Adjusted EBITDA add back?** ESOP charge **and** franchisee commission,
     the latter on the stated reasoning that "due to our unique franchisee model, [it] is also a finance
     cost" `[Prospectus p169/pr166]`. Adjusted EBITDA was **₹1,278.06 mn (FY2025)**, **₹1,054.23 mn
     (FY2024)**, **₹(272.79) mn (FY2023)** against statutory-definition EBITDA of **₹731.64 / ₹530.49 /
     ₹(560.34) mn** `[Prospectus p167/pr164, RESTATED]`.
- **The instruction.** The add-back is management's characterisation and it is testable: a payment that
  is the **higher of** a guaranteed return on capital **or** a share of revenue is part financing cost and
  part variable operating cost, and the split is determinable from the amounts. **Report the statutory
  EBITDA and the Adjusted EBITDA side by side, always, with the add-backs itemised in ₹ and the split of
  the commission between its guaranteed limb and its revenue-linked limb established or marked
  `NOT DISCLOSED`.** Never adopt Adjusted EBITDA as the operating measure without doing this.
- **Chain link.** `L2` and `L5` of the other-expenses chain; `L4` (counterparty — the franchisees);
  `L6` (the deposit liability and the inventory question); `L7` (`BROKEN-R5`'s franchisee-deposit limb).
- **If absent.** `NOT DISCLOSED -> AR FY26 other expenses note, financial liabilities note, commitments
  and contingent liabilities note, and the related-party note.` Consequence: **the store network may not
  be characterised as asset-light or capital-light** (§8.25), because who funds the inventory is exactly
  the question that phrase answers.

### 7.10 Online versus offline, and omnichannel attribution

- **What moves it.** Where the order is placed, where it is fulfilled, and which channel the company
  credits with the revenue.
- **Quantities required.** Revenue by channel (own website and app, marketplaces, own stores, franchise
  stores); the **attribution rule** for an order that begins online and completes in a store or vice
  versa; whether online-originated, store-fulfilled revenue is inside SSSG; order counts and AOV by
  channel; return rates by channel (the offer document describes a 30-day return policy with reverse
  pick-up `[Prospectus p265/pr262]`, and returns behave very differently online and in store).
- **Chain link.** `L2` and `L4` of the revenue chain.
- **If absent.** `NOT DISCLOSED -> AR FY26 revenue disaggregation note under Ind AS 115, and the
  quarterly investor presentations.` Consequence: **channel-mix claims are `SECONDARY`** and cannot carry
  a conclusion; and SSSG cannot be compared with a peer whose attribution rule is different or unstated.

### 7.11 Customer advances and instalment schemes

- **What moves it.** Enrolments × instalment size × tenure, less redemptions, less lapses.
- **The scheme.** The **10+1 Gold Mine Scheme (GMS)**, described in the offer document as implemented "to
  incentivise and **lock-in** our customers, as well as to **assure our revenue for 11 months by accruing
  cash for a planned customer purchase 11 months prior to the actual transaction**": ten monthly
  instalments from a minimum of ₹1,000, a **25% discount on the first monthly payment**, a company
  contribution in the eleventh month, and redemption by voucher — with nomination permitted so a third
  party may redeem `[Prospectus p265/pr262]`.
- **Quantities required.** Amount outstanding under GMS and its percentage of total liabilities (the offer
  document tabulates this for FY2023–FY2025); enrolments; redemption rate; average tenure; **breakage /
  lapse recognised in revenue**; the accounting for the 25% first-instalment discount and the eleventh-month
  company contribution (a reduction of revenue, or a marketing cost — the two produce different gross
  margins on identical economics); the split of "advance received from customers" between the instalment
  scheme and the old-gold credits of §7.6; and **compliance with the Companies (Acceptance of Deposits)
  Rules**, which govern advance-instalment schemes of this shape.
- **Chain link.** `L5` (recognition and breakage), `L6` (contract liability — Note 24 standalone
  `p207/pr228`, Note 25 consolidated `p288/pr309` `[AR FY26]`), `L7` (it is one of the three lines in
  `BROKEN-R5`).
- **If absent.** `NOT DISCLOSED -> AR FY26 contract liabilities note and revenue recognition policy; the
  offer document's Loyalty/Incentive Schemes section for the pre-listing series.` Consequence: **operating
  cash flow cannot be separated from customer-funded cash**, which is precisely what `BROKEN-R5` tests, and
  a growing scheme balance will read as operating cash generation when it is a customer deposit.

### 7.12 Lease accounting under Ind AS 116 in a store-expansion business

- **What moves it.** Store count × lease term × rent × discount rate, plus the renewal options management
  judges reasonably certain to be exercised.
- **Quantities required.** Right-of-use assets and the lease liability, opening to closing, with
  additions, terminations, accretion of interest and payments; depreciation on right-of-use assets;
  interest on lease liabilities; **short-term and low-value lease expense** (which stays in other
  expenses); **variable / revenue-linked rent**, which is common in mall locations and which Ind AS 116
  leaves in the P&L rather than capitalising — so two retailers with identical economics can show
  different EBITDA purely from the fixed/variable split of their rent; the incremental borrowing rate; and
  the lease-term assumptions including renewal options. Anchors are listed in §3.5.
- **The instruction.** **Compute a pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA** (EBITDA less the rent that Ind AS 116 removed,
  i.e. approximately depreciation on right-of-use assets plus lease interest, adjusted for the actual
  payments) **before any peer EBITDA-margin comparison or any comparison to a pre-2019 series.** State the
  adjustment in ₹. Report both figures.
- **Chain link.** `L5` and `L6` of the lease chain; `L7` via Test E (§3.5).
- **If absent.** `NOT DISCLOSED -> AR FY26 leases note (p182-183/pr203-204) and the significant judgements
  note on lease term.` Consequence: **EBITDA margin is not comparable** across companies or across time,
  and the operating-leverage statement cannot be made.

### 7.13 Gold price risk management and derivatives

- **What moves it.** The mismatch between the gold in inventory (an asset that rises with the price) and
  the gold owed (a liability that rises with it too), plus any hedge.
- **Quantities required.** The company's stated risk-management strategy — the FY2026 financial risk note
  says the strategy "includes procuring gold on loan basis, with a flexibility…" `[AR FY26, p225/pr246]`
  — the **net open position in grams**, the sensitivity disclosure (the effect on profit of a stated
  percentage move in the gold price), any forward or futures contracts and their notional, and the hedge
  accounting policy if any.
- **Chain link.** `L5` and `L6` of the inventory and gold-on-loan chains.
- **If absent.** `NOT DISCLOSED -> AR FY26 financial instruments and financial risk management notes.`
  Consequence: no statement may be made about whether the business is long or short gold, and the
  sensitivity of margin to the gold price is unquantified rather than absent.

### 7.14 Diamonds, and lab-grown substitution

- **What moves it.** Natural diamond prices, the certification regime, and the emergence of lab-grown
  stones at a different price point in the same display case.
- **Quantities required.** Whether lab-grown product is sold and at what share of revenue; the margin
  difference if disclosed; the repurchase/exchange policy on stones; certification arrangements (the offer
  document describes third-party BIS certification for hallmarking and separate stone certification
  `[Prospectus p55/pr52]`); and the inventory provision policy for stones, which age differently from
  gold because gold has a floor price and a cut stone does not.
- **Chain link.** `L3` and `L5` of the studded revenue and inventory chains.
- **If absent.** `NOT DISCLOSED -> AR FY26 revenue disaggregation and inventory notes; concall
  transcripts.` Consequence: the studded margin driver (§7.5) is undecomposed.

### 7.15 Hallmarking and the regulatory obligations of a jeweller

- **What moves it.** BIS mandatory hallmarking and HUID, import duty on gold, GST on jewellery and on
  making charges, PMLA/KYC thresholds on cash and high-value transactions, Legal Metrology, e-commerce
  and consumer-protection rules, and the deposit rules governing instalment schemes (§7.11).
- **Quantities required.** Compliance cost where separable; contingent liabilities and the litigation
  schedule; any show-cause notice, penalty or regulatory action; the disclosure of GST and customs
  matters. The offer document's industry section records mandatory hallmarking from 2021 and discusses
  infrastructure constraints `[Prospectus p210/pr207, industry section]` — but per §1.12 that section

- **Chain link.** `L4` and `L5`; and the Industry and Governance domains for `S4`.
- **If absent.** `NOT DISCLOSED -> AR FY26 contingent liabilities note, CARO report, and the offer
  document's Outstanding Litigation section.` Consequence: **the regulatory risk is unmeasured, not
  absent** — and §6.3's sentence form applies to it directly.

## 8. The register of what may NOT be said in this report

Explicit and exhaustive for the situations this overlay governs. Each item is a QC-checkable prohibition.
Where a prohibition has a permitted alternative, the alternative is given.

1. **No CAGR over a window shorter than four completed financial years on a single basis**, and none at
   all across the FY2025 basis boundary. Permitted alternative: a point-to-point growth rate labelled
   `n=<n>, point-to-point, not a trend`.
2. **No "improving trend", "deteriorating trend", "steady", "consistent" or "consistently" from fewer
   than three data points on one basis.** Two points are a difference, not a trend, and the word "trend"
   never appears without `n` beside it.
3. **No comparison of a restated figure to a reported figure without the `BASIS:` line** of §2.2
   immediately under the table, including the FY2025 pair and the classified difference.
4. **No claim of any kind about BlueStone before FY2023** (`GAP-001`). The company's evidence base starts
   at FY2023 restated, and anything earlier is outside it.
5. **No treatment of a short clean record as a clean record.** Permitted form: *"No such event has been
   recorded in the 13-month listed record; the record is too short to distinguish a low rate from a zero
   rate."* Forbidden: "clean track record", "no adverse history", "no issues to date", "unblemished".
6. **No sentence in which an empty base rate functions as reassurance.** §6.3's form is mandatory and
   verbatim in structure.
7. **No annualisation of a quarter** without stating the seasonal pattern (festive and wedding

8. **No use of "it is early days" or "as a recently listed company" to soften a finding.** The disclosure

10. **No peer comparison lacking the four sector qualifiers of §7.16** plus the D1–D4 declarations.
11. **No adoption of a management-defined metric without its definition, its add-backs and the statutory

## 10. Correction protocol

A later unit that falsifies anything in this document does not edit it. It appends, under the affected
section, a block of this exact form — matching the convention used in the BSE run's overlay:

```text
> **CORRECTION, <date>, from <unit>.** <What this overlay said.> <Why it is wrong, with the evidence
> and its tag.> <The corrected instruction, stated so a later agent can execute it.>
```

Two rules on corrections, both from doctrine §10:

1. **Nothing is deleted.** The original rule stays visible above the correction, so that a reader can see
   what the run believed and when it stopped believing it.
2. **A correction that removes a test must say what replaces it.** An overlay rule withdrawn without a
   replacement leaves a hole in the method, and the hole is invisible in the final report. If nothing
   replaces it, the correction says so explicitly and the affected question goes to the gap register.
